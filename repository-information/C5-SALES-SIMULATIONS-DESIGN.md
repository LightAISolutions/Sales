# C5 — Sales Simulations Design (design-gate output, Fable 5.1 xhigh, 2026-09-18)

**Provenance.** C5 is the sales-simulation phase of `PHASE6-CLASSROOM-DESIGN.md` ("objection/discovery scenario modules built from dossier facts, relationships, named projects, and policy exposure") and order 9 of `INTEGRATED-REMEDIATION-PLAN.md` §7.3 — design ×1 on **Fable 5.1 xhigh**, authoring ×~5 on **Opus 5 xhigh**, precondition "after ~6 landscape modules" (met since S2 session 6; S2 closed at 19 of 19, v06.45r). `IMPROVEMENT-PLAN.md` §5 held it at a design gate on three open questions — its own lesson `type`, whether the unattended pipeline may author one, and the gate. This document answers all three and is the **executable spec** the five authoring sessions work from, in the role `PHASE6-CLASSROOM-DESIGN.md` played for the Classroom build and `CLASSROOM-CURRICULUM-PLAN.md` §10 played for the segment layer.

**Status.** Written 2026-09-18 at v06.51r by the design session. **Nothing here is built.** Every decision in §3 is **proposed** — the developer approves or amends them before authoring session 1 runs (the paste-in brief for that session is `INTEGRATED-REMEDIATION-PLAN.md` §7.60, written in the same commit and conditional on the decisions standing as written). Every number in §2 was measured against the repository on the day; sessions re-measure rather than carry.

**The one-paragraph design.** A **scenario** is one rehearsed conversation: one seat (`storage-seller` or `aidc-power-seller`, the two implicit role paths of curriculum plan §10.10), one buyer segment, one named counterparty from that segment's registry roster, one stage of the sale, one mode (`objection` or `discovery`). It is an ordinary schema-v1 lesson literal inside the content fence with `type: "scenario"`, ten fixed section ids, three decision beats rendered by the existing `quiz` kind, and a claims ledger. Its premises are the counterparty's own record (dossier fields) and the segment's landscape module; its phrasing is authored; it quotes nobody and names no person. Its stamp always carries the landscape, so it folds to **`guidance` — contributor and admin, never analyst, never admin-only**. The unattended pipeline never writes one. It enters no drill deck and no track. It is played through a **Rehearsal** library on the page. Fourteen scenarios over nine buyer segments, two seats, five Opus sessions.

## 1 · What C5 is, and what it is not

**What it is — the assessment layer.** The curriculum teaches in three registers: the mechanism lessons teach how a thing works and why the buyer asks (understand), the segment lessons and the roster deck drill who sits where (remember, apply), and the landscape modules give the seller's judgment about a named field (analyse). Nothing yet asks the reader to **choose** — to take a position across a table, under time, against a counterparty whose record they have read, and be told afterwards what the record says a strong move was. That is the register Bloom calls *evaluate*, and it is what a sales career actually tests. C5 supplies it: a **scenario** is one such conversation, pre-authored, played once through the page, graded by the same `quiz` mechanic the drill already uses, and debriefed against the record.

**Two modes, because the two conversations a seller actually rehearses are different.** An **objection** scenario opens with the counterparty's position already formed — an incumbency, a rule, a risk, a competitor — and the beats are the seller's three moves to reframe it; the test is whether the reader answers the *rule* rather than the *person*. A **discovery** scenario opens with a blank position and the beats are which question to ask next and what a good answer would reveal; the test is whether the reader can tell what the record does not say and go and get it. Both end in a debrief that names the mechanism lesson each beat rested on, so a wrong move is a link back into the curriculum rather than a score.

**What it is not, stated because each was on the table.**

- **Not runtime AI.** In-app Q&A is deliberately deferred (`PHASE6-CLASSROOM-DESIGN.md`, "ask the instructor"; the 2026-08-22 guidance decision). A scenario is authored content with fixed beats; the page renders it with the `cl*` engine it already has. No API key, no budget surface, no page-side generation.
- **Not a branching engine.** Three linear beats, each a four-option choice with a written rationale for every option, and a `note` that carries the conversation forward on the assumption the strong move was taken. A branching tree would need a new section kind, a page renderer and a checker — a page feature, not a content phase — and the marginal realism does not buy what it costs. Recorded as a candidate in §12.
- **Not a source of customer language.** Gap-register row G12 (`CLASSROOM-CURRICULUM-PLAN.md` §6) is by design: field notes can never become content, and no dossier carries a transcript. A scenario's objection is therefore a **paraphrased position the record supports**, ledgered to the dossier field it rests on — never a quotation unless the dossier itself quotes a public statement, never attributed to a named person. §3 D4 fixes the rule; §10 shows it working against a real dossier.
- **Not a deck.** No scenario contributes an item to the mechanism deck or the roster deck. A "rehearsal deck" that resurfaces completed scenarios on a schedule is plausible later and is deferred (§12), for the same reason K2 was opt-in and separate: company-specific rehearsal must never leak into the mechanism deck's "never company trivia" contract.
- **Not a track member and not a study-next target.** Tracks are ordered reading lists of modules with a stated outcome; a scenario is chosen, not sequenced. It surfaces through its own library.
- **Not analyst-visible, and not admin-only.** Every scenario is contributor+ by construction (§3 D3). The team the developer may lead reads them; a reader with only public access does not; and no scenario is locked to the admin tier by resting on a report, because none rests on one.

## 3 · The decisions — D1 to D11, each with what it rejects

Each decision states the choice, the reason, the alternatives considered and why they lost, and what the choice costs. **All eleven are proposed for the developer's approval**; where one is overturned, §7.60 says how session 1 adapts.

**D1 — Form: a lesson with `type: "scenario"`, inside the content fence.** A scenario is a `clLesson<Name>_()` literal in `clLessons_()`, exactly like a module or a briefing, distinguished by `type` and by a required `scenario` block (§4). *Why:* it inherits everything a lesson already has — the stamp and its fold, `clRequireLesson_`, the per-tier card, section-level progress ticks, `revisions[].changed[]` deltas, the `cl*` renderer, and both checkers. *Rejected:* **(a)** a module distinguished only by an id prefix `scenario-`, the way `segment-` works — it would leave `type: module` on something tracks must refuse and the drill must skip, and the page's index already partitions cards by `type` (briefings as a feed, loose modules by lane), so a prefix would have needed the same page edit plus a string test in three places (rr27); **(b)** a third content family `clScenario<Name>_()` below the fence with its own registry and ops, the way guidance modules live — it would duplicate the gate, the card, the progress validator and the renderer wiring for no gain, and `clProgressValid_` would need a third admitted family; **(c)** a guidance module — a module gives guidance to a *group*, and a scenario is about one named counterparty by construction. *Cost:* the pipeline's write set (the fence) now contains a kind of lesson the pipeline must not write; D6 and P13 close that.

**D2 — Schema: additive, no `CL_LESSON_SCHEMA_VERSION` bump.** `type` gains a third value; `scenario` is a new optional-at-schema-level object that the checker makes required when `type` is `scenario`; no existing field changes shape. *Why:* `CLASSROOM-SCHEMA.md` "Extending the schema" rule 2 says a bump is for a **shape** change and that new optional fields need none — the renderer skips absent fields, and every older lesson is untouched. `IMPROVEMENT-PLAN.md` §5 assumed a bump and therefore a load-time adapter; the schema's own rule says otherwise, and the two documents disagreed (rr25). *The decisive cost of the alternative:* `CLASSROOM-COMMITTER-CONTRACT.md` §5.1 step 4 makes a schema-version mismatch a `BLOCKED` pre-flight, so a bump would stop **every** unattended run from the moment it landed until the contract was rewritten — for a change that adds no shape. *Cost of the choice:* a v1 page that has never seen `type: scenario` would file it among loose modules; session 1's page change is what makes that moot, and it lands in the same commit as the first scenario.

**D3 — Gate: contributor, by construction of the stamp; never analyst, never admin.** Every scenario's stamp carries **`guidance:landscape-<segment>-YYYY-MM`** for its own segment, so `clGateForProvenance_` folds it to `guidance`; the checker asserts the fold (§8). No scenario may carry a `report:` ref. *Why, on the ladder:* `INTEGRATED-REMEDIATION-PLAN.md` §7.2 decision 2 put **judgment about named companies at the contributor tier** so the future team reads it — a scenario is that judgment, rehearsed. It cannot honestly be public: a public stamp on a lesson that puts a position on a named buyer is the single-company analysis the 2026-08-29 content-scope rule keeps out of public modules. *Why, on the sourcing:* the landscape is not a citation of convenience — `the-sellers-play` and `each-players-bet` are where the strong move and the counterparty's posture come from, which is exactly why C5's precondition was "after ~6 landscape modules". *Why not admin:* `IMPROVEMENT-PLAN.md` §5 wrote "a scenario built from a report is admin-only, which is most of them"; measured, **none need be** — every fact layer C5 draws on is public and the only judgment layer it needs is the landscape (rr24). Reports rank fields; a scenario needs a buyer's position, not a ranking. Admin-only scenarios would defeat the phase's purpose, which is the team. *Rejected:* a new `rehearse` capability in `CL_ROLE_CAPS` — that is a parallel gate, which the Classroom rules forbid; the stamp is the gate. *Cost:* the analyst tier gets nothing from C5; §12 records the question of whether a public-only scenario class should ever exist, and answers "not unless the content-scope rule moves".

**D4 — The customer-language rule (G12).** An objection is written as **a paraphrased position the record supports**, in the scenario's own voice, and every premise in it is a row in the claims ledger pointing at the dossier field it rests on. Quotation marks appear only around words the dossier itself quotes from a public statement, and the ledger row says so. **The counterparty is a role, never a person** — "the head of procurement", "the tax-equity bank's independent engineer" — even though `decisionMakers[]` is public data: putting words in a real, named person's mouth is fabrication about a person, and no accuracy of the premise repairs it. *Why:* G12's judgment stands — notes are not sources, and no dossier is a transcript — so the honest form of a rehearsal is *reported* position plus authored phrasing, with the seam visible. *Rejected:* dialogue with invented quotations (reads better, lies about provenance); anonymising the company as well as the person (the landscape precedent, §10.6 (f), is that naming the player is what makes the guidance usable). *Cost:* a scenario reads as a briefing-before-the-meeting more than as theatre. That is the right register for a sales curriculum.

**D5 — Fact and analysis stay separate in the ledger.** A premise drawn from `strategyRead[]`, from a landscape's `each-players-bet` or `the-indicators` judgment, or from any sentence the dossier labels as assessment enters the ledger with kind **`analysis`**; a premise drawn from `policyExposure[]`, `relationships[]`, `recentDevelopments[]`, `productsAndServices[]`, `financials` or a project entry enters as **`fact`**. The objection's *rule* — the thing the seller must answer — is always a fact row; the counterparty's *posture* may be analysis. *Why:* `PROFILER-SCHEMA.md` forbids blending unsourced interpretation into fact, and the landscapes hold the same line ("the bet — analysis, not fact"). *Cost:* one more column in the ledger.

**D6 — The unattended pipeline never authors and never revises a scenario.** A C2 run that finds a scenario's pinned input moved lists it under `Needs the developer` and touches nothing; **P13** (§8) fails any pipeline diff that adds a `type: scenario` literal or changes an existing one. Scenarios are refreshed by developer sessions, and re-read whenever the quarterly guidance review revises the landscape they rest on (§12 item 2 — a Routine prompt change the developer approves). *Why:* the beats' correctness depends on the facts; a run permitted to refresh `what-the-record-says` under G3 without re-judging the beats would produce a scenario whose record and rehearsal disagree, which is worse than a stale scenario wearing a "review due" chip. And the pipeline's caps and G3 were written for lessons that teach claims, not conversations — `IMPROVEMENT-PLAN.md` §5 said so. *Rejected:* the split rule (facts refreshable, beats frozen) — checkable, but it manufactures exactly the disagreement above; and moving scenarios below the fence to make them unwritable by construction — impossible while they are lessons (the checker requires every `clLesson*_` literal inside the fence), and D1 wants them to be lessons. *Cost:* one new assertion and two selftest fixtures.

**D7 — No drill items.** `clDrillLessonItems_` skips lessons whose `type` is `scenario`; the content checker's truth table asserts that a scenario fixture yields zero `lc:`/`lq:` ids at every tier; the roster deck is untouched. *Why:* every scenario is company-specific, and the mechanism deck's contract is "never company trivia". *Rejected:* a third opt-in deck now — plausible, but it is K2's shape again and should be decided on evidence of use (§12). *Cost:* the "evaluate" layer has no spaced repetition in v1; completion is the record.

**D8 — Never a track member, never a study-next target.** `check_track` already refuses non-modules; it stays that way. `clStudyNext_` walks tracks only (rr26), so a scenario registered without a track is unreachable by it with no code change. Scenarios surface through the **Rehearsal** library (§7). *Rejected:* a "Rehearsal" track per seat — it would put a chosen exercise into a sequenced reading list and hand study-next a scenario as "next", which is the wrong prompt at the wrong time.

**D9 — Ten fixed section ids, the `quiz` kind as the decision mechanic, three beats.** The template is §5. Fixed ids for the same reason the segment lesson has them: uniform units, permanent progress keys, and a checker that can assert shape. Three beats because a real objection conversation has an opening, the objection itself and a close, and because three four-option items with full rationales is already ~a third of a lesson's text.

**D10 — Fourteen scenarios over nine buyer segments and two seats, in the order of §9.** Seven per seat; the two principal buyer classes (developers and IPPs, AIDC landlords) get both modes; the gatekeepers (utilities, capital, assurance, insurance, EPC) get the mode their role in the sale calls for. *Why fourteen:* the corpus supports it (§2), five sessions at two to three scenarios each deliver it, and a sixth session is not in the plan. The unit is **per buyer segment × seat × mode**, not "per segment" as the §7.3 row reads — ten of the nineteen segments are supply-side and are not counterparties for either seat (rr23).

**D11 — Model: all five sessions on Opus 5 xhigh, including session 1's machinery.** Session 1 is a code slice (checker, page surface, one server function) plus the first two scenarios. K2 — a larger code slice — closed in one Opus session at v06.49r, and `PROFILER-COVERAGE-PLAN.md` §2 reserves Fable for design and reading depth, not for wiring a type through three checkers. *Cost:* session 1 is the longest of the five and should not take a third scenario.

## 4 · The scenario schema — `type: "scenario"` and the `scenario` block

Schema **v1, unchanged number** (D2). A scenario is a lesson literal that satisfies everything `CLASSROOM-SCHEMA.md` already requires of a lesson, plus the rows below. `CLASSROOM-SCHEMA.md` remains the single source of truth once session 1 lands: it copies this table in under the lesson schema and widens the `type` row to `module · briefing · scenario`; until then the schema carries a "planned, not built" pointer at this document.

| Field | Type | Required | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `type` | string | yes | `"scenario"` — the third value beside `module` (default) and `briefing`. Tracks refuse it (as they refuse briefings); the index does not list it; the Rehearsal library does |
| `scenario` | object | yes when `type` = `scenario`, forbidden otherwise | The block that makes it playable and checkable — five fields, all required |
| `scenario.mode` | string | yes | `objection` · `discovery` |
| `scenario.seat` | string | yes | `storage-seller` · `aidc-power-seller` — the two implicit role paths of curriculum plan §10.10, now a field |
| `scenario.segment` | string | yes | A registered segment id in `profiler-segments.json` — the **buyer's** segment; also the segment whose landscape the stamp must carry |
| `scenario.counterparty` | string | yes | A dossier slug that is an `incumbent` or `challenger` member of `scenario.segment` in the registry (adjacent members are not counterparties — they are in the segment by association, not as the desk that signs). The profile file must exist and `profile:<slug>` must be in the stamp |
| `scenario.stage` | string | yes | Where in the sale the room is: `prospecting` · `discovery` · `rfp` · `shortlist` · `negotiation` · `post-award` |
| `id` | string | yes | `scenario-<segment-id>-<mode>`, with `-storage` / `-aidc` appended only when the same segment and mode exist for both seats (today: `scenario-utilities-discovery` and `scenario-utilities-discovery-aidc`), and `-<n>` only for a second scenario of the same kind. Longest in §9 is 48 characters against the 64 limit. Function name `clLessonScenario<CamelSegment><Mode>_()` |
| `group` | string | yes | **`The Value Chain`** — a scenario is keyed to a segment, and its segment lesson (layer 3) and landscape (layer 4) already sit in that lane; a fifth lane was considered and rejected because a lane is a topic, not a form |
| `edition` | — | forbidden | Briefings only, unchanged |
| `tiles[]` | object[] | yes, exactly four, this order | `{ k: <counterparty display name>, v: "across the table" }` · `{ k: <segment name>, v: "the segment" }` · `{ k: "Objection" \| "Discovery", v: "the exercise" }` · `{ k: <stage, capitalised>, v: "where in the sale" }` |
| `glossary[]` | object[] | no | As any lesson — define locally what the concepts registry lacks |
| `short` | string | yes | ≤160 chars, and it **names the counterparty and the mode** so a library card reads without opening it |
| `updated` · `reviewBy` · `revisions[]` · `provenance` · `sections[]` | | yes | As any lesson; `reviewBy` and the stamp are constrained in §6, `sections[]` in §5 |

Everything a scenario is, its `scenario` block says in five words that a checker can compare against two registries: the seat against the §10.10 enum, the segment against `profiler-segments.json`, the counterparty against that segment's roster and the profile files, the stage against the enum, the mode against the enum. Nothing about the gate is stored — the fold does that (§6).

## 5 · The section template — ten fixed ids

Ten sections, these ids, this order, every one present (a section with nothing to say states that in one line rather than being omitted — ids are permanent and P5 never removes one). Section kinds are the existing nine; **no renderer change is needed for the content**, only for the library that lists it (§7).

| # | Section id | Kind | What it says | Drawn from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `the-room` | `callout` | Who you are (the seat, in one sentence), who is across the table (**roles**, never names — the buyer's desk, and whoever else the record says is in the room: the lender's independent engineer, the tax-equity bank, the GC's buying desk), the stage, and what is on the table. Two to four short paragraphs | `scenario.seat`, `scenario.stage`, the counterparty's `decisionMakers[]` **titles**, the landscape's `the-sellers-play` |
| 2 | `what-the-record-says` | `table` | The facts you walk in with. Cols `What you know` · `The record` · `Where it sits`. Six to ten rows; every row is also a ledger row. This is the discovery-prep half of both modes | `productsAndServices`, `relationships[]` (supplier/customer/investor with `status`, `scale`, `via`, `project`), `policyExposure[]`, `recentDevelopments[]`, `financials` KPI overlay, the project entry |
| 3 | `the-position` | `prose` | **Objection:** the counterparty's position as the record supports it — the incumbency, the rule it stands behind, the risk it is pricing — paraphrased, ledgered, unquoted (D4). **Discovery:** what the record does *not* settle and what the meeting therefore has to establish, in the order a good conversation would take. Two to four paragraphs, each premise traceable | `policyExposure[]` (the rule), `relationships[]` (the incumbency), `strategyRead[]` and the landscape's `each-players-bet` row (the posture — `analysis`), `the-indicators` (the clock) |
| 4 | `beat-1` | `quiz` | The opening. `intro` carries the counterparty's line for this beat (a paraphrased position, one to three sentences). `items` has **exactly one** item: `q` the situation question ("Your move?" / "What do you ask?"), `c` **exactly four** options, `a` the index of the strong move, `why` a rationale that addresses **all four** — why the strong move works against this record, and what each of the other three costs. `note` carries the conversation forward on the assumption the strong move was taken | The landscape's `the-sellers-play` numbered moves; the segment's `buyingCriteria[]`; the mechanism lesson's `sales` lines |
| 5 | `beat-2` | `quiz` | The objection itself (objection mode) or the question that decides whether there is a deal (discovery mode). Same shape as beat 1 | As beat 1, plus the fact row the objection rests on |
| 6 | `beat-3` | `quiz` | The close — the commitment asked for, the next step, or the walk-away test. Same shape | As beat 1, plus the stage's next rung from `how-a-storage-project-happens` / `the-campus-as-a-power-project` |
| 7 | `the-mechanism-behind-it` | `callout` | One paragraph per beat naming the **mechanism lesson** (by id and title, in prose — there is no `lesson:` prefix and this is not a stamp input) that teaches the test the beat turned on, and the one sentence of it that mattered. The bridge from evaluate back to understand | Curriculum plan §3 — the lesson ids; the segment lesson's `read-next` map is the starting list |
| 8 | `debrief` | `table` | Cols `Beat` · `The strong move` · `Why, against this record` · `The trap`. Three rows. The `sales` line on this section is the one sentence the reader should carry into the real room | The three `why` rationales, compressed |
| 9 | `claims-ledger` | `ledger` | Every premise in sections 1–3 and every fact a `why` leans on, one row each: `[claim, source]` where source is `profile:<slug> v<profileVersion> · <field>[<index>] · fact` or `guidance:<landscape-id> · <section-id> · analysis` (D5). Eight to fifteen rows. Session 1 writes the first and the pattern is fixed there | Everything above |
| 10 | `what-the-record-does-not-say` | `callout` | Numbered: what you would have to learn in the room because no dossier carries it — the buyer's actual FEOC position, the closing date, the incumbent's terms, the operating figure behind the headline. In discovery mode this is the question list; in objection mode it is the list of things the seller must ask rather than assert | The dossier's own gaps, the landscape's `what-the-record-does-not-say` |

**Rules for the beats, stated once.** Four options, one strong move, three that cost something real and nameable — never a straw man, never "say nothing" unless the record supports silence, never an option that requires knowledge outside sections 1–3. The strong move answers the **rule**, not the person (D4). Beats escalate: opening → the thing that decides it → the close. The `why` is the lesson: it must name, for each losing option, which section of the record or which mechanism it ignored. No beat may depend on a fact that is not in `what-the-record-says` or the ledger — a reader who has read sections 1–3 has everything the beat needs. **Micro-markup rules are the lesson rules** (one level of emphasis, no nested `*`, no `[c:id]` tokens; `{{term}}` resolves lesson-glossary-first then the concepts registry).

**No `flashcards` section and no free-standing `quiz`** beyond the three beats, so a scenario has exactly three `quiz` items — and D7 keeps even those out of the deck.

## 6 · The stamp, the gate, and the review date

**Required inputs.** `profile:<counterparty>` with `date` = the `lastUpdated` read off the fetched `<slug>.profile.json`; **`guidance:landscape-<segment>-YYYY-MM`** for `scenario.segment`, with `date` = that module's `updated` in `guidanceDocs_()`. Both are always genuine inputs: the first is where every fact row comes from, the second is where the strong move and the posture come from.

**Conditional inputs, each only when a ledger row cites it.** `profile:<other slug>` for every other dossier a fact row rests on (the incumbent supplier, the competitor named in the objection, the tax-equity investor); `project:<slug>` when the room is set at a named project, dated by the file's last commit on the base revision (`git log -1 --format=%cs -- live-site-pages/profiler-data/profiler-projects.json`, on an **unshallowed** clone — the pin trap the regeneration passes measured); `graph:profiler-graph` when a `who-is-connected`-style edge is a premise; `concepts:profiler-concepts` when a `{{term}}` resolves there and the lesson defines it nowhere — never as a citation of convenience; `study:<slug>` is permitted and unlikely.

**Forbidden inputs.** `report:` (D3 — no scenario needs a ranking, and one would lock the scenario to admin); `corpus:` and `briefing:` (a scenario is not week-bound, and a briefing input would pull the fold to `briefing`, which gates identically to `guidance` but misdescribes the source). There is no `note:` prefix and never will be.

**The gate is asserted, not stored.** `clGateForProvenance_(clStampKinds_(scenario))` must equal **`guidance`** — the checker computes it from the stamp exactly as the server does, and errors on any other answer. A scenario whose fold reads `tracks` has lost its landscape input; one that reads `reports` has gained a report; both are authoring defects, not gate options.

**`reviewBy`.** The nearest dated gate among the ledgered claims — a policy `effectiveDate`, a closing, a tariff step, a certificate hearing — read out of the prose as well as the date fields (curriculum plan §10.6 (d), (g), (k): the gate can sit inside an `exposure` paragraph, and a sort of the date fields can return nothing or the wrong answer). **And never later than the landscape's own `reviewBy`**: a scenario cannot outlive the judgment it rests on. The checker warns when it does. Consequence to expect and accept: scenarios built on `landscape-utilities-2026-09` (`reviewBy` 2026-10-01) or `landscape-neoclouds-2026-09` (2026-09-30) ship inside their own 30-day horizon and render a gold chip on day one — the rule working, exactly as §10.6 (k) recorded for the utilities landscape itself. The quarterly guidance review is the backstop that moves both together (§12 item 2).

**Pins are read, never carried.** Every date in the stamp is a value the authoring session read off a document it fetched that run, against a freshly fetched `origin/main` — curriculum plan §8 item 2, and G2/G7 of the contract binding a developer session in spirit. ## 14 · Findings from the design session — (rr23) to (rr29)

Written into the register the next session reads — `CLASSROOM-CURRICULUM-PLAN.md` §10.6 — in the same commit, per (rr16); summarised here because they shaped the decisions.

- **(rr23)** The unit of C5 is not "per segment". Measured against the registry, **nine** of the nineteen segments are buyers for the two seats; the other ten are supply-side. The row's "~5 authoring sessions" holds only at two to three scenarios per session with one session carrying the machinery — D10 and D11.
- **(rr24)** `IMPROVEMENT-PLAN.md` §5's premise for the gate — "a scenario built from a report is admin-only, which is most of them" — inverts under measurement: every fact layer C5 draws on is public and the only judgment layer it needs is the contributor-tier landscape, so **none** need be admin-only. The same paragraph's counts are stale by more than double (60 → 147 dossiers with `policyExposure[]`; 497 → 1,362 relationships; 8 → 9 projects). (kk1)/(jj1) applied to a plan's premise rather than to a handover's number.
- **(rr25)** Two documents disagreed about what a shape change is. `IMPROVEMENT-PLAN.md` assumed the new `type` forces a schema bump and an adapter; `CLASSROOM-SCHEMA.md` "Extending the schema" rule 2 says a bump is for a shape change and additive optional fields need none. The hidden cost decided it: `CLASSROOM-COMMITTER-CONTRACT.md` §5.1 step 4 turns a version mismatch into a `BLOCKED` pre-flight, so a bump would have stopped every unattended run until the contract was rewritten — D2.
- **(rr26)** `clStudyNext_` walks `clTracks_()` only. The contract's §4.4 and the P5 row of `classroom-app.md` both say it walks "registry order then track order". The drift is benign for C5 — a scenario in no track is unreachable by study-next with no code change (D8) — and is recorded for the developer rather than fixed, because the contract is the developer's file (§12 item 7).
- **(rr27)** `clRenderIndex` already partitions cards by `type` — briefings as a dated feed, everything else as loose modules by lane. A `type: scenario` card with no third partition lands among the loose modules, which is the concrete reason the discriminator is a declared type and not an id prefix: the prefix route would have needed the same page edit plus a string test in three more places — D1.
- **(rr28)** Project-pinned relationships concentrate: **45 of 62** sit on three projects (Stargate 24, Lighthouse 11, Hyperion 10). A room set at a named project has material only at the top four, and Stargate's twenty-four parties make it a report's subject rather than a rehearsal's — the inventory uses Lighthouse and River Bend from two sides each and sets no scenario at Stargate itself (§9).
- **(rr29)** The seat split is already in the landscapes, but not uniformly: `the-sellers-play` names the storage seller in 8 of 19 modules and the AI-data-centre power seller in 8, **both in 6**. So `scenario.seat` cannot be read off the landscape mechanically; the authoring session chooses it and reads the landscape's play through it. Where a module addresses one seat only, the other seat's scenario for that segment leans harder on the mechanism lessons — which is the case for `landscape-aidc-developers-and-landlords-2026-09` (storage seller named, AIDC seat not), the segment §9 gives two AIDC-seat scenarios.
