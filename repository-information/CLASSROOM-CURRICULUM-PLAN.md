# Classroom Curriculum Plan

**Status:** design document — planning output 2026-09-02, **re-planned 2026-09-07 at v05.07r** (Phase 3 of `INTEGRATED-REMEDIATION-PLAN.md`, Fable 5.1 xhigh — design and evaluation against the grown corpus) and **extended 2026-09-07 at v05.09r with §10 — the segment layer** (S0 of the same plan's §7, Fable 5.1 xhigh; the dated revision log is §9). Ten of the lessons below are built — the five that existed at planning plus the §7 first five, all in `Classroom.gs` — and the other twenty-six are not (the twenty-four of the re-plan plus `reading-the-numbers` and `interconnection-for-large-loads`, specified at S0): no lesson JSON was authored at the re-plan or at S0, `Classroom.gs` was not touched, and every unbuilt id is still a *proposed* permanent id: once one is registered it can never change (`CLASSROOM-SCHEMA.md`, Id rules), so treat the id column as the thing to argue about before an authoring session starts.

**What this is for.** One person is learning the US BESS/AIDC market well enough to sell into it. Classroom (`PHASE6-CLASSROOM-DESIGN.md`) is the institution that turns the Profiler corpus into taught, retained, current knowledge. At the 2026-09-02 planning it held five lessons across two tracks; at the v05.07r re-plan it holds ten across three (`bess-foundations`, `aidc-grid-to-chip`, `aidc-campus`). This plan says what the corpus can support teaching, proposes the track structure that fits the material, specifies every lesson to the level an authoring session can build from, registers the gaps worth commissioning research for, and draws the cut line.

**The constraint that shapes everything.** A lesson exists only if it can be stamped: `provenance.inputs[]` pins the corpus material it was built from, and the lesson inherits the strictest gate of its inputs (`profile:` · `study:` · `project:` · `graph:` · `concepts:` → `tracks`, analyst and up; `guidance:` → contributor+; `report:` → admin only; field notes never). So the question this plan answers is not "what would be nice to teach" but "what does this corpus support, and where are the gaps worth filling". Every input named below is a source this planning session actually read; an authoring session re-reads it and pins the date off the fetched document (G2, `.claude/rules/classroom-app.md`).

**Reading order.** §1 inventories the corpus. §2 argues the track structure. §3 specifies every lesson. §4 fixes teaching order. §5 is the cross-cutting failure-point map the AIDC track is built around. §6 is the gap register — what to commission before certain lessons can exist. §7 is the cut line. §8 is what an authoring session needs to know before it opens `Classroom.gs`. §9 is the dated revision log — what was re-planned at v05.07r and what S0 added at v05.09r, against which corpus. **§10 is the segment layer** — the five-layer model, the floor rule, the generated segment lessons and their generator, the three Value Chain tracks under a fourth lane, the landscape modules, the K1 and K2 specs, the health script and the two implicit role paths.

## 2 · Recommended track structure

### 2.1 · The two existing tracks are the right lanes and the wrong cut

The `group` vocabulary — *Technology Foundations*, *The AI Data-Center Wave*, *Market Access & Bankability* — is the right taxonomy: it is the guidance lanes, it is what the index groups by, and it maps cleanly onto the stamp gates. Keep it. The two **tracks** built on it in C1 were a first assembly from what was easiest to stamp, and the seams show in three places:

1. **`bess-foundations` stops at the product.** It teaches the cell, the container, duration, degradation and the spec sheet, and never teaches the control layer that actually operates the asset (BMS → EMS → PCS: who decides what, second by second) or where the cells come from. A seller who has finished it can read a spec sheet and cannot explain what the EMS is trading off every afternoon — which is the conversation a developer's asset manager wants to have.
2. **`aidc-power-primer` starts at the service entrance.** It is two good lessons about the building (distribution, UPS, transfer; heat). Everything *before* the fence — interconnection, the substation, the transformer queue, and the on-site generation that now powers whole campuses — is missing, and that is precisely the half of the chain a **BESS** seller can enter. The inside-the-rack half (PSU, 48 V, the rack as one machine, 800 VDC) is also missing, and that is the half every customer conversation drifts toward.
3. **There is no track in the third lane at all.** Three of the seven guidance modules and every IPP/developer/EPC dossier are market-access material, and none of it is taught. For a *sales* curriculum, the lane that explains who buys, under what contract, against which certification file, inside which policy fence, cannot be the one with zero lessons.

A fourth, structural point: **both markets sit on the same electrical layer** — conversion physics, transformers, switchgear, protection, grid stability — and the current cut has no home for it. Teaching it twice (once inside BESS, once inside AIDC) would duplicate; teaching it nowhere leaves both tracks assuming knowledge a high-school-STEM reader does not have. It wants its own track under Technology Foundations, with both market tracks pointing at it as a prerequisite.

**Why five and not two.** Track length is a learning-design number, not a taxonomy number. Registry order is teaching order and the study-next pointer walks it literally (`clStudyNext_`: tracks in `clTracks_()` order, then each track's `lessons[]`), so a twelve-lesson track is a four-hour commitment with no natural landing between "I understand the grid interface" and "I understand the rack". Splitting the AIDC lane into the *equipment walk* (grid → chip, in physical order) and the *campus* (heat, water, worked examples, the BESS socket) gives the reader a completion point exactly where the material changes from "how it works" to "where I fit". The `prereqs[]` field (advisory, never blocking) carries the dependencies: tracks 3 and 4 name track 2; track 5 names tracks 1 and 3.

**The four lessons admitted at v05.07r keep every track under the line that paragraph draws.** The longest track is now `aidc-grid-to-chip` at ten — two short of the twelve the paragraph refuses *(S0, v05.09r: eleven with the G6 lesson at position 2 — one short of twelve, and the analyst still walks ten because the new lesson is guidance-gated; `market-access` goes to nine, five public then four guidance)* — and the two lessons added to it (`backup-generation`, `the-ups-room`) are equipment-walk lessons that sit in physical order between the service entrance and the rack, so the how-it-works → where-I-fit landing between tracks 3 and 4 is unchanged. `aidc-campus` goes to five; `market-access` goes to eight and stays four public then four guidance, so an analyst still finishes something coherent before the withheld count begins. No track was split and none created: the track ids are the five above.

**Why `aidc-power-primer` is replaced rather than extended.** Its two lessons are good and keep their ids — they simply move into `aidc-grid-to-chip` at their correct positions in the chain (lesson 3 and, for `heat-is-the-constraint`, into `aidc-campus` as lesson 1). A track id is permanent once progress keys on it; today nobody's progress rolls up to `aidc-power-primer` except the developer's, and the rollup is per-lesson underneath, so retiring the track id now — before analysts exist — costs nothing and avoids carrying a title that stops describing its contents. If the developer would rather keep the id, the same lesson list works under the old id with a new title; it is a naming choice, not a design one.

**What does not earn a place — and why. Re-tested 2026-09-07 (v05.07r) against the §6 closures.** The corpus has one study guide each on several technologies, and the temptation is to give each a lesson. The test applied, unchanged from 2026-09-02: *would a BESS/AIDC seller lose a conversation for not knowing it?* A dossier existing is necessary, not sufficient. The re-test counted, per topic, the guides and sections that carry it across all 154 guides and the terms the registry resolves, and then asked the question again. Eight verdicts, each with its reason; the two the remediation plan named that are not among the eight (cooling, utility) follow them.

- **Solar PV physics** (Jinko: band gaps, passivation, bifacial) — **KEEP.** Jinko is still the one physics guide (three sections); the registry resolves four PV terms. What grew is the *plant* side — Recurrent, Lightsource bp, RWE, AES, Clearway, Intersect, all solar-plus-storage owners — and that is where the seller's solar conversation lives: the built `the-control-stack` carries the midday solar glut in `a-day-in-dispatch`, and `how-a-storage-project-happens` pins Primoris. No conversation is lost over the cell.
- **Hydrogen / electrolyzers** (Black & Veatch, Siemens Energy) — **KEEP, reason rewritten.** The 2026-09-02 reason — "thin corpus, no concept entries" — is no longer true: 23 guides mention hydrogen and the registry holds twelve entries (hydrogen co-firing, solid-oxide fuel cell, HVO, flame speed, flashback, ammonia…) because Mitsubishi Power (`hydrogen-by-volume`), Bloom (SOFC) and Siemens Energy (`industrializing-the-electrolyzer`) landed. A section, not a lesson.

### 3.1 · Track `bess-foundations` — BESS Foundations (Technology Foundations)

**Track `short` (revised):** "Finish this and you can read a grid-storage spec sheet, explain what the control stack is trading off every afternoon, say where the cells came from, and know where batteries stop and engines begin." **Order:** `cell-to-container` → `duration-and-degradation` → `spec-sheet-decoded` → `the-control-stack` → `how-a-cell-is-made` → `where-batteries-stop`. #### `the-control-stack` — BMS, EMS, PCS: Who Decides What
- **short:** Three layers of control run a storage plant — one protects the cells, one converts the power, one decides the day. What each owns, and where the money is made and lost.
- **group:** Technology Foundations · **gate:** `tracks` (public)
- **inputs:** `study:flexgen` (what an EMS is; a site's day — dispatch, state of charge, warranty budget, augmentation mixing) · `study:sinexcel` (the PCS as gatekeeper: follows grid commands in milliseconds, rides through faults; harmonics and power factor) · `study:wartsila` (GEMS as fleet control; frequency response as the fastest responder) · `study:fluence` (the project cast — who operates, who warrants) · `concepts:profiler-concepts` (BMS, EMS, PCS, round-trip efficiency)
- **outline:**

  | Section id | Kind | Teaches |
  |---|---|---|
  | `three-layers` | prose | BMS (cell-level guardian: voltage, temperature, balancing, disconnect) · PCS (the bidirectional gate: MW rating, grid commands, ride-through) · EMS (the supervisor: price, contract, warranty budget). Which layer a given number belongs to |
  | `a-day-in-dispatch` | timeline | One 24-hour day on three lanes — price, state of charge, dispatch — midday charge on the solar glut, ancillary readiness through the afternoon, evening discharge, the warranty cycle budget as the ceiling the whole day runs under |
  | `who-owns-which-number` | table | Number (SoC window · cycle count · MW setpoint · ride-through · balancing) → owning layer → what goes wrong if that layer is wrong |
  | `the-warranty-envelope` | callout | Cycling harder earns more today and ages the cells measurably; the EMS is an optimiser inside a contract, not a trader |
  | `where-it-fails` | callout | BMS communication loss = a string offline; EMS mis-dispatch = a voided warranty; PCS anti-islanding trips during grid faults = revenue and ride-through penalties. Why each failure lands on the layer it does |
  | `drill` · `check-yourself` | flashcards · quiz | 6 cards, 5 items — mechanism, never product names |
- **why:** After `duration-and-degradation` the reader knows the asset earns by stacking revenue but cannot say *who* decides, at 4:55 pm, whether to discharge — or why a developer's asset manager and a warranty engineer disagree. This lesson lets the seller hold that conversation and recognise when a tender's control requirements (ride-through, fleet dispatch, warranty reporting) are the real spec.

#### `four-machines` — Rectifier, Inverter, DC-DC, Transformer

**Two ordering rules the authoring sessions must respect:**

1. **Within a track, the equipment walk is in physical order** — grid → fence → substation → building → rack → die — and the market track goes buyer → project → contract → file → policy → procurement. Do not reorder for gate convenience (e.g. moving guidance lessons to the end): the study-next pointer already skips withheld lessons, so an analyst walking `aidc-grid-to-chip` lands on `where-the-chain-breaks` straight after `the-800-vdc-shift` and loses nothing.

**Insertion versus append.** The unattended pipeline may only *append* to a registry or a track's `lessons[]` (P5). A developer authoring session is not bound by P5 and should place each new lesson at its position above, re-sequencing `clLessons_()` and `lessons[]` in the same commit — then run `check-classroom-pipeline.py --base origin/main` and expect P5 findings on that commit as the known cost of a curriculum reorder (the contract's §7 selftest must still pass; P5 is a diff assertion on pipeline commits, not a schema rule). ## 5 · The failure-point map (grid to chip)

This is the spec for `where-the-chain-breaks` and the checklist every equipment lesson's `where-it-fails` section draws its row from. It is built from the study guides only (public), and each row names the guide that carries it so the authoring session pins what it actually uses. Four causes recur, and the lesson says so: **current squared becomes heat**, **direct current has no zero crossing**, **heat flows through a series path**, and **human hands are in the loop**.

| # | Stage (grid → chip) | Common failure | Why it fails there | Who owns it | Carried by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interconnection | The date moves: study re-runs, queue re-pricing, an audit that pauses a batch | The study models the grid as it will be, and the grid keeps changing; the requester funds upgrades it does not control | Developer · utility | `study:burns-mcdonnell`, `study:stack-infrastructure` |
| 2 | Substation transformer | Bushing failure; moisture in paper insulation; tap-changer wear; a slot that slips | Bushings carry a conductor through a grounded wall — the highest-stress interface in the tank; paper that was not dried enough ages fast; the unit is custom and the queue is people-limited | OEM · owner's engineer | `study:hitachi-energy`, `study:ge-vernova`, `study:mortenson` |
| 3 | MV switchgear and protection | Miscoordination (a downstream fault opens a main); relay settings never re-checked after a load change; arc-flash energy | Time-current curves converge at high fault current; protection is a networked application now and drifts like software | Electrical contractor · EPC | `study:abb`, `study:eaton` |
| 4 | On-site generation | Turbines at part load or in hot thin air; air permits that lag the shell; fuel supply; a slot reservation that slips | A large turbine is efficient only at full load; the permit is for what comes out of the stack, not for the building | Power partner · developer | `study:kiewit`, `study:wartsila`, `study:xai`, `study:vantage` |
| 5 | Transfer switch and generator paralleling | Open transition with no stored energy; generators that do not synchronise; load that is not shed in priority | An open transition deliberately breaks before it makes; paralleling is a control problem with a human-written sequence | Electrical contractor · operator | `study:eaton`, `study:rosendin` |
| 6 | UPS | Load exposed on bypass while a monolithic unit waits for a specialist; eco-mode detection that is fast for the typical fault and not for every fault; modules parked below their efficiency sweet spot | Hold-up time is 10–20 ms and a monolith's repair time is hours; redundancy taxes efficiency | Operator · OEM | `study:eaton`, `study:huawei-digital-power`, `study:liteon` |
| 7 | Distribution and busway | Harmonic heating of transformers and neutrals; power-factor penalties; a tap-off added without a coordination re-check | Chopped current reflects distortion back into the building; distortion is heat doing no work | Electrical contractor · operator | `study:eaton`, `study:sinexcel` |
| 8 | Rack whip and PDU | Termination quality; A/B feeds that share an upstream point | Thousands of terminations are proven only in commissioning; redundancy that meets upstream is not redundancy | Electrical contractor · commissioning agent | `study:rosendin`, `study:hitt` |
| 9 | Server power supply | Inrush on a live swap; a failed unit dragging the shared bus; two supplies each at half load | ORing and precharge are engineered, not given; efficiency curves peak near full load | OEM · operator | `study:liteon` |
| 10 | 48 V bus and the last centimetre | A bus that behaves like a heater; a thousand amps with no thermal margin at the regulator | Loss scales with current squared; the final conversion is millimetres from the silicon | Server OEM | `study:delta-electronics` |
| 11 | DC distribution (800 VDC) | A sustained series arc; a stage deleted without a plan for its fault duty; a stock PSU never rated for DC | A DC arc has no zero crossing and must be engineered out; every stage removed makes the survivor's job harder | OEM · electrical contractor | `study:zhonhen`, `study:abb`, `study:delta-electronics` |
| 12 | The load itself | Tens of megawatts swinging in milliseconds, periodically, exciting resonances below line frequency | Training steps are rhythmic; the grid has never served a load of this shape at this scale | Operator · utility · buffer vendor | `study:eaton`, `study:megmeet` |
| 13 | The thermal chain (cooling) | One exchanger's approach pinched; recirculation for want of blanking panels; a sequence of operations that heats and cools at once | Heat flows in series and the worst stage limits the path; the sequence is invisible and decisive | Mechanical contractor · operator | `study:vertiv`, `study:schneider-electric` |
| 14 | Commissioning | Integrated tests skipped or run on temporary power; "rated for" read as "operating at" | Level 5 is the theatre of failure — what was not pulled under load was not proven | Commissioning agent · GC | `study:hitt`, `study:switch` |

Rows 1–12 are the power chain the developer asked for; rows 13–14 are the two failures that most often *present* as power failures and are not. The capstone's quiz is scenario-based ("the hall browned out for 40 ms and one row rebooted — which row of this table?"), which is the drillable form of the skill.

Two cross-cutting feeds ride the layers rather than sitting in them: the **briefing** (the pipeline's dated edition, any gate the inputs fold to) and the **drill** (every card and quiz item in layers 2–3, plus the opt-in roster deck of §10.8). Content flows up the layers and never down the access ladder: a segment lesson is public because its every input is; a landscape is contributor-tier because judgment about named companies is what the 2026-08-29 content-scope rule kept out of public modules — §7.2 moved that fence to contributor, not to public; a report is admin because it ranks. The gate is the stamp's fold in every case (`CLASSROOM-SCHEMA.md`), never a field.

**Why generated, and why a human layer above it.** Layer 3 states what the record states — the registry's roles, the dossiers' own product lines and figures, the graph's edges, the developments' dates — and drills structure (the amended content contract). It contains no judgment, so it can be regenerated without anyone re-reading nineteen lessons. Layer 4 is the judgment, and judgment is authored: it cites the dossiers it rests on in a claims ledger and carries a `reviewBy`. The seam between them is the reason the layer can scale with the corpus: S3 adds a dossier, the Profiler Command assigns its segment, the next generator run rewrites one lesson's player table, and the landscape is revised only when the new player changes a judgment.

### 10.3 · The segment-lesson template

One lesson per segment, **schema v1, no shape change** — an ordinary `clLesson<Name>_()` literal inside the content fence, distinguishable only by its id prefix and by the fact that a script wrote it. Everything below is fixed so the generator is deterministic and a regeneration diffs cleanly.

- **Identity.** `id` = `segment-<segment id>` (e.g. `segment-cells-and-chemistry`) — permanent, because progress and the roster deck key on it; the segment id is itself permanent (PROFILER-SCHEMA.md → Segments registry). Function name `clLessonSegment<CamelId>_()`. `type` = `module`. `title` = the segment's `name`. `short` = the first sentence of its `definition`, cut at 160 characters at a word boundary. **`group` = `The Value Chain`** — a fourth lane, added because none of the three guidance lanes fits a segment (the schema's own rule: add a lane only when none fits; recorded here as that decision).
- **Dates.** `updated` = the generation date. `reviewBy` = the earliest **future** `effectiveDate` among the members' `policyExposure[]` entries (the fence's own dated gate), else `updated` + 6 months — the guidance discipline's default for slow-moving fundamentals.
- **The stamp** — every member `profile:<slug>` with `date` = that profile's `lastUpdated` **read off the fetched file** (G2), plus `graph:profiler-graph` with `date` = the graph's `built`, plus `concepts:profiler-concepts` with `date` = the file's last commit date on the base revision (PROFILER-SCHEMA.md → Registry revision signals). Every input is `public`, so the stamp folds to **`tracks`** — analyst-visible. No `study:`, no `guidance:`, no `report:` ever enters a segment lesson's stamp: the numbers and the fence are read from the profiles, and the judgment lives in the landscape. A `note` per input records what the lesson took ("member — incumbent" / "edges among members" / "term resolution").
- **Tiles** (up to four): members on record · incumbents · challengers · curated edges among members.
- **No lesson-local glossary.** `{{term}}` tooltips resolve against the concepts registry (1,210 terms); the generator warns on any term it emits that the registry lacks, exactly as `check-classroom-content.py` does.
- **Sections — ten, fixed ids, this order.** A section with nothing on record is still emitted with a single stated line ("No member carries a normalized figure for this period"), never omitted: ids are permanent and P5 never removes one.

| # | Section id | Kind | Generated from | What it says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `the-segment` | prose | `definition` | What the segment makes or does; one paragraph, the registry's words |
| 2 | `where-it-sits` | table | `position`, `tier`, the segments at `position ± 1`, and the demand segments that buy from it | Upstream · this segment · downstream, and who the buyers are |
| 3 | `what-is-bought-and-on-what` | table | `buyingCriteria[]` | One row per criterion — the criterion and, where a mechanism lesson teaches it, the lesson id |
| 4 | `the-players` | table | `members[]` grouped incumbent → challenger → adjacent | Company · **dossier slug** (the identity the content checker's segment assertion and the roster deck key on — added at S1 because the Classroom renderer has no chip syntax; a clickable dossier chip is a renderer feature for K2 or C3) · role · the `basis` line. **The roster deck's source (§10.8)** |
| 5 | `the-numbers` | table | Each member's `financials.periods[]` **normalized KPI overlay only** — `revenue` (`usdMillions`, `fxBasis`), `gwh-shipped`, `backlog-gwh`, `mw-energized`, `mw-contracted` (`qty`) for the latest annual period, with `periodEnd` | The comparable figures the dossiers already carry, and only those; a member with no overlay row is listed as such. No `flashcards` section: the roster deck (§10.8) enumerates its cards from `the-players` rows, so a segment lesson adds nothing to the mechanism deck and adds to the roster deck only when an account opts in.

- **Revisions.** The first generation carries no `revisions[]` entry. Every regeneration that changes anything appends exactly one — `{ "date": <generation date>, "note": "regenerated: <the inputs that moved, as ref old→new>", "changed": [<the section ids whose generated JSON differs>] }` — computed by **diffing the sections as P8 does** (parsed JSON, base → head), so `changed[]` is the differs set and nothing else, and the freshness delta reaches every account that completed a section. `updated` advances; pins advance only on the inputs that moved and only to the dates read off them.

### 10.4 · The generator — `scripts/build-classroom-segments.py` (S1, Fable 5.1 High; built v05.17r)

- **Inputs:** `profiler-segments.json`, `profiler-companies.json`, every member's `<slug>.profile.json`, `profiler-graph.json`, `profiler-concepts.json`, and the base revision's commit date for the concepts file. **Output:** the nineteen `clLessonSegment*_()` literals in `Classroom.gs`, inside the content fence — a new literal appended after the last existing literal and before the registries, a regenerated one **replaced in place, byte for byte where nothing moved** — plus the ids appended to `clLessons_()` and to the three Value Chain tracks' `lessons[]` in chain order (first run only).
- **Stable ids, fixed sections, deterministic output.** Same inputs → the same bytes: sorted members, sorted edges, stable JSON key order, no timestamps but `updated`. That is what makes "regenerate only what moved" a diff rather than a rewrite.
- **Regenerate only segments whose members moved.** A segment is **due** when any of: a member profile's `lastUpdated` is later than its pin; the graph's `built` is later than its pin; the concepts file's commit date is later than its pin; the segment's member set or any role in the registry differs from the lesson's `the-players`; the registry's `definition` or `buyingCriteria` differ from sections 1–3. `--check` lists due segments and exits 1 if any (the health script calls it); `--segment <id>` regenerates one; `--all` forces all nineteen; `--today` sets the generation date for a reproducible run. Nothing due → nothing written, and the run says so.
- **A developer-session tool, not a pipeline one.** The Routine never runs it: a run that regenerated nineteen lessons would fail P10's caps and, more to the point, a regeneration is not a G3 contradiction — it is the record restated. The developer (or the S1/S3 session) runs it inside a session, then does what every content commit does: bumps the GAS version, writes the generic changelog line ("Curriculum updated"), runs `check-classroom-content.py`, `check-classroom-pipeline.py --base origin/main` (expect P1 on the developer files and P5 on the first run's registry inserts; **never P3** — the generator touches no gate symbol, so `gateDigest` is unchanged), `--selftest`, `node --check` and `check-gas-inner-scripts.js`. The pipeline treats `segment-*` lessons as **frozen**: it never revises one, and a run whose pin table finds a segment lesson's inputs moved reports it under `Segment lessons due for regeneration` rather than acting (`classroom-app.md`, the content-contract section).
- **What it never does.** No prose beyond the registry's own sentences and the dossiers' own headlines; no ranking beyond the registry's role; no `strategyRead` (that is analysis, and analysis is the landscape's); no `study:` or report input; no field the overlay does not carry; no invented figure; no new section id, ever.
- **Verification it carries itself:** every `profile:` ref resolved by opening the file and matching `slug` (G7); every emitted `{{term}}` checked against the registry; the ten section ids present in order; the-players rows equal to the registry's member set; JSON strictness (double-quoted keys, no trailing commas). Then the two Classroom checkers judge the commit as they judge any.

### 10.7 · K1 — the study-pool fix (Fable 5.1 High; independent of S1)

### 10.8 · K2 — the roster deck (Opus 5 xhigh; after S1)

### 10.9 · The health script — `scripts/check-classroom-curriculum.py` (S1, Fable 5.1 High; built v05.17r)

- **Lessons per lane and per track**, against §4 and §10.5 — and the gate distribution per lane (`tracks` / `guidance` / `briefing` / `reports`), so the analyst-visible share is a number.
- **Segment coverage vs the floor** — per segment: members, incumbents, challengers, floor met, segment lesson present, landscape module present. *Strict:* a landscape on a below-floor segment; a registered segment with no lesson (after S1); a member slug with no profile; `unassigned[]` non-empty is reported, not strict.
- **Stale pins** — per lesson, inputs whose live date is later than the pin (the pipeline's candidate list, made visible); for `segment-*` lessons this is **"regeneration due"** (it calls the generator's `--check`).
- **Drill pool size** — `sf` · `ss` · `lc` · `lq` · `rc` counts against their caps, so the study-pool gap of §8 item 5 could never again go unnoticed.
- **Review dates** — every lesson and module `reviewBy` within 30 days or passed.

And **one assertion added to `check-classroom-content.py`** (the schema checker, so it fails a commit): for every `segment-<id>` lesson, the ten section ids are present in order, its `profile:` inputs equal the registry's member set for that segment exactly, and `the-players` rows equal the members — a generated lesson that drifted from its registry is an error.

### 10.10 · The two implicit role paths

Two readers use the same curriculum in a different order, and neither path is a field in the data — the study-next pointer walks registry order and `prereqs[]` only suggests. They are written down so a session (and later C6's placement quiz) can point a reader at the right door:

- **The storage seller** — product first: `bess-foundations` → `electrical-foundations` → **Value Chain I** → `market-access` (through `reading-the-numbers`) → **Value Chain III** → then `aidc-grid-to-chip` and `aidc-campus` for the socket → landscapes for integrators, cells, developers and IPPs, utilities, capital (contributor).
- **The AIDC power seller** — the chain first: `electrical-foundations` → `aidc-grid-to-chip` (through `interconnection-for-large-loads` for contributors) → `aidc-campus` → **Value Chain II** → **Value Chain III** → then `bess-foundations` and `market-access` → landscapes for landlords, hyperscalers, bridge generation, in-hall power, grid equipment, EPC.

Both begin with `reading-the-graph` before any segment lesson, and both end on the landscapes their tier can read. When C6 makes a role explicit (a placement quiz that sets a starting track), it sets one of these two; until then they are documentation.
