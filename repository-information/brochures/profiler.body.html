What Profiler Does01

A dossier you can trust twice

The value of Profiler is not that it holds information about companies — it is that the information is shaped identically for all 89 of them, and that you can always see where each claim came from.

The dossier — ten standard sections

Open any company and you get the same tabs, in the same order. Nothing is bespoke.

Snapshot

Identity, HQ, ticker, ownership, employees, and the company's role in the ecosystem.

Products & Services

The priority section. Full product-line enumeration with positioning against named competitors, sold-through model, target segments, and dated roadmap.

Technical Specifications

Flagship-product specs — the numbers you would be asked about in a technical conversation.

Recent Developments

Trailing 12–18 months, each event carrying a strategy takeaway rather than a bare headline.

Relationships

Who they supply, buy from, partner with, and compete against — with inbound evidence from other dossiers.

Policy Exposure

Tariffs, credits, domestic-content rules, and the regulatory surface that moves their economics.

Decision Makers

Names, titles, career backgrounds, verified LinkedIn URLs, company-published photos.

Financials vs Expectations

Reported results set against analyst consensus — the honest version, never sourced from the company alone.

Strategy Read

Explicitly labeled analysis. Inference from the sourced facts — never presented as a company statement.

Sources

Every citation, tiered by party. First-party IR/PR first, then filings, then trade press.

How Profiler Works02

Two-stage research, and four rules that never bend

A dossier is only as good as its discipline. Profiler's is written down, applied to all 89 companies identically, and enforced by verification scripts that run before anything is published.

The research protocol

Stage 1 — First party
Exhaust the company's own channels first: investor relations, annual and interim reports, results releases, the full press archive back 24 months, product and datasheet pages, leadership pages.
Stage 2 — Third party
Fill and check: exchange filings, trade press, consultancy rankings. This supplies what a company cannot credibly say about itself — consensus, independent rankings, litigation, misses.
Stage 3 — Cite in order
Company IR/PR/product pages, then filings, then trade press. Figures available only from an aggregator are marked as such rather than quietly promoted.

Products & Services gets the research budget first. Depth is allocated to the full product-line enumeration — names, specs, competitive positioning, roadmaps with dates — before identity and snapshot facts are rounded out. That ordering is deliberate: the product line is what you actually sell against.

The four standing rules
  • Analysis stays labeled. Every inference carries a visible tag — "We assess — analytic judgments from open sources, not company statements." A reader can always tell fact from read.
  • Notes are not sources. Your private field notes never appear as a citation and are never blended into a sourced section. They may steer research and shape the strategy read; they can never masquerade as public fact.
  • Expectations honesty. A "beat" or "miss" verdict is never sourced from the company. Consensus comes from independent coverage or it is not stated.
  • Standard treatment. Every company gets exactly the standard schema. No bespoke sections, no favourites — which is the whole reason comparison works.

Five house styles, one set of facts

The schema decides what a dossier contains; the active writing style decides how it reads. Five are registered — default analyst prose, Bloomberg research-report form, equity-research sell-side note, intel-briefing (currently active) with confidence-tagged judgments, and Smart Brevity. Switching restyles the app, the section labels and the exported document. It never changes the rules above: public sources only, expectations honesty, notes-are-not-sources and labeled analysis apply identically under every style.

Verified, not just written

A profile write is not finished when the JSON is saved. A registry sync reconciles the roster's coverage line from the profile itself, the ecosystem graph is rebuilt so the Relationships tab and Network explorer cannot go stale, the report checker validates every citation and coverage pin, and a role-matrix script asserts that each of the ten gated surfaces still resolves to the right tier. A write that fails any of these is incomplete, not published.

Field notes — your private layer

Everything else in Profiler is public disclosure. Field notes are the one place your own observations live — what a contact said, what you saw at a site, what a competitor let slip. They are stored in Drive behind the access list, never published, and never cited.

Three ways in

  • The in-dossier note box. Every dossier carries an "Add a Field Note" box with the company already selected. Type it, attach Word or PDF files, save. The write is immediate — no deploy, no commit, no wait.
  • Ask Claude. profiler note <Company>: <text> captures your words verbatim and asks for your confidence rating.
  • Send a document. Hand Claude a deck or PDF and it extracts the content and writes a faithful summary for the note text; you attach the file itself through the note box.

An existing note can be edited or deleted from the note box's management panel. That is a deliberate asymmetry: you may revise your own notes freely, but Claude never alters note text during a triage pass.

Confidence — the number that does the work

Every note carries a 0–100 rating that you supply. It is never invented, never defaulted, and never inferred from tone. It decides how the note is used everywhere downstream:

RatingTreated asWhat it is allowed to do
75–100Reliable first-hand intelSteers research direction and shapes analysis — the strategy read, prep talking points — stated as your observation.
40–74A leadWorth investigating; must be corroborated against public sources before anything leans on it.
0–39RumorMentioned only with explicit hedging. Never the basis for a conclusion.

Triage — how a note reaches a dossier

Capturing a note never edits a dossier. Promotion is a separate, deliberate decision made during every refresh of that company — or on demand when you say triage notes. Each pending note is weighed on three axes: your confidence rating, materiality (would an analyst reading the dossier want this?), and corroboration (does any public source now support it?). The outcome is recorded on the note itself:

Promoted

Folded into the dossier revision — typically as recent-developments context or a line in the strategy read. Always as unsourced context, never dressed up as public fact.

Logged-only

Kept as recall context with no dossier change. Still searchable, still weighted, still yours — it simply did not clear the bar this cycle.

Either way the response tells you which notes were triaged and why, so promotion is never silent.

Why notes are quarantined from sources

A dossier's credibility rests on the claim that every sourced statement can be traced to a public document. The moment private intel is allowed to blend in unmarked, that claim is false for the whole corpus — and no reader can tell which parts are still safe. Keeping notes structurally separate is what lets the dossier be handed to someone else without caveats.

How Profiler Works02

Staying current without being asked

A research corpus decays. Profiler's answer is not "remember to update it" — it is a set of scheduled jobs that fire on the market's calendar rather than yours, plus a news bridge that keeps every dossier readable against what has happened since it was written.

Refreshes fire on the market's clock

  • One desk, one calendar. A single recurring job runs each weekday against a calendar listing every covered company, its next report date, whether that date is company-confirmed, and what to watch for when it reports.
  • Verify before writing. When a row comes due the session first confirms the report actually published — if the company slipped, the row is re-dated and nothing is written from an announcement that does not exist.
  • The calendar is the queue. Each run takes at most three companies and advances the rows it finished; anything still due is due again tomorrow. When nothing is due the run stands down without a commit.
  • Kept where you can see it. The calendar is a file in the repository, so a date that moves shows up in a diff. It replaced per-company one-shot triggers whose state lived nowhere the repository could check — a chain that ended silently in August 2026.
  • Quarterly sweeps cover the private and unit-level companies with no earnings calendar — Crusoe, Hithium, FlexGen, Rosendin, Turner, Bechtel, Kiewit, Vantage, QTS and the rest — checking for material developments and refreshing only when something actually moved.

The news bridge

Every refresh reads that company's Scraper timeline since the dossier's last update, plus any pending relationship candidates mined from articles naming two covered companies. Trade press is a citable independent source — unlike a field note — so qualifying items are promoted into recent developments with the article itself as the citation. The discipline that keeps this honest:

Verify at the article

The item is checked against the article itself, not the summary that surfaced it, before anything is written.

One outlet is a lead

A single-outlet claim is never promoted as sourced fact. Corroborated and company-confirmed items rank higher.

Nothing is written back

Items that do not qualify are simply left. Scraper's own reconcile pass tracks them; there is no state to keep in sync.

Relationships are curated

An edge candidate becomes a real relationship only when the article supports one — co-mention is not a relationship.

Nothing is ever overwritten

A revision copies the outgoing dossier into the archive and stamps it with the version being superseded before the live file is touched, then records the swap in an archive index. The archive is mirrored off-repo automatically on every merge. The result is a complete, diffable revision trail per company — 193 archived versions and counting — so you can always answer "what did we believe about them in June, and what changed?"

Freshness is shown, not assumed

Every dossier carries its own lastUpdated date, and the roster renders it as a tier: fresh (≤45 days), aging (≤120 days), stale. Reports carry a coverage block pinning each cited company's date and listing the gaps. A stale dossier is never hidden — it announces itself, which is the only way staleness can be acted on.

Using Profiler03

Your workflow

Two loops run side by side — what you do in the app, and what you ask Claude to do to the corpus. Most of the value comes from running both.

In the app

  1. Sign in with GoogleThe whole app sits behind a sign-in wall gated by the Master ACL. Your role decides which surfaces render.
  2. Start at the roster, not the dossierFilter by category, then read the coverage line before you open anything: freshness tier, source count, first-party percentage, whether KPIs are normalized. A stale pin is a signal, not a footnote.
  3. Read the dossier by tabSnapshot to orient, Products for the commercial conversation, Technical Specs for the engineering one, Strategy Read for the angle. Hover any jargon for a concept tooltip.
  4. Check Coverage before a callThe news timeline shows what happened since the dossier was written. This is where a dossier stops being a static document.
  5. Compare before you positionPut the target next to its two closest peers. Normalized KPIs make the comparison honest and the denominators are stated on the page.
  6. Export what you need to carryWord or PDF in the active house style, with a preview so you can check the document before it leaves the building.
  7. Log what you learned, immediatelyOpen the note box while the call is fresh. Type it, attach the deck, rate your confidence honestly — that rating is what makes the note useful six months from now.

Using Profiler03

Getting the most out of it

  • Rate notes honestly, not optimistically. The confidence number is the single input deciding whether a note steers a dossier or gets hedged into a footnote. Inflating it degrades everything downstream — including your own recall of how sure you actually were.
  • Read the freshness pill first. A dossier last updated 140 days ago is not wrong; it is old, and it says so. Ask for a refresh before a meeting that matters rather than trusting a stale pin.
  • Run profiler prep before a first technical meeting. The dossier tells you what they sell. The prep pack teaches you why it matters in their industry, working backwards from the product to the concept chain. Those are different jobs and the prep pack is the one that survives a hard technical question.
  • Reports are snapshots, not dashboards. A published report is immutable by design; when the picture changes a new edition supersedes it. That is what lets you show what you believed, and when.
  • The Sources tab is the audit trail. When a figure is challenged in a meeting, that tab is the answer — and the first-party percentage on the roster tells you at a glance how well-grounded the dossier is before you rely on it.
  • Ask for a batch. Naming several companies at once runs one research pass each and lands them together, which is far cheaper than six separate asks.
