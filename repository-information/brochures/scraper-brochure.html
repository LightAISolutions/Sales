What Scraper Does01

Two editions, built before you wake up

Scraper's job is not to show you the news. It is to decide, defensibly, which twelve of the day's hundred-odd articles are worth your attention — and to show its working when you disagree.

The morning digest

Two editions build daily at 6am desk time and deliver from 7am: Your Morning Digest (BESS), tuned to utility-scale storage, and Your Morning Digest (AIDC), tuned to the data-center power chain. Each edition sorts what survives into three sections:

Companies
Stories naming a company you cover in Profiler — the ones that change a dossier or a conversation.
Market
Structural movement with no single company at the centre: pricing, policy, interconnection, demand.
Incidents
Fires, safety findings, siting opposition and permitting fights — the stories that move public sentiment.

Every item carries a reported summary and, set apart from it, the desk's read — an analytical line kept deliberately shorter than the reporting. The two are colour-keyed and the key is printed in the footer: amber means analysis, and concrete figures are highlighted so a number never hides inside a paragraph.

How Scraper Works02

The roster, and why it is short on purpose

Thirty-five outlets is a deliberate number. A larger roster does not produce a better digest — it produces more noise for the scorer to reject, and more silent failures nobody notices.

Tier 1 — the record and the beat leaders

Seventeen feeds, including five primary federal sources adopted after an executive order naming battery storage and grid inverters as in-scope critical infrastructure reached the desk only through second-hand trade coverage, days late and partial. The roster had been entirely secondary — thirty outlets reporting on government action, none reading it.

Primary sourceWhy it is the record, not a report of it
White House — Presidential ActionsExecutive orders in full. The operative detail is in the definitions section that trade pieces summarise away.
Federal Register — FERCA FERC order takes legal effect on publication here, not on the agency's own newsroom.
Federal Register — IRSCredit and domestic-content guidance — the rules that move project economics.
US Dept. of EnergyProgramme announcements, loan authority, funding awards.
EIA — Today in EnergyThe primary data series behind most market claims you will read elsewhere.

The rest of Tier 1 is the beat leadership — Utility Dive, Data Center Dynamics, Energy-Storage.news, ESS News, Canary Media, POWER, Data Center Knowledge, Data Center Frontier, pv magazine USA, Microgrid Knowledge, T&D World, The Next Platform. Tier 2 adds eighteen outlets of breadth and specialist depth.

No outlet is adopted from reputation

Every feed is probed live before it joins the roster: HTTP status, that the body is actually XML, and that it carries items with recent dates. A feed URL is never taken from memory or from the outlet's standing in the industry. When one is dropped, the reason and the date are recorded so a future session cannot re-propose it as "a gap in coverage" — which is precisely what used to happen.

Blocked is not the same as gone

Two failure modes look identical in a browser and are opposite facts. Scraper distinguishes them and treats them differently in the UI:

Blocked — the outlet is alive

A bot wall answers every automated client. Kept visible in Tune, struck through, toggle frozen off — so you can see the beat is intentionally uncovered rather than quietly missing.

Offline — the publication ended

The domain is parked and the feed is a redirect stub. Filtered out entirely; the record is kept so re-adding the key would reactivate it if it ever returns.

Six outlets currently sit on the do-not-re-propose list, five blocked and one genuinely gone. None of them cost coverage: battery and storage run through Energy-Storage.news and ESS News, solar through pv magazine USA and Solar Power World, data centres through Data Center Frontier, DCD and The Register, and the federal agencies through the Federal Register — where their output is legally effective anyway.

How Scraper Works02

How an article is scored

Every article gets a number out of 100 from four weighted bands, then a geographic multiplier. Below 25 it does not even enter the intake — it is stored for the corpus and never shown.

BandWeightWhat earns it
Company40Names a company you cover in Profiler. Matched on the dossier's permanent slug, not its display name, so a rename never silently detaches a row from its company. Additional companies add a smaller increment each.
Topic25Hits one of 24 topic vocabularies — interconnection, tariffs, offtake, safety, siting, supply chain and the rest.
Substance20Carries concrete detail — figures, capacities, dates, named counterparties — rather than a press-release restatement.
Emphasis15Weighted toward companies you are actively working: a dossier updated within the last 45 days earns the full recency component.

The segment gate — specificity beats breadth

Segments are hierarchical. seg-bess is the broad parent; beneath it sit utility-scale, data-centre/behind-the-meter, residential, C&I and long-duration. Two rules follow from that shape:

  • A child hit demotes its parent. When an article matches a specific child segment, the generic parent's evidence is discounted — a residential product story cannot ride the broad "energy storage" match into a utility-scale edition.
  • Off-segment company news is zeroed, not merely down-ranked. If an article's only segment hits are segments you have turned off, its company, topic and engagement signals are all set to zero. A covered automaker's vehicle recall no longer clears the bar on the strength of the company match when you have "EVs & automotive" switched off.
  • No segment hit at all changes nothing. The gate only fires on positive evidence of an excluded subject — never on absence.

Geography — a multiplier, not a penalty band

The US market is priority one. Related markets — China, Mexico, Chile, Canada — matter mainly when what happened there affects the US. That is applied as a multiplier on the finished score, because an additive penalty would leave a strong company match on a foreign story still clearing the bar:

×1.00 — US or unmarked
The load-bearing default. Most US trade coverage never says "United States" — it says ERCOT, or a county in Texas, or nothing at all.
×0.55 — Related market
A foreign subject in a priority-two country with no US connection named anywhere in the piece.
×0.25 — Elsewhere
Everywhere else. The lead should never be an Australian transmission story.
Why the default is the important part

Penalising articles with no geographic marker would empty the digest, because the overwhelming majority of relevant US coverage carries no such marker. A penalty therefore requires positive evidence of a foreign subject — never merely the absence of evidence of a domestic one. This is the single rule most likely to be broken by a well-meaning "improvement" to the scorer.

How Scraper Works02

The morning build

The build runs in chunked steps rather than one long pass, so a slow feed or a rate-limited model degrades one step instead of failing the edition. Each phase hands its state to the next.

  1. FetchWalk the enabled feeds for the edition's window, parse each one, and drop anything already seen. A feed that fails is skipped and logged — never allowed to abort the run.
  2. Score & gateApply the rubric, the segment gate and the geographic multiplier to every item. Anything below 25 is stored for the corpus and goes no further.
  3. ClusterDetect articles covering the same event and collapse them into one story, keeping the best-ranked outlet's version. Six outlets reporting one deal is one item, not six.
  4. SummarizeGenerate the reported summary and, separately, the desk's analytical read — capped shorter than the reporting on purpose. Gemini or Claude Sonnet, selectable in the UI; failures are classified as soft (retry) or terminal (stop asking) so one bad call cannot burn the whole budget.
  5. BackstopIf the edition is coming in thin, a second pass widens the net rather than shipping three items with no explanation.
  6. Render & deliverBuild the HTML, resolve every link through the intake table so clicks land on the real article, store the edition, and email subscribers from 7am.
  7. ReconcileA daily pass mines articles naming two covered companies into relationship candidates for Profiler, and prunes orphaned intake rows.

The footer tells the truth

Every edition ends with a line like 14 of 15 relevant · 104 scanned · 1 more held back. That is not decoration — it is the contract. Items that cleared the relevance bar but lost to a section cap are counted in that number and readable in the Held back panel.

What happens when an edition is too big to store

A Sheets cell caps at 50,000 characters, so a very large edition has to be trimmed to fit. The trim is ordered: analysis text goes first, then summaries, then the held-back list is halved. Crucially, when the held-back list is trimmed the edition says so rather than reporting a smaller number — because "nothing was held back" would not be a smaller truth, it would be a false one. The total count survives the trim untouched.

Retention

400 editions kept

Roughly six months of three weekday editions, browsable and searchable. Narrow reads mean depth no longer costs page-load time.

240 editions of live links

About sixteen weeks. Beyond that an edition stays readable but its links fall back to opening the app.

Using Scraper03

Your day with Scraper

The daily loop takes five minutes. The tuning loop runs weekly at most. Both feed the same scorer, and the second is what makes the first keep getting better.

The five-minute morning

  1. Open the editionIt was built at 6am and is waiting. Read by section — Companies first if you are prepping a call, Market first if you are thinking about positioning.
  2. Read the summary, then the readThe reported facts and the desk's interpretation are visually separated so you always know which is which. Figures are highlighted; you can scan for numbers alone.
  3. Click through what mattersThis is not passive. Clicks are logged and become an engagement boost for that company and topic, so tomorrow's ranking reflects today's attention.
  4. Note the missesThere is no per-item rating any more — the rubric replaced it. When a story should not have been there, the fix is a segment toggle or a topic weight, not a verdict on the item.
  5. Ask "Why thin?" when it looks lightYou get the real reason — a quiet news day, a failing feed, a segment you turned off — instead of guessing.
  6. Check "Held back"Everything that cleared the bar but lost to a section cap. Worth a glance on days when the top of the edition felt off.

Tuning — the weekly loop

ControlWhen to reach for it
Segment togglesA whole beat is noise right now — residential storage, EVs, consumer gear. Turn the segment off and the gate zeroes those stories even when they name a company you cover.
Topic weightsYou are working a specific problem — interconnection queues, domestic content, fire codes. Weight it up for the duration.
CompaniesSynced from Profiler automatically. Adding a dossier there adds it to the scorer here; the two never drift.
Why thin?The other direction from "Score it": instead of asking why one article ranked where it did, it explains why a whole edition came out light — a quiet day, a dark feed, or tuning you turned too far.

The one trap worth knowing

Segment vocabularies live in the running spreadsheet, not in the source file — so editing the words in code changes nothing until the seed's version marker is bumped and you press Sync now. Rebuilding alone does not sync. If you have customised a segment's terms yourself, it is deliberately never overwritten, which also means it will not receive future vocabulary updates.

Using Scraper03

Getting the most out of it

  • Click through, don't just skim. The engagement boost is built from real click-throughs, so a week of reading headlines in the email teaches the scorer nothing. Opening the three stories you actually care about is the cheapest tuning available.
  • The rubric is the relevance model, not a vote. Per-item 👍/👎 rating and the guided Calibrate pass were retired in August 2026 when the scoring rubric replaced them. Your steering signals are click-throughs, segment toggles and topic weights.
  • Toggle segments before you touch sources. Almost every "this edition is noisy" problem is a segment problem, not a source problem — and a segment toggle is reversible in one click, where retiring an outlet costs you a beat.
  • Trust the unmarked-geography default. If you ever find yourself wanting to penalise articles that do not mention the US, remember that most of the coverage you want does not mention it either.
  • Use "Score it" when a ranking surprises you. Guessing at the rubric wastes more time than checking it. The band-by-band breakdown usually shows a segment you forgot you turned off.
  • Read the footer count on thin days. "104 scanned, 14 relevant" is a quiet news day. "104 scanned, 3 relevant" with a held-back count of zero is usually a feed that has gone dark — check the background-errors panel.
  • Let editions specialise. Rather than one edition tuned to a compromise, run BESS and AIDC separately — and add a third for whatever you are actively pitching. Presets make that a one-click setup.
  • Bump the version when you edit segment terms. This is the single change most likely to look like it worked and silently do nothing.

Where things go wrong, and what the app tells you

SymptomWhat to look at first
Edition is unusually shortWhy thin? — distinguishes a quiet day from a broken feed from an over-tightened tuning profile.
An obviously relevant story is missingScore it on the article's URL. Usually an off segment zeroing the company match, or a geographic multiplier you did not expect.
The same story appears twiceClustering did not recognise them as one event — usually different headlines over the same wire copy. Nothing to do in the edition; it self-corrects as the cluster keys settle.
Summaries look truncatedThe edition hit the storage cap and was trimmed in order. The footer will say so.
A term change did nothingThe seed version was not bumped, or Sync now was not pressed before the rebuild.
A source stopped contributingSource stats and the background-errors panel. It may have been bot-walled rather than gone quiet.

The Corpus Bridge04

Where the news goes after you read it

An article read once and forgotten is worth little. Scraper's second job is to hold everything it scored in a queryable corpus, and hand the right slice to the other two apps when they need it.

What the corpus holds

Every scored article — including the ones that never made an edition — is stored with the Profiler slugs it matched, its summary and analysis, its event type, and the figures captured from it. A daily pass also mines edge candidates: articles naming two covered companies, which may signal a relationship the graph does not yet know about.

Timeline
One company's news since a given date, newest first, deduplicated — what a dossier refresh reads to find what it missed.
Candidates
Co-mentions awaiting a judgment about whether a real relationship actually exists between the two.
Read-only, bounded
The route cannot write and returns at most 200 items — a window onto the corpus, not a control surface.

Who reads it, and how it is protected

  • Profiler dossier refreshes. Every refresh pulls the company's timeline since the dossier's last update plus its pending candidates, verifying each promising item against the article itself before promoting it.
  • Classroom's weekly pipeline. Corpus items are one source the curriculum run reads when deciding what a seller now needs to know.
  • Access is token-gated. The route requires a shared secret held server-side in both scripts, never committed and never quoted back. A session without it proceeds without news context and says so — rather than failing, or inventing coverage.
  • The gate follows the content. Corpus-derived material is contributor-tier wherever it lands — a lesson built from it inherits that gate automatically.
One outlet is a lead, never a fact

Nothing crosses the bridge automatically. A single-outlet claim is a lead requiring corroboration; corroborated and company-confirmed items rank higher; every item is verified against the article itself. And there is no write-back — unqualified items are simply left for the daily reconcile pass. Nothing has to stay in sync, so nothing can fall out of sync.

Where Scraper sits

Profiler — the skeleton
Who everyone is. Supplies the covered-company list driving the 40-point company band.
Scraper — the blood
What just happened. Feeds refreshes, relationship candidates and curriculum material back.
Classroom — the institution
What it means and what to retain. Turns both into taught, gated knowledge.
In one line

Scraper answers "what changed today, does it matter to me, and why did you think so?" — and keeps everything it read, so the answer is still there months later when a dossier needs it.
