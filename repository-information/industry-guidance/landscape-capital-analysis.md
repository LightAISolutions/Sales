## 1. Executive read

**§2 was drafted before any other section**, per §10.6 **(j)**, because this segment has a
registered guidance module sitting squarely on one of its five buying criteria.

Six things carry the module.

**First, the roster inverts session 12's and creates a problem no previous landscape has had.**
`neoclouds` was 1 · 6 · 0 and `who-dominates-and-on-what-basis` had one company to explain.
`capital` is **4 · 1 · 3**, and the four incumbents' own registry `basis` lines describe **four
different instruments** — a landlord-by-proxy that is also a major lender, an operationally
integrated owner of generation and platforms, the exemplar finite-life fund that is more often a
lender than an owner, and sovereign capital. Four bases have to be sorted against each other, and
the honest answer is that they are not competing on one axis at all.

**Second, the graph settles the rivalry question rather than leaving it to judgment.** Thirteen
edges connect the eight, eight of them curated, carrying thirteen typings. Six of those thirteen
read `competitor` — and they are **three pairs typed in both directions**, forming a complete
triangle among the three asset managers. **The fourth incumbent is typed `competitor` with
nobody**, appearing instead as a transaction counterparty on one side and a portfolio owner on the
other, and **the single challenger is typed `competitor` with nobody either**. So the segment
holds exactly one rivalry set of three, and the module says so instead of asserting a four-way
contest the record does not carry.

**Third, the cut between the instruments is stated inside the corpus, by two members, about each
other, in almost the same words.** Blackstone's own strategy section: *"Macquarie, running the
closest comparable platform in Aligned out of finite-life infrastructure funds, exited at eight
years. Same asset class, opposite structural obligation."* Macquarie's own: *"a closed-end fund
must eventually return capital, which is precisely the obligation Blackstone's perpetual vehicles
do not carry over QTS. Same asset class, opposite structural clock."* Two dossiers written
independently reach for the same comparison from opposite ends. **That is the sharpest fact in the
segment and it is the module's spine**: the question that sorts these four is not how big the
money is, it is **when it must leave**.

**Fourth, the adjacents run the opposite way from every previous landscape's.** All three are
ranked — incumbent or challenger — in exactly **one** other segment, `aidc-developers-and-
landlords`, and **all three invert** into `adjacent` here: a 100 % rate on a single source segment.
They are not investors that bought operations; they are **operators that learned to sell capital** —
a landlord running a USD 3.25 bn third-party fund on 20 % of the equity, a broker-dealer that owns a
campus, and the landlord whose capital-markets franchise invented the data-centre securitization in
2018. That is where the threat to the incumbents actually sits, and the single challenger is not it.

**Fifth, the public segment lesson's dash rows are produced by four different mechanisms at once.**
Measured against the parsed `CRITERION_LEXICON` and `READ_NEXT`: one criterion maps to a lesson that
is in `READ_NEXT` **and built**; one maps to a lesson in `READ_NEXT` that is **unbuilt**; two hit **no
lexicon key at all**; one hits a **built lesson that is not in this segment's `READ_NEXT`** and is
filtered out by the generator's own eligibility guard. Sessions 8, 10, 11 and 12 each met one
mechanism; this segment produces all of them in five rows (§3a).

**Sixth, four of the eight buy nothing this corpus sells, and their own files say so.** The
practical consequence for a seller is in §7, and it is the reason this landscape is not a target
list.

itself.** In `bess-bankability-2026-08` it is a **gauntlet with a pass mark**: cycling regime against
warranty limits, retention curves against the pro forma, the LTSA that the warranty depends on. Here
it is a **sorting instrument**: its presence tells you which seat the counterparty is in. The module
never asks who sits in the seat; the landscape never teaches what the report tests.

## 3. Who dominates, and on what basis — four incumbents, four instruments, one clock

### 3a. First, the curriculum measurement — FOUR mechanisms in five rows, and the fourth named

`build-classroom-segments.py` builds `what-is-bought-and-on-what` by intersecting
`CRITERION_LEXICON` with the **segment's own `READ_NEXT`**: `if lid in eligible and any(k in low
for k in keys)`. Run programmatically against the parsed literals on 2026-09-16 — the lexicon was
**not** taken from the brief:

| # | Criterion | Lexicon hit | In `READ_NEXT`? | Built? | Rendered | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contracted cash flow — tenant credit, toll length, resource-adequacy terms | `contract` → `contracts-and-revenue` | **yes** | **yes** | *Who Carries Which Risk* | **A genuine intersection.** The only one of the five |
| 2 | The independent engineer's report and the lender's approved-vendor file | `independent engineer`, `lender` → `what-bankable-means`; and `engine` → `bridge-power` | `what-bankable-means` **yes**; `bridge-power` **no** | **no** | *The Independent Engineer's Report* **(planned)** | **Mapped but UNBUILT** — the candidate fourth mechanism |
| 3 | Securitizability — ABS-grade collateral, standardized buildings, ring-fenced SPVs | **none** | — | — | **—** | **No lexicon key at all** |
| 4 | Platform scale and the exit route (IPO, sale to a strategic, fund-to-fund) | **none** | — | — | **—** | **No lexicon key at all** |
| 5 | Regulatory exposure of owning both energy supply and demand | `regulat` → `how-a-utility-buys` | **no** | yes | **—** | **Disjoint sets** — (cc3)'s keyword artefact |

**So the answer to the brief's question is: "mapped but unbuilt" is a VARIANT OF THE FIRST
mechanism, not a fourth one — and the reason is mechanical.** Rows 1 and 2 are produced by the
*same* code path: both lessons pass the `lid in eligible` guard and both reach `lesson_ref()`.
`lesson_ref()` then returns the registered title for row 1 and `PLANNED_TITLES[…] + " (planned)"`
for row 2. **The intersection succeeded in both cases**; only the registry lookup differs. A dash
row is what the *reader* sees when the intersection fails, and rows 1 and 2 are not dash rows at
all — they are the same mechanism reporting two different curriculum states. Sessions 8, 10, 11 and
12 were all diagnosing **dashes**; this row is a populated cell.

**The three genuine dash rows here are produced by two mechanisms, and the module says which:**

- **Rows 3 and 4 hit no key in the 27-entry lexicon at all.** Their vocabulary — securitizability,
  ABS-grade collateral, ring-fenced SPVs, the exit route — appears nowhere in the curriculum's
  keyword map because **no lesson in the plan teaches it**, built or unbuilt. That is session 8's
  **(y3)**: a real curriculum gap, and the module declines to fill it.
- **Row 5 is session 12's (cc3) disjoint-set artefact.** `how-a-utility-buys` is built, public, and
  teaches the regulatory machinery — but it is not in this segment's `READ_NEXT`, so the generator's
  eligibility guard filters it before the keyword ever matters. **The reader sees a dash against a
  criterion a built lesson substantially covers.** The module says so.

**And the row-2 false positive never reaches the page**, which is worth recording because it is the
guard working. The lexicon key `engine` matches the word *engineer* and maps to `bridge-power` — a
bridge-generation lesson mapped against an independent engineer's report. `bridge-power` is not in
this segment's `READ_NEXT`, so `lid in eligible` drops it. **The generator's own comment says this
is the point**: *"a bare keyword hit across the whole curriculum is noise."* Session 12 recorded a
false positive that *did* surface; this one is caught.

**Net: 1 of 5 criteria has a built owner, 2 of 5 are mapped, and 3 of 5 render as a dash for two
different reasons.** Against session 8's two-of-six unowned, session 10's five-of-six and session
11's six-of-six, this segment sits in the middle — and is the first where the diagnosis differs
**row by row** rather than segment-wide.

### 3b. Four incumbents, four instruments — and the cut the corpus makes about itself

There is no ranking to make here and the module does not manufacture one. **No published source in
this corpus ranks these four against each other on anything this segment is about**, and one of them
says so in its own file: *"no publisher ranks data-centre owners by capacity or value"*, and its own
four scale figures *"measure four different things and do not crosswalk."* Where a ranking does
exist it contradicts the obvious reading — Infrastructure Investor's 2026 edition puts the largest
alternatives manager **eighth** in five-year infrastructure fundraising at USD 45.7 bn, behind
BlackRock at USD 113.4 bn and behind Macquarie Asset Management at USD 87.9 bn, **while IPE Real
Assets' table for the prior year puts Macquarie first by assets under management.** Stock and flow
disagree, and a size ranking would therefore be a choice of publisher.

**What can be sorted is the instrument, and the corpus sorts it itself.** The question is not how
much money each holds; it is **when the money must leave**:

| Incumbent | The instrument | When the money must leave | The evidence, from its own file |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blackstone** | Perpetual capital, plus a credit book that lends to assets it does not own | **Never.** It is not obliged to sell | USD 555.6 bn of perpetual-capital AUM at 30 June 2026, USD 463.0 bn fee-earning — **48 % of all fee-earning AUM**. QTS held five years and the BREIT position **rose** from 33 % to 35.4 % |
| **Brookfield** | Operational integration — generation, transmission-adjacent assets and data-centre platforms in one franchise | **At fund life, but the franchise persists** — a fee-and-carry entity with a deliberately small balance sheet | Fee-bearing capital **USD 672 bn** at 30 June 2026, +19 % y/y, against total assets of **USD 20,080 m**. The parent retains effectively **100 %** of carried interest in mature funds and 33.3 % on newer ones |
| **Macquarie** | The finite-life infrastructure fund; and, far more often, senior debt | **At fund life, and it has been demonstrated** | Aligned: entered April 2018 at **85 MW across two facilities**, exited 21 July 2026 at **51 campuses and 6.4 GW**, about **USD 40 bn enterprise value**. Its live corpus appearances are a GPU-secured facility, a senior loan, preferred equity and a tax-equity partnership |
| **MGX** | Sovereign capital, and access rather than analysis | **Undisclosed — and so is everything else** | Fund I closed **1 July 2026 at USD 49 bn** against a USD 45 bn target; a Stargate initial equity funder; AIP co-founder; owner in Aligned and Vantage. **No AUM total, no ticket sizes, no stake percentages, no headcount** |

**The cross-attestation is what makes this a finding rather than a framing.** Two of the four
reach for the *same* comparison about each other, independently, in almost the same words —
Blackstone's *"Same asset class, opposite structural obligation"* against Macquarie's *"Same asset
class, opposite structural clock."* **Neither dossier cites the other; both were written 2026-09-06.**
S2 has met shared bases before (session 12's third-party rating) and shared subjects, but not two
members' analysts converging on one axis unprompted. **The axis is the clock, and it is the
segment's real structure.**

**And the graph confirms the cut by refusing to extend it to the fourth.** Six of thirteen curated
typings among the eight read `competitor`, and they are three pairs in both directions:
Blackstone↔Brookfield (*"Direct peers in infrastructure and digital infrastructure, and
counterparties on the pending Aypa Power sale"*), Blackstone↔Macquarie (*"counterparties on the 2024
AirTrunk sale — Blackstone bought the platform Macquarie was exiting"*) and Brookfield↔Macquarie
(*"Peers in infrastructure, and the two rankings that measure them disagree about which is larger"*).
**MGX is typed `competitor` with none of them.** Its typed edges are `other/historical` to Macquarie
(*"a transaction counterparty, not an ongoing relationship"* — it bought Aligned from Macquarie's
funds) and `portfolio/active` to Vantage. Its edges to Blackstone and Brookfield are **derived-only,
with no curated typing at all.**

**So the module's own judgment, labelled as analysis: this is three competitors and a counterparty,
not four rivals.** The three asset managers compete for the same assets on different clocks — and
two of those three competitor edges are *the same transaction seen from both sides*, which is what
competition in this segment actually looks like: one of them buying the platform another is
obliged to sell. Sovereign capital is not in that contest. It is the party the exits are sold **to**,
and it has no stated clock at all.

## 4. Who threatens — one challenger, and a bench that is three operators selling capital

**The challenger bench is one company, and the size gap inside it is the segment's own sharpest number.**
Excelsior Energy Capital runs **USD 1,505,418,687 of regulatory AUM** across two pooled vehicles
with 25 employees, against an incumbent holding **USD 1,346.3 bn** of total AUM. That is roughly
**one to nine hundred**. Session 8's **(y4)** showed a one-challenger threat section is writable when
the registry's `notes` explains the shape; **this registry entry has no `notes` at all**, so the
shape had to be measured — and the measurement says the challenger is not the threat.

**What the challenger actually is:** the only member of the eight holding the **same** role in

replaced tax equity, and a loan to a platform a different member of this very segment owns. **The
seat decides who signs, what gets diligenced and how long it takes**, and the tell is usually which
operating group or fund the source names.
