## 1. Executive read

**Four incumbents hold this segment on four different bases, and only one of the four is a volume lead in the thing the segment makes.** CATL is first in energy-storage batteries for five consecutive years, with ESS battery sales of 93 GWh in 2024 and 121 GWh in 2025 — and its own dossier records the lead narrowing, company-reported share falling to 30.4% in 2025 from 36.5%, with Benchmark estimating roughly 20% and Hithium and EVE at about 12% each. Hithium's basis is specialisation: stationary-only, Top 2 in both total and utility-scale ESS cell shipments for 2025, cumulative shipments past 100 GWh. BYD's basis is a platform amortised across another industry — a Blade LFP line shared with its vehicle business across planned capacity above 500 GWh — and in **cells specifically it is consistently fourth**, at 35.7 GWh and 7.7% on SNE and 6.7% on Benchmark. LG Energy Solution's basis is not a shipment rank at all: it is the provenance of the chain, five North American sites targeting roughly 50 GWh of regional cell output by the end of 2026, a 140 GWh order backlog and first-half 2026 ESS shipments up 357% with about 86% going to North America.

**That matters because the registry's basis line for BYD reads "top-tier in every storage ranking", and the rankings it is top-tier in are mostly rankings of a different segment.** Benchmark ranked BYD the number-one global BESS *system integrator* for 2025 at roughly 60 GWh and 13% share. In cells, the product this segment is defined by, BYD's own dossier says fourth and says it plainly. A seller who reads the player table and hears "number one" has crossed a segment boundary the table does not draw.

**Eleven challengers are contesting the segment and none of them has taken it, and the reason is the fourth buying criterion.** The registry lists six criteria; the fourth is *prohibited-foreign-entity status and the factory's location — status follows ownership, not site*. That criterion is a wall a challenger cannot climb by building, and Gotion is the proof: it is the only Chinese cell maker in this corpus with an operating US plant — five lines at Manteno, Illinois, on more than USD 536m of pledged state incentives — and it is named in NDAA §154(b), so its US subsidiaries are captured with it and **the plant it actually runs cannot claim the advanced manufacturing production credit for what it makes there**. Capacity does not buy access in this segment; ownership does. Envision's Fixx restructuring is the only executed workaround on the record, and whether it survives Treasury's final rules is the largest open question in the file.

**So the challengers' share moves while their standing does not, and the economics explain why.** Cornex bought seventh place with two 70-to-80 GWh plants commissioned ten months after groundbreaking and tender bids of RMB 0.27–0.28 per watt-hour at or below industry cash cost, funded by a structure with no audited accounts of any kind. CALB's storage build is funded by a traction business that is larger and better ranked than the storage one, and its gross margin did not move at all in the first half of 2026 — 16.71% against 16.69% — on another year of 60% revenue growth. Gotion's headline profit is 92.3% non-recurring, with fair-value changes at 72.35% of total profit. Sunwoda's EV and storage cell gross margin is under 5% with core ex-non-recurring profit near zero. Great Power's margin thesis is channel mix rather than cost leadership, overseas gross margin running at roughly double domestic in every disclosed period. **In most of these companies the money is not in the cell**, which is exactly why eleven of them can grow shipments without changing who the segment belongs to.

**And the one genuinely different bet in the segment is not on the ladder at all.** Form Energy sells a hundred-hour iron-air asset against a still, overcast week rather than an evening peak, and because the chemistry uses no lithium, cobalt, nickel or graphite, the foreign-entity material-assistance tests, the Section 301 and AD/CVD actions and the cell-sourcing questions that dominate every other dossier here **simply do not reach it**. Its two largest announced counterparties are AI data-centre buyers. Its exposure is a different kind entirely: federal demonstration funding and state cost-recovery decisions.

## 3. Who dominates, and on what basis

**Four incumbents, four different bases.** In the integrator segment the dominance question is *which ranking house do you believe*. Here it is a different question, because the four incumbents are not competing for the same crown.

1. **CATL — the volume lead, and it is narrowing.** First in energy-storage batteries for five consecutive years; ESS battery sales 93 GWh (2024) → 121 GWh (2025); sole BESS supplier of 19 GWh of TENER into Masdar's USD 6bn, 5.2 GW round-the-clock project, which the dossier calls the largest single BESS order to date. Against that, the company's own reported ESS share fell to 30.4% in 2025 from 36.5%, Benchmark estimates roughly 20%, and Hithium and EVE are at about 12% each on third-party estimates. **The dossier's own first judgment is that the lead is narrowing** — which is an unusual thing for a segment's incumbency claim to rest on and is the most useful sentence in the file.
2. **Hithium — specialisation.** Fifth globally in 2023, third in 2024 at 35.1 GWh and roughly 11%, Top 2 in both total and utility-scale ESS cell shipments for 2025 on InfoLink, SMM and ICC; cumulative shipments past 100 GWh in August 2025. Stationary-only, which no other incumbent here is: CATL, BYD and LG Energy Solution all fund and amortise cell capacity across a vehicle business.
3. **BYD — a platform priced by another industry, and a fourth place in this one.** The cost position comes from vertical integration and a Blade LFP platform shared with the vehicle business across planned capacity above 500 GWh. In cells it is consistently fourth (SNE 35.7 GWh and 7.7%; Benchmark 6.7%), and even there SNE's volume and Benchmark's implied volume differ by about 3 GWh. The "top-tier in every storage ranking" in its basis line is mostly a *systems* ranking: Benchmark put BYD first among BESS integrators for 2025 at roughly 60 GWh and 13%, while Wood Mackenzie and InfoLink put Tesla first and BYD third for the same year.
4. **LG Energy Solution — the passport, not the position.** The dossier's own verdict on where the advantage comes from is blunt: **regulatory, not technical.** Five North American ESS sites targeting roughly 50 GWh of regional cell output by end-2026 (global above 60 GWh, more than 80% North American), a 140 GWh backlog at end-2025, first-half 2026 ESS shipments up 357% year on year with about 86% going to North America, and the Tesla Megapack 3 contract plus a DTE, Qcells and Excelsior pipeline as direct evidence. It appears in no cell-shipment league table in this corpus.

**The format ladder is the segment's visible technology axis and it does not separate the incumbents from the challengers.** Read the Ah class across the members and the ladder runs: 280 and 314 Ah (CATL's EnerOne cabinets, Narada's self-made cell, the class everything is measured against) → 392 and 472 Ah (REPT's Wending, Cornex) → the 587-to-684 band where nearly everyone now sits (CATL 587, Great Power 587/588, CALB 588 and 684, Cornex 588, Sunwoda 625 and 684, EVE 628, REPT 648, Envision 750-plus) → Hithium's kAh class at 1,175 and 1,300 Ah → and BYD's long Blade at 2,710 Ah, which is a different construction rather than a higher rung. EVE was first to mass-produce the 600 Ah-plus class and its own dossier claims the system consequence: roughly half the components and half the installation workload against a 314 Ah build. Sunwoda claims 27% fewer containers and about 8% lower levelised cost on 684 Ah. **Six members sit within a hundred amp-hours of each other**, which means the ladder is a race everybody is running — a threat to price, not a source of standing.

**Where the segment does separate is on criterion 4, and it separates it into two groups that do not overlap.** *Prohibited-foreign-entity status and the factory's location — status follows ownership, not site.* On one side: LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI, the only two members whose chain qualifies, and Form Energy, whose chemistry puts it outside the test entirely. On the other: every Chinese member, whatever it builds and wherever it builds it. Gotion is the sharpest case in the corpus — an operating US plant it cannot claim the production credit for — and Envision's Fixx structure is the only executed conversion of a named Chinese maker's US asset into tolerable supply.

## 4. Who threatens, and on which route

**Route 1 — the format ladder, on price and without standing.** Six challengers are on the 587-to-684 rung and one incumbent is a full class above it. EVE forced the transition and is first at each rung; Sunwoda produced a million 684 Ah cells in under three months; CALB, Cornex and Great Power arrived at 588 within about a year of CATL and EVE. Cornex's own dossier says the quiet part: its 588 Ah cell *follows* CATL's and EVE's formats by about a year, and speed of build is the real product. This route takes price, and the record says it is taking it — a Chinese storage-cell price war with tender bids at or below cash cost — but it has produced no change in who the segment's incumbents are.

**Route 2 — the ownership passport, and it has exactly two runners.** Samsung SDI is the only challenger attacking on the same axis LG Energy Solution is defending: high-power NCA and LMO for data-centre UPS and BBU guided to grow more than 70% in 2026, a world-first pass of a UL indoor large-scale fire test for UPS batteries, 1.6 GWh of first-half 2026 ESS shipments to AI data centres, and US LFP cells from the StarPlus Indiana joint venture from October 2026. **Both runners are Korean, and no Chinese challenger can enter this route at all** — which is the structural fact that decides the segment's US half.

**Route 3 — chemistry outside the lithium chain.** Form Energy is the only member selling against a multi-day outage rather than an evening peak, at roughly 0.15 kW per 15 kWh cell and a design duty cycle of eight to thirteen discharges a year — deliberately optimised against the metrics lithium wins on, with independent estimates putting iron-air round-trip efficiency at 35–50% against 85–90% for lithium. CATL's sodium line (Naxtra, TENER Sodium) is the incumbent's version of the same hedge; Great Power reached sodium mass production in 2023 ahead of most of the corpus but discloses no volume, no revenue line and appears in no third-party sodium ranking; Narada's differentiation is semi-solid. **This route is the only one on which a challenger holds something an incumbent cannot copy quickly**, and it is also the least commercially proven.

**Route 4 — the adjacency into the AI campus, where the incumbent is not in this segment.** Gotion claims above 28% global share and first place in base-station and data-centre UPS backup batteries **in its audited annual report** rather than only in marketing — a higher bar than most sector positioning clears — while no independent house publishes such a category at all and no named data-centre operator appears anywhere. Narada is second in CNESA's 2024 base-station and data-centre backup shipments with a customer list spanning China Tower, China Mobile, China Telecom, GDS, ByteDance and Alibaba. Great Power and REPT each ship an 85 Ah data-centre backup cell. Hithium launched an AIDC line and its own dossier calls it a product-marketing position rather than a business: two launches, four SKUs, and zero named customers, pilots or UPS-integrator partnerships eight months later. **The reference incumbent of this niche — Panasonic, claiming roughly 80% of the data-centre BBU market — sits at `adjacent` in this segment**, because backup is not what the segment is defined by. The honest reading for a seller: five members market an AIDC cell line and not one of them names a counterparty.

**The counter-current: one challenger is in creditor reorganisation, and one is retreating from the growth.** Narada carries a creditor reorganisation petition, a going-concern-qualified audit, an adverse internal-controls opinion, an ST designation, 163 frozen accounts and roughly 80–92% leverage — its dossier's verdict is that this breaks the bankability a ten-to-twenty-year warranty requires, whatever the product does. CALB is structurally weakest where the market is actually growing: overseas revenue was 2.1% of FY2025 and 4.4% of the first half of 2026, US revenue was RMB 1.5m, and InfoLink reports non-China shipments were 53.2% of global first-half 2026 volume — a majority for the first time.

## 7. What it means for the active engagement — the seller's play

**The storage seller — three moves.** *First*, read the fourth criterion before the first three. In this segment the ownership test decides which conversations are possible at all, and it follows the owner rather than the plant: an operating US factory does not change a named parent's status, and the corpus carries a worked example of exactly that. Qualify on the credit before you qualify on the cell. *Second*, when a competitor quotes a cell rank, ask which house and which cut — the same company sits third on one table and seventh on another for the same half, and one member's "fourth globally" claim cites no research house at all and is only supportable on a utility-scale-only sub-metric. *Third*, know which rung of the format ladder the counterparty has standardised on, because six suppliers are within a hundred amp-hours of each other and the differentiation that survives at that rung is delivery slot, yield record and fire history — criteria 5 and 6, not criterion 2.

**The AI-data-centre power seller — two moves.** *First*, the backup cell and the grid cell are different products from the same segment, and the incumbent of the backup niche is placed `adjacent` here precisely because backup is not what the segment is defined by. ## 10. Freshness gate — the `reviewBy` judgment, resolved

Envision's "late 2026" is load-bearing — it is the largest open question in the file and the first indicator row — but **it is not a date**, and §10.6 asks for a dated gate.
2. **1 January 2027 is a gate the module carries, on the segment's own members, dated to the day.** It is a *rate that changes on a schedule* in the literal words of step 10. It reaches every Chinese exporter in the segment — eleven of the fifteen players in the bets table — and it is priced into every 2026 framework two of them have signed. One member's own indicator list names it as the test of its margin thesis; another's fifth judgment prices its whole export book against it. It is also the date on which a share of the sector's reported 2026 shipment growth is expected to reverse, which means the ranking tables the dominance section rests on will read differently after it.
3. **Taking it buys a genuinely earlier review, not a cosmetic one.** `updated` + 6 months is **2027-03-14**. 4. **The partial dates resolve to the first of the month and the resolution is checked rather than assumed.** `2027-02` → 2027-02-01 and `2027-10` → 2027-10-01. The §154 group is the check: two members state it as `2027-10` and four state it as `2027-10-01`, so first-of-month is the corpus's own convention for this regime rather than a convenience of this session's.

**The divergence from the segment lesson is expected and correct, and it is wider here than it was at session 1.** `build-classroom-segments.py`'s `review_by()` takes the earliest future `effectiveDate` matching a full `YYYY-MM-DD` (`DATE_RE.match(eff)`), so it is blind twice over: to the two partial `2027-10` entries, and to every gate stated inside an `exposure` paragraph. A hand-authored module may read the paragraph and say why; a generator that guessed at prose would be inventing precision on every run. **Two rules, two artefacts, two dates** — §10.3 for the lesson, §10.6 for the module — and the gap is the honest output of both.
