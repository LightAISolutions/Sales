The landscape module for `insurance-and-risk-transfer`, the nineteenth and last segment of the value chain: the brokers, insurers and risk-analytics firms that price and carry the risks a financing cannot hold, and whose underwriting decides how a fire record prices into the next project.

That inversion is not a curiosity. It disables two of the standing instruments this lane has built up over seventeen modules, and the honest result is to say so and measure the property that disabled them, rather than to dress an empty reading as a superlative. §2 does that first, before any section that ranks anybody.

## 1. Executive read

**This segment has three members and no bench, and that shape is the subject rather than a footnote.** Two brokers and one managing general agent. Nobody else in the covered corpus sells into it, and none of the three sells anywhere else — the registry types all 314 memberships across nineteen segments, and these three companies hold exactly one apiece.

**The two incumbents are not equals, and the ranking inverts on the two measures this corpus exists to track.** Marsh McLennan is first among global brokers on 2025 total revenue for a sixteenth consecutive year at USD 26.98bn, against Aon at USD 17.20bn — a gap of about USD 9.8bn. On AI data-centre capacity and on organic growth, Aon leads: its Data Center Lifecycle Insurance Program went from USD 1.5bn to USD 5bn in twelve months, in four steps, against Marsh's Nimbus at USD 2.7bn, and FY2025 organic growth was 6 per cent against 4. **So "who dominates" has two correct answers depending on which market is being asked about, and the module says both.**

**The challenger's role label is expiring on a published clock.** kWh Analytics agreed on 10 March 2026 to be acquired by Beazley, which is itself being acquired by Zurich, with completion expected before the end of 2026. The registry's own `notes` say in as many words that `challenger` "may be the wrong label by the end of 2026". That sentence is what sets this module's `reviewBy`.

**And the finding that spans all three members belongs to this segment rather than to any one dossier: neither incumbent broker anchors its battery-storage guidance to a named standard.** Across seven Marsh documents on four company web properties spanning 2021 to 2026, and across every Aon battery and renewables publication reviewed, no UL 9540A, NFPA 855, DNV, CSA, TÜV, IEC, IEEE or FM Global reference appears. ## 2. Three instruments, two of them empty — written before any other section

§10.6's lane has accumulated three standing instruments for reading a roster: the **role-inversion** instrument of **(aa2)**/**(ee3)**, which asks whether a member ranked one way here ranks differently elsewhere; the **terminus** instrument of **(ii4)**, which asks which direction a roster's external memberships point; and the **graph density** instrument of **(ii3)**, which asks how many of the possible member pairs the corpus connects.

On this segment the first two have **no input at all**. The third fires **complete**. Writing that down is this session's first job, because **(kk3)** established the rule: an instrument that returns the same answer from any position is measuring the position, not the segment — **check the denominator before publishing the result, and where the denominator is zero, publish the absence rather than the reading.**

### 2a. The role-inversion instrument has an empty input set

The instrument needs a member that belongs to two segments. Measured across all nineteen segments and all 314 memberships: **`kwh-analytics`, `marsh-mclennan` and `aon` hold zero external memberships between them.** Not one appears anywhere else in the taxonomy.

Session 17 found `habitat-energy` notable as the only company in the corpus whose entire identity was a single segment. **Here every member is that case.** The instrument cannot be run, there is nothing to invert, and no statement about this segment's relative standing can be derived from it. That is the finding — not a caveat to one.

The property that disables it is worth naming, because it is the same property that makes the segment small: **risk transfer is not a business anybody in this corpus arrives at from somewhere else.** **Sixteen of the other eighteen segments carry at least one adjacent member** — the bench, in this lane's vocabulary — and only `neoclouds` and `hyperscalers-and-ai-labs` join this one at zero. A broker does not sell batteries, and a battery company does not bind paper.

## 3. Who dominates, and on what basis

### 3a. Two incumbents, ranked by two named third parties, for fifteen and sixteen consecutive years

This is one of the few segments in the taxonomy whose incumbency rests on an **external league table rather than on a dossier's judgment**. AM Best ranks Marsh McLennan first among global brokers on 2025 total revenue at **USD 26.98bn**, for a sixteenth consecutive year, and Aon second at **USD 17.20bn** — for more than fifteen. Business Insurance's 2026 Broker Profiles ranks them first and second both globally and among the largest brokers of US business. Marsh's own dossier notes the gap is "more than the third, fourth and fifth brokers combined".

**And the measure is contested in the record itself, by the company it favours less.** Aon's file states that AM Best's total bundles Marsh McLennan's consulting businesses, which are not broking, and that AM Best's own 2018 table showed Aon larger on a broking-only basis. No source published since offers a clean broking-only comparison of the two. **A ranking that has held for sixteen years, on a measure one party disputes and nobody has restated for eight, is worth quoting with its basis attached** — which is why the module's first table column is "Ranked on what".

### 3c. Where dominance stops — and it stops at batteries, for both of them

**Neither incumbent's battery-storage credentials survive an independent check, and both dossiers say so in their own collection-gap lines.**

- **Marsh McLennan:** "no independent source located in this research names this company as a protagonist in any battery-storage placement." Its BESS credentials — a USD 200m Asia facility, 3.8 GW of UK grid-scale storage placed, a 60-strong London power and renewables team — appear **only on its own website**. The one exception is the two Broker Council readouts published by the challenger.
- **Aon:** one documented placement — Pulse Clean Energy's Plymouth and Dowlais projects, 7 July 2026, backed by Ariel Green's Technology Performance Insurance for up to thirteen years, described as the first such policies placed for UK energy storage. Against that, the only megawatt-scale battery figure it publishes, "+150MW BESS projects insured", comes from an **Australia and Pacific brochure coded June 2018** that also claims "more than 72,000 colleagues" against about 60,000 today.

**So the segment's two incumbents are incumbents of data-centre risk on independent evidence and of battery risk on their own say-so.** A module that reported the first and not the second would be reporting half of what it measured.

### 3d. The standard-naming gap, and why it is a group finding rather than a company one

Marsh's battery page says separation "between enclosures" and independent grid routing; Aon's says "three metres or more of spacing". **Neither names the document that sets those numbers.** Seven Marsh documents across four web properties, 2021 to 2026, contain no UL, UL 9540A, NFPA 855, DNV, CSA, TÜV, IEC, IEEE or FM Global reference. Every Aon battery and renewables publication reviewed does the same.

**Both dossiers independently conclude that this is a property of broker-authored guidance rather than an oversight by one firm** — and they reach that conclusion about each other, at high confidence, without either citing the other. That is a cross-attestation, and it is the reason this belongs in the landscape rather than in either company's file: it is a statement about the **role**, not about a company.

**What it means for a seller is concrete.** The broker will tell you what the insurer requires in engineering language and will not tell you which test method or code the requirement descends from. The party that names the standard is two places upstream in the chain, in `assurance` at position 17, and the graph says all three of these companies already talk to its incumbent.

## 4. Who threatens — one challenger, and its label is expiring

### 4a. The bench is empty, so "threat" means one company and one mechanism

**Zero adjacent members.** There is no bench to proportion a threat section to, and **(l)**'s rule — read the registry's `notes` before sizing a threat section — cuts the other way here from how it cut at `utilities`: there the `challenger` role was a structural placeholder and the real threat came from the adjacents; **here there are no adjacents at all**, and the single challenger is a real one with a real mechanism.

The mechanism is **delegated authority**. kWh Analytics is a managing general agent: it holds no balance sheet, binds paper on carriers' behalf under full delegated authority from Aspen Specialty, and prices renewable-asset risk on its own loss and performance database. Aspen, Swiss Re, Everest and Munich Re carry every risk it writes. Its own file assesses at high confidence that **the data is the asset**, and that its authority has expanded on the strength of that database — USD 75 million per renewable-energy project location in 2024, **USD 100 million from the February 2026 renewal**, with a USD 20 million excess natural-catastrophe layer added 28 August 2025.

**What it threatens is not the brokers' revenue. It is the brokers' monopoly on the judgment.** A broker markets a submission to carriers who price it. An MGA prices it itself. Both incumbents' files say the same thing about why that makes them partners rather than rivals: neither operates a renewables or storage managing general agency of its own, so the MGA is a product they distribute. Marsh and its subsidiary McGriff hold **two of the eight seats** on kWh Analytics' Renewable Energy Broker Council in both published years — a quarter of the 2026 council — and **neither readout discloses the common ownership**. Aon holds one seat, represented by the same person in both years, the only firm on the council with that continuity.

### 4b. The instruments, and the one that does not exist

| Instrument | What it does | Where the risk sits |
|---|---|---|
| Property, primary layer | All-risk physical damage for utility-scale solar, BESS and wind in the continental US | Aspen Specialty paper, up to USD 100m per location |
| Excess natural catastrophe | Severe convective storm and named windstorm above the primary | Up to USD 20m per location, A-rated carriers |
| Solar Revenue Put / SRP+ | Guarantees up to 95 per cent of a plant's P50 output for 10–25 years (30 under SRP+), so lenders size more debt | Carrier paper; company reports "average debt sizing increases of 10%" |

**The absence is the important row.** There is no storage revenue, availability or degradation product. The battery-storage sector page offers property cover and data services only. **The put idea has been applied to solar and, parametrically, to wind — never to storage**, and the company's own post-Moss Landing writing is about property rates and thermal-runaway underwriting rather than availability. A seller who reads "revenue put" and hears "somebody will insure my battery's earnings" has misread the segment. Nobody in it will.

### 4c. The label is expiring, and the registry says so before this module does

On **10 March 2026** kWh Analytics agreed to be acquired by **Beazley plc**, which manages six Lloyd's syndicates and stated the firm "will be integrated into Beazley's MAP Risks team". Beazley is itself completing a £8.1bn all-cash takeover by **Zurich Insurance Group** — shareholder-approved April 2026, EC-cleared 7 July 2026, **completion expected before the end of 2026**.

**Two consequences the module states and does not resolve, because the record does not.**

1. **The independence that makes it a challenger is ending.** Its own file assesses at high confidence that the acquisition ends its independence as an MGA and puts the Aspen-led capacity structure in question — a Lloyd's-managing insurer has no reason to keep a competitor's paper behind its own MGA once it can write the risk itself. **Neither party has said whether Aspen stays.**
2. **The completion is undocumented.** The company's own disclaimers page calls it "a wholly owned subsidiary of Beazley plc" and Beazley's H1 results refer to "the addition of kWh Analytics in March"; against that, trade press reported closing in the second half of 2026 subject to regulatory approvals, and **no completion release exists**. The dossier treats the ownership as a subsidiary on the company's own word, at moderate confidence, and records the closing date, price and any retention terms as unpublished.

**So the registry's `notes` are right that `challenger` may be the wrong label by the end of 2026 — and the module's job is to say what the label would become, not to guess when.** If Aspen's paper stays and the brand survives, the role holds. If the book moves onto Beazley syndicates, this segment's only non-broker becomes a division of a carrier that Zurich owns, and the segment's roster is two brokers and nothing else.

## 7. The seller's play — §10.10's two paths

### 7a. If you sell BESS

**Your insurance conversation is with the buyer's broker, and the broker is not the underwriter.** Three practical consequences.

1. **The requirement you are handed will be in engineering language and will not cite a code.** "Separation between enclosures", "independent grid routing", "three metres or more of spacing" — these are what the brokers publish. Ask which test method and which edition the requirement descends from, and expect the broker not to know. The party that owns that answer is a certification body, not a broker.
2. **The underwriting question is thermal runaway, and the technology sort is indoor legacy NMC against modern outdoor LFP.** The segment's only underwriter says so directly: most underwriters are hesitant to insure indoor installations, technology-risk rates have been stable at roughly **30–40 cents per USD 100 of insured value**, and Moss Landing was "an impactful but not defining loss for the industry". A submission arrives as a **thermal runaway risk evaluation form**, with hazard-mitigation analyses and emergency-response plans requested.
3. **Do not promise a revenue product that does not exist.** There is no storage revenue put, no availability cover and no degradation cover anywhere in this segment. Solar has a revenue put; wind has a parametric hedge; storage has property cover and data services. If a customer's model assumes an insurable revenue floor on a battery, that assumption fails in this segment and has to be met in the contract instead.

### 7b. If you sell AIDC power

**The two incumbents are largest on different phases, and the phase decides who you are talking to.** Aon's tower runs the whole lifecycle and is priced around construction — at USD 5bn it carries construction all risks, delay-in-start-up, property damage and business interruption, third-party liability of USD 200m outside the United States and USD 100m inside it, cyber and technology errors and omissions at USD 400m, project cargo at USD 500m. Marsh's Stratus covers the operational phase alone — up to USD 10bn on a single placement across thirty traditional and alternative capital providers.

**The structural feature to sell against is the one Aon names explicitly: embedded commissioning cover with automatic delay-in-start-up sum reduction as phases energise.** A phased campus that energises in blocks reduces its own DSU exposure as it goes, automatically, inside the policy. **Your equipment's commissioning window is therefore inside somebody's insurance schedule** — a slipped energisation date is not only a construction problem, it is a coverage-period problem, and the broker is the party who will notice.
