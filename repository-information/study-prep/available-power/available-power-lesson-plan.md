# Available Power — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the distributed play** — why a Texas battery is sized at 9.9 MW, what a distributed generation resource is and who studies its interconnection, how a small battery is sited on leased commercial land, how a developer sells it at notice-to-proceed with a turnkey EPC attached so a financial buyer can own it without building, how the pre-COD project marketplace sets the price, and where the model fails — including how to read a company whose record has gone silent.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Spearmint plan** teaches the development stage ladder, financing a merchant battery and the pre-COD market at transmission scale; the **GridStor plan** teaches the buyer of late-stage projects — the other side of Available Power's table; the **Invenergy** and **Aypa plans** teach the large developers. The **Classroom** owns responsive reserve, fast frequency response and the hour-by-hour revenue of an ERCOT battery. None of that is repeated.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** Interconnection is a queue with a fast lane and the fast lane has a size limit, so a developer that designs to the limit trades megawatts per site for months per project. A develop-and-sell company is only as real as its buyers, and none of Available Power's has ever been named. And a silent record can be read: newsroom, ISO trackers, counterparties, people — four checks that here all return nothing.

**One boundary, held throughout.** The premise this session was given — a CAISO/ERCOT merchant storage specialist — was wrong on geography (Texas only), model (develop-and-sell at NTP, not owner-operator) and sponsor (none named), and it missed the parent's 2025 rebrand to Available Infrastructure, an edge-AI and cybersecurity company. Every figure here is from a company or partner release (EIN Presswire, PR Newswire, Stem, Linxon, CleanCapital) or two Energy-Storage.news interviews; the corporate storage pages no longer exist and the Wayback Machine was unreachable.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 with the April 2023 Energy-Storage.news interview open (~30 min). Module 2 with the Greenport and Linxon releases open (~25 min). Module 3 with the March 2024 interview open (~30 min). Module 4 (~20 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — Why 9.9 megawatts

**The single idea:** ERCOT treats resources under 10 MW on a distribution feeder as distributed generation resources with 'a less stringent planning study process', studied by the distribution service provider — the wires company — not by transmission planners. 'You can skip a part of the interconnection process with ERCOT (with projects under 10MW) so your time to market is quicker.' Registration with ERCOT still happens above 1 MW; the market is not skipped, the transmission study is.

**The trade.** A hundredth of a gigawatt project's revenue per site, each with its own lease, permit, study and EPC mobilisation — workable only with standardised development and buyers who want portfolios. Two hours because 'going to three or four you really get hammered on the Capex'.

**Self-check:** Bimergen, GoodPeak, ON.energy and Plus Power now build to the same size. What does that say about the template? *(That the arithmetic works — and that it is a market anyone can enter, not a moat.)*

## Module 2 — Siting on leased land

**The single idea:** the landowner contributes an acre near a feeder and receives rent; the developer contributes the grid analysis, the interconnection, the permit and the contracts; the turnkey EPC carries the asset to operation after the sale; the buyer contributes capital at NTP. Greenport: a 100 MW / 200 MWh exception at 345 kV on a proposed airport campus 'to host data centers' — a campus that fifteen years after its Chapter 381 agreement 'has yet to be approved'.

**Self-check:** what does a leased site expose the battery to that an owned site does not? *(The landlord's plans and permits.)*

## Module 3 — Selling at notice to proceed

**The single idea:** NTP is the last rung before construction spend — most risk retired, highest price relative to money at risk. The seller keeps the development margin and gives up the operating life; the buyer, a financial owner without an EPC relationship, gets one contract to commissioning. Price moves with IRR: 'A project that was at 12% IRR last year, is now at 14-15% IRR because of the BESS price falls.' Greenport went to a LevelTen auction; three 9.9 MW assets were 'recently sold' to no one named.

**Self-check:** falling battery prices raised the projects' IRR. Who captures the gain — seller or buyer? *(The seller, until buyers' required returns rise to meet it; the auction decides.)*

## Module 4 — Where it fails, and how to read silence

**The single idea:** no buyer, no business. The four checks: the parent's newsroom (no storage release since 2024), the ISO trackers (absent from Cleanview and Modo), any counterparty naming it (none), the people (the president remains; the VP who explained the model is gone). Add the host site's unapproved status, equipment bought before the FEOC rule, and a parent whose capital plan is a '$5 billion' micro-data-centre fleet.

**Self-check:** the record cannot distinguish private sales from no sales. What is the honest dossier statement? *(That three sales were stated by the company in March 2024 and never confirmed by any other party, and that nothing operating is attributable to the company.)*
