# Blackstone — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** Blackstone Inc. (NYSE: BX) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no finance background.

**What this teaches.** Not Blackstone trivia. The machinery of a listed alternative asset manager: how it earns, which of its several "assets under management" numbers actually produces revenue, what a perpetual-capital vehicle changes about an owner's behaviour, and why a firm can beat its earnings forecast five quarters running and still lose a quarter of its market value. The AI-data-centre material is the worked example, not the syllabus.

**Why this gap.** Two `investor` guides already exist. `excelsior-energy-capital` teaches the closed-end fund and the platform beneath it; `mgx` teaches the sovereign wealth fund and reading a fund that publishes no numbers. Neither teaches **the manager's own profit and loss account** — the thing a listed GP is actually selling to its shareholders. That is this guide's territory.

## Module 1 — What an asset manager sells
Two engines: the management fee (recurring, predictable, produces fee-related earnings) and performance income (lumpy, depends on realisations, produces carried interest). Why managers publish distributable earnings instead of leaning on accounting profit. Exercise: given fee-related earnings and distributable earnings for two years, say which year depended more on selling things.

## Module 2 — Three numbers called AUM
Total AUM, fee-earning AUM, perpetual-capital AUM, and the overlap of the last two. Why a large fundraise raises the headline before it raises revenue (dry powder). Exercise: from Blackstone's Q2 2026 figures, compute the share of total AUM that earns nothing today, and say what would have to happen for it to start earning.

## Module 3 — The clock
Closed-end funds must return capital; perpetual vehicles need not. The controlled experiment inside this corpus: Blackstone has held QTS five years and increased its stake; Macquarie held Aligned eight years and sold. Same asset class, opposite structural obligation. Then the cost of perpetual capital: redemption gates, proration, and why BCRED gating in June 2026 moved the share price when the earnings did not.

## Module 4 — What a take-private does
Using QTS. Equity consideration versus transaction value including debt, and the roughly USD 1.9bn that does not reconcile between the filed figures and the USD 10bn headline. What disappears from the public record at deregistration, and what the new owner can do that a quarterly-reporting REIT could not — buy land and power years ahead of a tenant.

## Module 5 — Reading a Blackstone appearance correctly
The vehicle names and what each one implies: Blackstone Infrastructure Partners and BREIT own; Blackstone Credit & Insurance lends; Tactical Opportunities does hybrids. Three corpus examples where reading "Blackstone" as ownership gives the wrong answer (Strata, Arevon, Aligned). Then the two disambiguation traps: BlackRock is a different firm, and Revantage is not Vantage.

## Pacing
Five modules, roughly 45 minutes each. Modules 1-2 are prerequisites for everything else. Module 3 is the conceptual core and rewards a second pass. Modules 4-5 are applied and can be read in either order.
