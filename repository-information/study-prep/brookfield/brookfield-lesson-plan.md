# Brookfield — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** Brookfield Asset Management Ltd. (NYSE/TSX: BAM) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no finance background.

**What this teaches.** How a single investment franchise organises itself as five separately listed companies, why it would do that, and what a reader has to check before comparing any two of its numbers. Then the operating-owner advantage — what a firm that owns generation can offer an AI data centre that a purely financial investor cannot.

**Why this gap.** The Blackstone guide teaches the manager's income statement; the Excelsior and MGX guides teach fund structures. None of them teaches **corporate structure as a subject** — listed affiliates, paired corporations, foreign-private-issuer reporting, and the question of who keeps the carry. Brookfield is the corpus's only company where getting the entity wrong changes every figure by an order of magnitude.

## Module 1 — Five companies, one brand
The entity table: Brookfield Corporation, Brookfield Asset Management, Brookfield Infrastructure Partners, Brookfield Renewable Partners, and the paired corporations. Which files what form, under which accounting rulebook. Exercise: given three figures and their sources, say which entity each belongs to and whether they can be added together. (They cannot.)

## Module 2 — Why the filing form matters
Domestic filer versus foreign private issuer; 10-K/10-Q versus 20-F/40-F; US GAAP versus IFRS. What depth and frequency you lose, and why an IFRS balance sheet and a US GAAP balance sheet are not directly comparable even in the same currency.

## Module 3 — Manager, fund, vehicle
Who does the deal, who pays for it, who reports a slice of it. Compass Datacenters worked through: funds acquire co-control alongside Ontario Teachers'; the listed affiliate holds about 15%, equity-accounted, not consolidated. Exercise: estimate the error introduced by reading the affiliate's accounts as the platform's.

## Module 4 — Where the money ends up
The Relationship Agreement: the listed manager collects fees while the parent retains effectively all carried interest on mature funds and a third on newer ones. Contrast with Blackstone, where the listed parent keeps its own carry. Then the 2025 plan of arrangement and why attributable net income quadrupled without operations changing.

## Module 5 — Bring your own power
The interconnection queue as the binding constraint on AI data centres, and dedicated generation as the answer. Paducah as the worked example. Then the discipline that must accompany it: a framework agreement is an intention with a number attached, and a fund's size is not a programme's ambition — the USD 10bn/USD 100bn distinction is a tenfold error waiting to be made.

## Pacing
Five modules, roughly 45 minutes each. Module 1 is a prerequisite for all the others and is worth memorising rather than reading. Modules 3 and 4 are the analytical core.
