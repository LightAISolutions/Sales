# Compass Datacenters — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the sponsor's chair** — how a pension fund and an infrastructure fund come to own a build-to-suit data-center landlord, what "co-control" actually buys, how to read a private landlord through its listed sponsor's letters and segment reporting, the pipeline ladder (operating, contracted, reserved, land) that every megawatt figure sits on, why "delivered or under development" needs a rung and an owner, how one leased building gets paid for three times (sponsor equity, construction loan, asset-backed securities) and then a fourth (asset-level permanent capital), what a rating agency decides along the way, why a manufacturing model is what a sponsor pays for, who pays for the substation, and what consent now costs. Compass Datacenters is the worked example because its sponsor is listed and wrote down what it paid and what it bought, because the company runs a serial ABS programme and a factory-on-campus delivery model, and because 2026 gave it both a lost 2,000-acre site and a 4–1 midnight vote.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Vantage** plan teaches the wholesale operator-landlord and the campus lifecycle; **QTS** the standardized building and perpetual capital; **STACK** the product ladder and the ownership chain of funds and fund managers; **Aligned** power-first development and the infrastructure-fund exit; **Switch** the securitization round-trip; **Fluidstack** the lease from the tenant's chair; **Excelsior** how a fund is raised and read from its filings. This plan climbs to the sponsor and repeats none of those. The **EdgeCore** plan (same session) opens the single-tenant lease from the landlord's side; the **PowerHouse** plan teaches powered land, entitlements and the utility contract behind a campus.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A megawatt figure is meaningless without its rung on the pipeline ladder and the name of who counts it. A build-to-suit building is financed three times, and the sponsor's return comes from the refinancing, not the rent. And the binding constraint on a well-capitalised landlord in 2026 is consent, which is now a priced line in the campus budget.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Compass publishes no accounts, names no tenant and no general contractor (except Power Construction at Hoffman Estates), and states no portfolio total; Brookfield Infrastructure's 2025–2026 letters aggregate it into a segment without naming it. Data Center Dynamics and Mississippi Today could not be read. Where the plan gives arithmetic (the 1,279 MW sum of the Markets page; USD 18 million per MW from the ABS appraisal) it says so.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 in one sitting (~30 min). Module 2 with Brookfield's Q2 2023 letter open (~35 min). Module 3 with the Compass Markets page and the 2026 Outcomes Report (~30 min). Module 4 with the Bisnow ABS coverage (~40 min). Module 5 (~25 min). Module 6 (~25 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — Who the sponsor is, and what co-control buys

**The single idea:** a financial sponsor buys a landlord for its machine — land, leases, team — and structures the ownership so that two sponsors share the cheques and the decisions while the founder keeps running it.

**The parties.** Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan (a pension fund: decades-long, inflation-linked liabilities that a 13-year investment-grade lease matches) and Brookfield Infrastructure (an infrastructure-fund manager with a listed vehicle, BIP, that keeps a slice of each deal and syndicates the rest to its private funds). Before them: RedBird Capital and Ontario Teachers' (2017), Azrieli Group (2019). After them, at asset level: KKR (December 2025).

**The price, read correctly.** Brookfield's Q2 2023 letter: "a co-controlling stake in Compass Datacenters for USD 1.35 billion, including Brookfield Infrastructure's equity of approximately USD 375 million." The listed vehicle put in a quarter; its funds the rest of Brookfield's share; Ontario Teachers' its own half separately. The press "USD 5.5 billion including debt" is an enterprise-value guess never confirmed.

**Co-control.** Equal stakes, board seats for both, consent of both on new campuses, new debt and a sale; the founder-CEO stays. Exercise: list five decisions a co-control agreement must allocate and say which party would care most about each.

**Self-check:** why does a pension fund want a data-center landlord? *(A long, investment-grade, inflation-adjusted lease on a hard-to-replace building has the shape of a pension liability.)*

## Module 2 — Reading a private landlord through its listed sponsor

**The single idea:** the sponsor's documents are the only priced view of the landlord, and each one answers a different question.

**Letter to unitholders at acquisition.** What was paid; what was bought: "approximately 170 megawatts of operating capacity", "735 megawatts" operating, contracted and reserved, "85% underpinned by investment grade hyperscalers", "675 megawatts" contracted with a "13-year weighted average contract duration" and "approximately 60% ... triple net".

**Quarterly segment reporting.** The "data" segment's FFO (USD 502 million for 2025; USD 149 million in Q1 2026, up 46%); events in one line ("raised USD 830 million of investment grade asset-backed securities to fund contracted growth"); a platform-wide pipeline with rungs (3.6 GW potential, 1.2 GW operating, 1.1 GW contracted backlog, 1.3 GW land bank). What it hides: which platform — Compass, Data4 or colocation — any number belongs to.

**Filings.** The same text as 6-K exhibits on EDGAR; nothing extra on a private subsidiary.

**The landlord's own disclosure.** An ESG report with delivery metrics (195 employees; 1.27 PUE; 0 WUE; 100% uptime; 0.56 TRIR; 84% offsite for the Moonraker product) and a Markets page with per-campus MW that mix operating and development. No total, no revenue, no tenant.

**Exercise:** open the Q2 2023 letter and the Q4 2025 letter side by side; write down every number that could be Compass and every number that cannot be attributed.

## Module 3 — The pipeline ladder, and 'delivered or under development'

**The single idea:** operating → contracted → reserved → land with power → land. Each step down removes a risk and adds value; a figure without a rung is marketing.

**Compass's rungs.** 170 MW operating (2023); 675 MW contracted (13-year WALT); 735 MW through reserved; the Markets page's 1,279 MW (this dossier's sum) across all rungs — Red Oak 360 "nearing completion", Meridian 320 "broke ground", Goodyear 212 and El Mirage 108 "development", Loudoun 79 operating.

**The phrase.** "Delivered or under development" is EdgeCore's January 2026 wording — attached to the Compass row by the coverage plan. It is two rungs with no boundary. PowerHouse's "in planning, underway, or completed" is three. Brookfield's "contracted backlog" is one, labelled.

**What the rung is worth.** An operating, 100%-leased pool appraised at USD 3.6 billion for 198.2 MW (~USD 18 million per MW); a Red Oak building under construction financed at a fraction; a field with by-right zoning worth the substation it can carry. The triple-net share (60%) tells the sponsor how much rent survives an operating-cost shock.

**Exercise:** take the three F7 companies' headline figures and write each one's rung and its verifiable operating base (Compass ~200 MW securitized-plus-Red-Oak-and-Loudoun; EdgeCore two buildings, ~62 MW; PowerHouse one building, 60 MW, sold).

## Module 4 — Paying for a building three times: equity, construction loan, ABS (and a fourth: permanent capital)

**The single idea:** a build-to-suit building is financed in stages that track the pipeline ladder, and the sponsor's equity comes back at the refinancing, not from the rent.

**Stage one — equity.** Land, the substation contribution (USD 52.5 million and a ~USD 20 million right-of-way in South Dallas), early works. Nothing is lendable until a lease exists.

**Stage two — the construction loan.** A limited-recourse loan to the campus entity, sized so contracted rent covers debt service (DSCR), drawn as the building rises, against the tenant's investment-grade credit. Compass does not disclose its construction loans; the EdgeCore plan shows four of them in detail.

**Stage three — the ABS.** Bonds backed by the rent of energised, leased buildings, issued from a master trust (Compass runs two, not cross-collateralized): Series 2025-1/2 (USD 885 million, three buildings, S&P A/A-); Series 2026-1 (USD 830 million, six buildings, 198.2 MW, USD 3.6 billion appraisal, "100% leased to four firms", Moody's AAA on the USD 500 million senior tranche — the first on hyperscale ABS). The bond repays the loan; the surplus over the loan is equity recycled.

**The rating split.** S&P caps data-center ABS at single-A; Moody's, Fitch and DBRS go to AAA. The arranger chooses; a notch on USD 500 million is real money yearly.

**Stage four — permanent capital.** KKR (December 2025): "a portion of its operating data centers and future assets", "several billion dollars", described by Compass as an "evergreen financing partnership". Equity recycling without a sale of the platform.

**Exercise:** for the 2026-1 pool, compute the appraisal per MW and compare it with ABX-1's sale price per MW in the PowerHouse plan; explain the gap (shell vs turnkey, lease, operating platform).

## Module 5 — The manufacturing model, and who pays for the substation

**The single idea:** schedule is rent; a sponsor pays for offsite fabrication because it makes "contracted" turn into "operating" on a date, and pays for the substation because it makes the date the developer's rather than the utility's.

**Offsite fabrication.** Moonraker "84% fabricated offsite"; 80–90% of power-centre level-3/4 work moved to the factory in 2025; a Schneider Electric plant on the Red Oak campus under a USD 3 billion five-year agreement; up to 1,500 Siemens medium-voltage skids; Vertiv CoolPhase Flex co-developed; integrated logistics across PHX, IAD and DFW (451 shipments, USD 16.8 million) extending to ORD and JAN in 2026. Three different offsite percentages on the company's own pages — quote with source and date.

**The co-serve utility model.** Crosby's op-ed: developer-funded right-of-way and substation, "creative tariffs" so large customers carry expansion cost proportionally. Mississippi Power's PSC-approved special contract (~500 MW at Meridian, price undisclosed). Islanding on HVO generators; a 191 MW diesel microgrid in Phoenix; endorsement of Governor Abbott's five standards three days before he published them.

**Exercise:** map each Compass campus to its utility and to who funded the substation; mark which are regulated special contracts and which are ERCOT/ComEd/APS service.

## Module 6 — Consent as a cost line

**The single idea:** capital and manufacturing do not help when the constraint is a council; consent is bought, and the developer with the cheapest capital can pay most.

**The record.** Prince William Digital Gateway (2,000 acres with QTS) voided on a public-notice defect, upheld on appeal 31 March 2026, abandoned 29 April. Second Red Oak campus (~830/890 acres) approved 4–1 at midnight with closed-loop, no-municipal-water, noise and setback conditions; county abatement withdrawn in June "due to community pushback"; USD 15 million shelter and USD 12.6 million training building the same month; Governor calling for repeal of exemptions. Statesville, NC 8–0 with 17 speakers against. Infrastructure for America with Digital Realty, QTS and Vantage (September 2026).

**Exercise:** write the consent budget for a hypothetical 300 MW Compass campus — payments, conditions, timeline risk — and say which of them a sponsor would underwrite.

**Where it fails (for the whole plan):** unnamed tenants and unseen concentration; a GW without a rung; retro-edited boilerplate; the rating-agency split; consent as the binding constraint; a sponsor view that is aggregated.
