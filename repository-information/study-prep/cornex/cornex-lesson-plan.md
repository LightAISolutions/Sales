# Cornex — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** the industrial economics of the storage-cell business — how a prismatic LFP cell becomes a 5 MWh container, why every research house ranks the same maker differently, what a price war does to a challenger and an incumbent, how a 500 GWh plan is financed when nothing is audited, and why the best-certified Chinese cell cannot be sold into a US tax-credit project.
**Assumed starting point:** high-school STEM. The CALB plan covers the cell's growth from 280Ah to 684Ah and the Great Power plan covers the wider battery map; the Mitra Chem plan covers the cathode powder one link upstream. This plan assumes none of them, but reads best after CALB's.
**What this is not:** a company crib sheet. Cornex's dates, executives and ownership percentages stay in the dossier.

**Suggested pacing.** Eight modules. Modules 1–3 (cell, container, fire system) are the engineering and take one sitting of about an hour. Modules 4–6 (rankings, the price war, the financing) are the economics and take a second sitting; module 5 is the most transferable. Modules 7–8 (policy walls, reading a private Chinese company) close the map.

**The one sentence to hold onto:** in a commodity cell market, capacity and price buy share; what they cannot buy is the disclosure a customer, a lender or a landscape author needs to know whether the share will last.

## Module 1 — The prismatic LFP storage cell, in one page

**Concepts:** intercalation · ampere-hours · energy density · cycle life · why cells grew.

Describe the object: a sealed aluminium can the size of a hardback, five to ten kilograms, holding a stack of LFP-coated aluminium foil and graphite-coated copper foil with a porous separator between, soaked in electrolyte. Lithium ions shuttle between the coatings; the can's job is to hold that reaction safely for fifteen years.

Teach the three numbers — capacity in Ah (314, 472, 588), energy density in Wh/kg and Wh/L, cycle life to a capacity threshold — and have the learner convert 588Ah at 3.2 V into about 1.9 kWh. Then the format race: every cell carries fixed overhead (can, terminals, vent, a BMS channel) and a container holds a fixed number in series, so a bigger cell puts more MWh in the same box. Cornex's 20-foot container went from 5.0 MWh on 314Ah to 6.26 MWh on 588Ah with the same footprint. The learner should be able to say why makers fought over 587 versus 588 versus 628: pack tooling standardises on one number.

## Module 2 — What a 5 MWh container is

**Concepts:** series strings and parallel groups · C-rate and duration · liquid cooling · IP ratings · certification.

Read a container datasheet line by line. 12 × 1P416S means 416 cells in series (about 1,331 V) in twelve parallel strings; power over energy is the C-rate (2,500 kW on 5,015 kWh is 0.5C, a two-hour block; the bigger M6 at 0.25C is a deliberate four-hour block). Liquid plates under the packs hold every cell within a few degrees — the biggest lever on cycle life. IP55 on the box and IP67 on the pack say what weather each survives. A 46-tonne box needs a crane pad and a road permit, so density per acre is what the site engineer prices.

**The certification row** is the one to dwell on: UL 9540A is the fire-propagation test a US fire marshal reads, UL 1973 and UL 9540 the product standards, IEC 62619 and 63056 the European equivalents. Passing them is necessary to sell a container in the West and — as Module 7 will show — not sufficient.

## Module 3 — Cooling a cell versus drowning it

**Concepts:** thermal runaway · thermal propagation · immersion cooling versus immersion suppression · gas versus liquid suppression.

Separate the two uses of "immersion". Immersion cooling runs cells permanently in dielectric fluid; immersion suppression, Cornex's 浸默, cools normally through plates and floods a failing pack with liquid only when a runaway is detected. The physics: a failing LFP cell generates its own heat without needing outside oxygen, so a gas that starves a flame does little; a liquid removes heat by mass. The engineering target is propagation — stopping one cell's failure pushing its neighbours past their trigger temperature.

Ask the learner what a buyer should demand: a full-scale UL 9540A unit test report with the suppression system active, and its finding on propagation to adjacent containers. The company reports certificates and a first deployment; it does not publish the test reports.

## Module 4 — Seven houses, seven ranks

**Concepts:** universe · allocation · share versus rank · which house to type against.

Lay out the H1-2026 spread: SNE seventh (30.2 GWh, 6.5%, 461 GWh universe), InfoLink, TrendForce and ICC sixth, Benchmark fifth (6.5%, +116%, over 490 GWh), SPIR third (524 GWh). Then explain it: each house counts a different universe and allocates integrator-made and captive cells differently; rank moves with the neighbours' allocation, share moves less (6.5% on two houses at two ranks).

**Two rules to install:** name the house and period beside every rank; type a segment's roles on one house consistently, and treat a rank that names no house as marketing. Practice on the company's "global top three" — true on SPIR, false on SNE — and on the 10 GWh gap between the company's "over 40 GWh" and SNE's 30.2 for the same half.

## Module 5 — The half-yuan-per-watt-hour move

**Concepts:** cell cost structure · capacity utilisation · below-cost bidding · who wins a price war.

Tell the 2023 story: a pledge to sell 280Ah cells at no more than RMB 0.5/Wh ex-tax, 40% below the market; two years later the market below 0.3 and the company's own bids at 0.27–0.28, at or under cash cost. Then the arithmetic a challenger runs: materials dominate variable cost, the line dominates fixed cost, and an empty plant costs almost as much as a full one; filling it at a small loss beats idling at a large one if scale lowers unit cost and incumbents cannot follow without hurting their margins.

**The second half:** the incumbents did follow, the industry's cell margin went to zero or below, and a price war started by a challenger is won by whoever can bleed longest — usually the incumbent with the balance sheet, unless the challenger has outside cash. Cornex's is a car-dealer group's and, the investigative press suggests, a province's. Have the learner place "break-even from July 2025" — a sentence with no figure, from a company with no income statement — at the confidence it deserves.

## Module 6 — Who pays for a 500 GWh plan?

**Concepts:** effective versus planned capacity · capex per GWh · owner financing · government funds · what "commissioned" proves.

Build the two-column table: "over 110 GWh" effective (unchanged for fourteen months), two plants commissioned in August 2026, one signed and two filed, a plan of "over 500 GWh" that grew 150 GWh in nine months — against a RMB 10 billion bank facility, an owner's group with revenue of RMB 78 billion and profit near RMB 300 million, two RMB 6 billion-class government funds that appeared and vanished, and no audited account anywhere. The learner should be able to state the gap in one honest sentence and refuse to resolve it.

Then the ten-month builds. What speed proves: procurement, construction and staffing faster than any Western peer. What it does not prove: utilisation, yield and customer qualification — the three figures that turn capacity into shipments, and the three the company has not published.

## Module 7 — Why the US is closed

**Concepts:** Section 301 · prohibited foreign entity and the material-assistance cost ratio · Section 1260H · China's export VAT rebate.

Walk the three US rules: a 25% Section 301 duty from 2026 that erases the price advantage; a credit test that counts PFE content by cost, in which cells are about half of a system and a Chinese-owned maker is a PFE by ownership; and a Defense Department list that now carries CATL, BYD, CALB and EVE but not Cornex — a distinction that bars nothing today. Then the Chinese side: an export rebate falling from 13% to 9% to 6% to zero in 2027, which is a margin removed and a pull-forward incentive that flatters 2026.

**The map that results:** frameworks signed in Munich, Dammam and São Paulo, and AI data centres reached only through a Taiwanese integrator's DC-side project in Malaysia and Thailand. Have the learner explain why a company with US fire certificates and two 2024 US framework partners signed nothing in the US in 2026.

## Module 8 — Reading a private Chinese company, and the industry map

**Concepts:** business-registration records · aggregator disagreement · listed-supplier disclosure as a proxy · government pages as stakeholders · the Chesterton list.

A private joint-stock company with no listing owes the public only its registration record, read through aggregators that disagree (registered capital 4.0 or 4.94 billion; ownership 76%/92.5% or 55/25/12.5/7.5). What fills the gap: listed suppliers' exchange filings (1.3 million tonnes of cathode and a million tonnes of electrolyte — at roughly 2.2 kg of cathode per kWh, several hundred GWh of intended output — plus the subsidiaries' registration data the company never publishes), government pages that are stakeholders rather than referees, and trade press that carries what the chairman says in interviews.

Close with the map. Above: cathode from Longpan and Huayou, anode from BTR and Shanshan, electrolyte from Tinci and Capchem. Beside: CATL, EVE, Hithium, BYD, CALB, REPT, Great Power, Gotion, AESC — the SNE top twelve, now complete in the corpus. Below: CRRC Zhuzhou Institute (25 GWh and a joint 688Ah cell), Dongfang Electric, CLOU, the European developers, the Saudi and Egyptian partners, TECO's data centres. And the Chesterton list the learner should recite: no CATL suit exists; M5 shipped from February 2024; no 290 GWh approval can be found; the Wuhan subsidiary dates from 2017; "top three" is one house. The point of the exercise is not to catch the company out. It is that a challenger's story is told mostly by other people, and the reader's job is to know which ones.
