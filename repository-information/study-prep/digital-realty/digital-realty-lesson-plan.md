# Digital Realty — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **how a finished data centre is valued, and how a company that is forbidden from keeping its own profits still builds gigawatts**. Specifically: why real-estate accounting makes earnings meaningless and what replaced them; how a cap rate turns rent into a price; why building at one yield and being valued at another is the entire economic case for development; what happens to rent when a lease expires in a market that has moved; and what a landlord gives up when it grows on other people's money.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Equinix** plan teaches colocation economics, interconnection and the REIT structure in outline. **QTS** teaches the standardized building and perpetual capital; **STACK** the product ladder and the ownership chain; **Aligned** cooling and power-first development; **Switch** density, Uptime tiers and the securitisation round-trip; **Compass**, **EdgeCore**, **PowerHouse** and **Prime** the sponsor's chair, the build-to-suit lease, powered land and the recapitalisation ladder. **This plan assumes all of them and repeats none.** Equinix's four bullets on REITs and FFO are correct and stop exactly where the interesting part begins; this plan starts there.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A REIT's tax advantage is inseparable from a funding constraint, and every structural choice the company makes descends from that trade. A building's value is its net operating income divided by a number that has nothing to do with the building — which is why the same asset can lose a third of its value in a year with the rent unchanged. And the gap between what it costs to build and what a finished one is worth is not a market inefficiency; it is the price of carrying construction and leasing risk.

**One boundary, held throughout.** This plan stops at the meter and the loading dock. Cooling architecture, rack density, PUE, electrical topology and interconnection mechanics belong to the nine guides listed above.

**A note on the subject.** Digital Realty is used because it is the only large data-centre landlord whose economics are fully public — every figure here is checkable against an SEC filing. It is not held up as exemplary. Where its own claims fail the test, the plan says so, and one of them fails outright: the proposition that it is the largest AI-data-centre landlord is **unfalsifiable**, because no published ranking measures that category at all.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 with the FY2025 10-K open (~35 min). Module 2 with the Q2 2026 supplement and a calculator (~45 min). Module 3 with a calculator — this is the arithmetic-heavy module (~45 min). Module 4 with the renewal-spread table (~35 min). Module 5 with the fund and joint-venture disclosures (~35 min). Then the flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — The REIT bargain and the constraint inside it

**Concepts:** REIT status and the 90% distribution test · single versus double taxation · why property income qualifies · retained earnings and why a REIT has none · dilution · the UPREIT structure and operating-partnership units.

**The move to make:** treat the tax advantage and the funding constraint as one fact rather than two. Students consistently learn the first and forget the second, then find every later structural choice arbitrary.

**Work through:** Digital Realty's FY2025 net income of USD 1.27bn against roughly USD 1.9bn of depreciation. Then the 2026 funding record — 6.3 million shares at USD 197.54 for Kansas City and Teraco, 12.3 million more for the Blackstone buy-in, USD 1.5–2.0bn of guided debt — and ask where else the money could have come from.

**Exercise:** a REIT earns USD 100 of taxable income and wants to spend USD 300 on development. Show the three funding routes and state what each costs the existing shareholder.

**Common error to pre-empt:** believing a REIT is a type of asset. It is a tax election. The buildings are identical to a private landlord's.

## Module 2 — Why earnings lie, and the three replacements

**Concepts:** depreciation as a non-cash charge · FFO · Core FFO and management-defined adjustments · AFFO and recurring capex · constant currency · promote income as a contaminant.

**The move to make:** stop treating "earnings" as one number. Make the student ask *which definition* before reading any figure.

**Work through:** the Q2 2026 ladder — net income USD 1.21, FFO USD 2.73, Core FFO USD 2.65, Core FFO ex-promote USD 2.13, constant-currency ex-promote USD 2.11 — all describing the same three months.

**The set-piece:** consensus was USD 1.98 and the headline was USD 2.65. Let the student compute the "beat" both ways before revealing that consensus was framed ex-promote and the real beat is fifteen cents, not sixty-seven.

**Exercise:** a landlord adds a fee-earning fund business. Describe two ways its headline earnings measure becomes less informative, and one disclosure that would fix it.

**Common error to pre-empt:** assuming Core FFO is comparable between companies. Each firm defines its own adjustments.

## Module 3 — Cap rates, yield on cost, and the development spread

**Concepts:** NOI and what is excluded from it · cap rate as both a yield and a valuation multiple · the inverse relationship between cap rate and value · yield on cost · the development spread · replacement cost · per-megawatt comparison and its traps.

**The move to make:** get the inversion into muscle memory. A *lower* cap rate is a *higher* price. Students reverse this constantly.

**Work through:** the real disclosures — a >6.5% initial stabilised cap rate on Digital Realty's Blackstone buy-in (288 MW, USD 7.8bn gross), and an approximately 11.5% pre-tax stabilised cash yield on the 1,402 MW pipeline. Then the arithmetic: USD 100 built at 11.5% produces USD 11.5 of NOI, worth USD 177 at a 6.5% cap rate.

**Exercise set:**
1. NOI USD 40m at a 6.5% cap rate — value it. Then at 8%. State the percentage change and confirm the rent never moved.
2. Build at 11% and sell at 7%: what does USD 200m of spending create?
3. A campus cannot get grid power until 2032. Which of the three valuation methods now governs, and why?

**Common error to pre-empt:** treating the development spread as free money. Every point of it is payment for risks — overrun, delay, and above all lease-up — that a buyer of a finished building does not take.

## Module 4 — What a lease is worth when it ends

**Concepts:** mark-to-market and renewal spreads · cash versus GAAP spreads · churn · lease-expiry schedules and roll-over concentration · bookings · backlog and commencement lag · pre-leasing as a risk measure.

**The move to make:** show that the *level* of expiring rent matters more than the percentage change, and that percentages of a growing base conceal absolute risk.

**Work through:** Q2 2026 renewals — 0–1 MW at +5.2% (USD 331 → USD 348 per kW) against >1 MW at +66.7% cash and +92.3% GAAP (USD 159 → USD 265 per kW). Ask why the large tenants were paying half as much per kW before assuming superior pricing power today. Then 25.9% of annualised rent expiring in 2026.

**The set-piece:** pipeline underway rose 82% to 1,402 MW while pre-leasing fell 64% → 54%. Have the student convert both to unleased megawatts (about 277 MW → about 645 MW) before commenting. The percentage understates the change; only the absolute figure shows that speculative capacity more than doubled.

**Exercise:** given backlog of USD 1.9bn commencing USD 635m / USD 480m / USD 312m across 2026/27/28+, and in-place rent, estimate the revenue already contracted for next year and state what could still prevent it.

**Common error to pre-empt:** extrapolating a record mark-to-market. It is past scarcity being repriced once, not a recurring capability.

## Module 5 — Funding growth without retained earnings

**Concepts:** the debt / equity / partner triangle · refinancing risk when a legacy coupon rolls · third-party fund management · fee income and promote · capital recycling in both directions · what consolidation hides.

**The move to make:** connect Module 1's constraint to the fund business. The fund is not diversification; it is the structural answer to being unable to retain earnings.

**Work through:** USD 18.6bn of debt at 2.90% with USD 8.9bn maturing 2027–2029 against guided new issuance at 4.5–5.5%. Then the USD 3.25bn hyperscale fund — 20% retained plus the management mandate — and fee income up 98.3% to USD 143.8m. Then the reverse: buying Blackstone out of three buildings for USD 3.5bn.

**Exercise:** a landlord can build a USD 1bn campus alone, or put in USD 200m and manage a fund for the rest at a fee. Compare the two on capital employed, control and reported growth, and name what a reader loses in the second case.

**Common error to pre-empt:** reading fee-driven growth as portfolio growth. Twenty unconsolidated entities sit between the reader and the buildings.
