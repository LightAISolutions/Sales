# EdgeCore Digital Infrastructure — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the single-tenant build-to-suit lease from the landlord's chair** — what the landlord promises to one hyperscaler (critical load, design standard, delivery milestones, ready-for-service date, liquidated damages), what the tenant promises back (rent per kW on contracted load, take-or-pay, term, escalators, triple-net costs), the options that turn one building into a campus relationship (expansion options, rights of first offer), why the tenant's name is the one thing the landlord will not say, how a bank turns the lease into a limited-recourse construction loan in a special-purpose borrower, how the first stabilized lease becomes a bond, what density and cooling clauses commit the landlord to before the building exists, who pays for power delivery, and what "we have never missed a customer milestone" does and does not cover. EdgeCore is the worked example because it is the purest single-tenant case in the corpus ("one building, one customer" on every campus page), because it financed four campuses in thirty months with the same instrument, and because it stated its own power-cost walk-away threshold in public.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Fluidstack** plan opens the same lease from the tenant's chair (who signs, who guarantees, what the lender can reach); **Vantage** and **STACK** teach the product ladder and the operator-landlord model; **Nscale** teaches what an anchor's signature buys and what "contracted" means; **Aligned** teaches cooling physics. The **Compass** plan (same session) teaches the sponsor's capital stack and the ABS market; the **PowerHouse** plan teaches the land, entitlement and utility contract under the lease. This plan sits across the table from Fluidstack and repeats none of those.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A build-to-suit lease is a construction contract stapled to a rental contract, and every clause reads differently from the landlord's, the tenant's and the lender's chair. A limited-recourse loan is sized to the lease, not the land, which is why a USD 1.5 billion "fully leased" financing can name no tenant. And a landlord runs two clocks — the lease clock its milestones cover, and the entitlement clock that slips before any lease exists.

**One boundary, held throughout.** EdgeCore publishes no lease terms, no tenant, no revenue, no headcount, no general contractor and no ESG report; Partners Group's reports mention it only as a label; Data Center Dynamics could not be read. Lease mechanics are taught as the industry's standard form, anchored to the outline EdgeCore and its sponsor do disclose ("contracts ... based on their contracted power capacity", "fully leased to single hyperscale tenant"). Where the plan gives an illustrative number it says so.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 in one sitting (~35 min). Module 2 with the April 2026 Sterling financing release open (~35 min). Module 3 with the Culpeper 2024 release and the 2026 campus page side by side (~25 min). Module 4 with the Ashburn campus page (~25 min). Module 5 with the Data Center Knowledge ratepayer interview (~20 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — Anatomy of a build-to-suit lease, from three chairs

**The single idea:** the construction half fixes what is delivered and when; the rental half fixes what is paid and for how long; the options fix the relationship beyond one building.

**Construction half.** Critical IT load (36 MW in the 2024 spec; 72 MW at AS01, 42 MW at AS02); design standard (N+1 or better, a PUE band, a WUE, density); delivery milestones ending in a ready-for-service date ("initial occupancy: November 2026" for AS01, "July 2027" for AS02); liquidated damages per day of delay.

**Rental half.** Rent per kW of contracted load per month, take-or-pay on the whole contracted load, a term of ten to fifteen years or longer, an escalator of a few percent a year, and in a triple-net lease the taxes, insurance and maintenance on top. Partners Group's thesis: "customers charged a price based on their contracted power capacity, leading to highly visible cashflows."

**Options and boilerplate.** Expansion options on the next building; rights of first offer on adjacent land (why EdgeCore bought 43.87 acres next to Mesa and 697 acres in Louisa before any lease); confidentiality ("declined to identify tenants"); change of control (what happens when Partners Group's funds exit).

**Risk allocation.** Construction risk with the landlord until ready for service; utilisation risk with the tenant from day one; credit risk with the landlord, which is why it wants an investment-grade tenant and why Fluidstack's landlords want a guarantor when they cannot get one.

**Exercise:** take the "same clause, three chairs" table in the study guide and add a row for a water-use covenant; write what each chair reads.

## Module 2 — From lease to loan: the limited-recourse campus financing

**The single idea:** the loan is sized to the lease; the borrower is the campus; the sponsor is not on the hook.

**The four deals.** Mesa USD 1.9 billion (January 2024; MUFG, TD, ING, Scotiabank, Santander; borrower MECP1 Mesa 1, LLC; "first green loan"); Reno and Ashburn USD 1.1 billion (March 2024); Santa Clara USD 440 million (April 2024; MECP1 Santa Clara 1, LLC); Sterling USD 1.5 billion (April 2026; Apterra, Global Infrastructure Partners, ING; two "senior secured, limited-recourse term loans"). About USD 5.2 billion of campus-level debt, each dollar tied to a specific lease at a specific building.

**Sizing.** Contracted rent less landlord costs must cover interest and principal by a margin (DSCR). A 72 MW building taken or paid for fifteen years by an investment-grade tenant supports a great deal of debt; an empty one almost none. The lenders learned the tenant's name in the presale; the market was told its class.

**Labels.** Green loan (ING as structuring agent; proceeds fund assets meeting an environmental standard); accordion and letter-of-credit facilities for expansion and utility credit support.

**From loan to bond.** July 2025: USD 235 million ABS on PH01's single lease (ECore 2025-1; S&P A-/BBB-) — one stabilized lease becomes a bond. Compare Compass's Moody's AAA on a six-building pool (the Compass plan).

**Exercise:** for AS01 (72 MW) assume an illustrative rent and a DSCR of 1.3 and back out how much debt the lease supports; state every assumption.

## Module 3 — Two clocks: 'milestones met' and the pipeline that slipped

**The single idea:** customer milestones are lease-clock dates set after a signature; the entitlement clock — land, power, permits, signature — slips for reasons the landlord does not control, and the claim is silent about it.

**The lease clock kept.** "100% Customer Milestones Met"; "10 out of 10" 2025 targets; PH01 (2019) and SV01 (September 2025) delivered; AS01 topped out September 2025 for November 2026 occupancy; AS02 topped out July 2026 for July 2027.

**The entitlement clock slipped.** Reno: launch market February 2018, "225 MW" planned by 2019, groundbreaking August 2023 on a different site (56 acres at TRIC, 216 MW, not 136 acres by the airport at 400+), "late 2025" target, still "first of three 72MW" building in January 2026. Culpeper: announced July 2024 at "216MW" initial and "up to 432MW" with power in early 2028; campus page now 196 MW from mid-2029. Richardson, Texas (165 MW, 2018): gone from every later list. Mesa expansion: cut from three buildings to two in March 2026 for an SRP switchyard.

**Reading a delivery claim.** Count buildings a third party has seen opened (two), buildings topped out under a lease (three), and put everything else on the entitlement clock. Nscale's plan teaches "contracted" as a word; the test here is which clock.

**Exercise:** build a two-lane timeline for Reno from 2018 to 2026 (entitlement lane, lease lane) and mark where a milestone could first have existed.

## Module 4 — Density and cooling written into the lease

**The single idea:** a density clause is a promise about a building not yet built, and it commits the landlord to structure and plant, not just electricity.

**The three numbers.** Rack density: "Designed for 30-40 kW per rack" (2023–24 spec) versus "rack densities well over 50 kW" and "direct-to-chip" readiness (2026 pages). PUE: "<1.25" at Mesa Building 1 (its engineer), "<1.3" at Santa Clara. WUE: "below .01 L/kWh" everywhere, from closed-loop chilled water and dry coolers.

**What the clause commits to.** Floor loading, ceiling height, mechanical galleries "sized for variable water-to-air cooling ratios", pipework to the row; AS01/AS02 "structurally engineered to accommodate liquid to liquid loads, including NVIDIA VR racks" — space and structure for a tenant that may convert from air to liquid mid-lease.

**Intent versus capability.** No third-party source describes an operating liquid-cooled EdgeCore hall; the delivered buildings were built to the 30–40 kW spec. Aligned's plan has the physics; here the point is who pays for the retrofit if the promise was under-engineered (the landlord).

**Water as entitlement.** Mesa "water-neutral" by SRP watershed accounting on top of a design that withdraws little; MIT Technology Review's confirmation that the Reno building uses an air-cooled closed-loop chiller. In water-constrained markets a near-zero WUE is the permit, not the marketing.

**Exercise:** write the density, PUE and WUE clauses for a hypothetical AS01 lease and, for each, name the landlord's engineering decision that makes it keepable.

## Module 5 — Who pays for power delivery: pledge, process, walk-away threshold

**The single idea:** the landlord is the utility's customer; the tenant sees only the ready-for-service date, and 2026 is the year utilities and governments wrote down who pays.

**Process.** SRP's Large Customer Integration Process (2025): customers fund dedicated upgrades up front. Arizona HB 2756 (June 2026): minimum bills, contract terms, infrastructure payments and collateral for "extra high load factor customers". Illinois (March 2026): USD 500,000 per 100 MW above 200 MW, retainable — the PowerHouse plan shows what that did to Joliet.

**Pledge.** The Ratepayer Protection Pledge (signed 13 August 2026): bring or buy supply, pay for delivery infrastructure, pay whether used or not, invest locally, contribute to resilience. It does not decide utility cost allocation.

**The company's own words.** EVP Finance Brewer (Data Center Knowledge, 20 August 2026): power delivery infrastructure is "roughly 5-10% of upfront project costs"; "We expect to bear 100% of the costs"; EdgeCore "will abandon projects if power economics become financially unworkable". A walk-away threshold stated in public — before signature the site is optional; after it, the lease clock binds.

**The record.** Mesa lost a building to SRP's switchyard; Louisa's 1.1 GW has a cooperative's affiliate as supplier and no announced generation plan; Sterling sits on Dominion after Loudoun's allocation constraints.

**Exercise:** compare Compass's "co-serve" pre-funding, EdgeCore's pledge-plus-threshold and PowerHouse's cancelled Joliet agreement as three answers to the same question, and say what each costs the developer.

**Where it fails (for the whole plan):** the tenant you cannot name; two clocks under one claim; a headline that counts land; design intent read as capability; a utility that can redraw the campus; a sponsor whose funds have a life and a change-of-control clause that will be exercised.
