## Module 1 — The mechanical half of an AI data hall

**The single idea:** every watt a GPU draws becomes heat the mechanical contractor must move out of the building.

1. **The path.** Chillers or dry coolers outside; pumps and piping to the halls; CRAH units for air-cooled rooms; coolant distribution units with secondary loops to the racks for liquid-cooled ones; the controls that balance flows and temperatures.
2. **The other mechanical trades.** Plumbing and process piping, high-purity piping where there are labs or fabs, sheet-metal air handling, fire protection — pre-action sprinklers and clean-agent suppression in data halls.
3. **The AI multiplier.** Management's own figure: an AI campus needs 1.5–2× the mechanical scope of a cloud build and 1.25–1.5× the electrical. Density is the cause — 100 kW racks need liquid to every rack, bigger pipes, more pumps and more heat rejection per square foot.
4. **Prefabrication.** Pipe racks, pump skids and air-handling assemblies welded and tested in a shop; the group's largest shops run to 250,000 sq ft. The record attributes mechanical margin gains to 'virtual design and construction, prefabrication, and automation'.
5. **For a seller.** The mechanical contractor sets and pipes your chillers and CDUs and often buys them under its contract; its shop drawings decide how your product connects.

**Self-check:** why does mechanical revenue grow faster than electrical when the mix shifts to AI? *(The heat-removal path scales with density more steeply than the power path.)*

## Module 2 — How a specialty contractor reports

**The single idea:** three disclosures turn a contractor's filings into a quarterly demand indicator for the supply chain.

1. **Percentage of completion.** Fixed-price revenue is recognised as costs are incurred against each job's estimated total cost (the cost-to-cost method). Revenue follows work, not cash; a cost estimate change on one job flows through the segment's margin in that quarter.
2. **Remaining performance obligations.** Signed contract value not yet recognised — backlog, including confirmed and pending change orders. Construction RPO is not reduced for cancellation rights because cancelling carries 'an inherent substantial economic penalty'.
3. **The market-sector table.** Each construction segment's revenue split by end market, including 'network and communications (including data centers, data and fiber projects, and cabling)'. It is the only audited quarterly read on data-centre construction spend by trade; the dossier's spec table carries the series.
4. **Segment operating margin.** Mix explains most moves: construction-manager roles and GMP contracts earn fees; self-performed fixed-price work earns more when executed well.
5. **For a seller.** Read the N&C line and its share of each segment every quarter; rising share in mechanical means AI halls, not cloud halls.

**Self-check:** a contractor's N&C share of mechanical revenue rises from 22% to 35% in a year. What does that tell you about the halls being built? *(Liquid-cooled, higher-density — the mechanical-heavy AI mix.)*

## Module 3 — Reading backlog

**The single idea:** growth, conversion and in-year bookings are three different signals.

1. **Growth.** Backlog rises when bookings outrun revenue; a 44% rise in a year is demand.
2. **Conversion.** The share expected to be recognised within twelve months fell from about 85% to about 75%: projects are larger and longer — more visibility, more exposure to owner pauses and power delays.
3. **In-year bookings.** The share of this year's revenue booked this year fell from about 60% to 40–45%: the company lives more on last year's signings, so a bookings slowdown would surface in revenue a year later.
4. **For a seller.** Data-centre backlog is equipment not yet bought or installed — a leading indicator for orders.

**Self-check:** which backlog figure would first show a slowdown in new campus awards? *(In-year bookings share, then RPO growth; revenue last.)*

## Module 4 — Fixed price, GMP and cost-plus

**The single idea:** margin moves with contract mix as much as with execution.

1. **Lump sum.** The contractor keeps savings and eats overruns; highest margin when execution is good and the job is familiar.
2. **Guaranteed maximum price.** The owner pays cost up to a cap and usually shares savings; the contractor earns a fee.
3. **Cost-plus.** Cost plus a fee, no cap — chosen when design is still moving, which describes much AI work.
4. **Reading a dip.** The record shows mechanical margin falling 110 basis points on 'a greater percentage of revenues generated from projects for which we are acting as either the construction manager or prime contractor' and 'an increase in the number of guaranteed maximum price and cost plus contracts, particularly in newer geographies'. A 'challenged project' — a fixed-price job gone wrong — is the other kind of dip, and the company names it when it happens.

**Self-check:** owners move to GMP when design keeps changing. What does that do to a contractor's margin and risk? *(Lower margin, lower risk — a fee instead of a bet.)*

## Module 5 — The union labour model at scale

**The single idea:** union craft can be surged and will travel; the price is wage floors and pension exposure, and the constraint it does not solve is supervision.

1. **The structure.** Craft hired from locals under roughly 450 collective bargaining agreements; contributions to about 200 multiemployer pension plans.
2. **The advantages.** Trained pools callable from other locals; standard travel and per-diem terms; workers who 'will travel themselves' to rural campuses.
3. **The costs.** Wage floors; withdrawal liability — an employer that leaves a multiemployer plan owes its share of unfunded obligations, which the filings say 'could be material'.
4. **The real bottleneck.** 'Our core product is really field labor supervision and leadership… our real bottleneck… is supervision.' Superintendents and foremen, not electricians, cap growth.
5. **The merit-shop contrast.** Open-shop rivals train their own and win where union density is low — the subject of the Faith Technologies plan.

**Self-check:** why does the company refuse to use labour brokers? *(Brokered crews arrive without the supervision and relationships that make a job productive; it buys contractors that bring both.)*

## Module 6 — Why a national contractor buys local ones

**The single idea:** a campus in a new state needs local supervision, union relationships and a shop; buying them takes a quarter, building them takes years.

1. **The programme.** 'Low- and medium-voltage contractors to expand its geographic reach and strengthen market density' — the Southeast, Texas, the Midwest.
2. **What is bought.** People and relationships, booked as customer relationships and trade names amortised over years; the acquired company keeps its name because the name is the relationship.
3. **The seller's case.** An ESOP-owned contractor sells when its trust needs liquidity; a family contractor sells for succession — both appear in the record.
4. **For a seller.** The subsidiary map is the map of where the group can take your equipment next.

**Self-check:** what does keeping an acquired contractor's name preserve? *(The local customer and union relationships that were the point of the purchase.)*

## Module 7 — Reading a beat

**The single idea:** consensus is an average of estimates, guidance is the company's own range, and a stock can fall on a beat.

1. **Consensus.** Trackers aggregate different analyst sets; a quarter can be a 1% miss on one and a slight beat on another. State the tracker.
2. **Guidance.** The company's range for the year; a raise matters more than one quarter. Two raises in a year is a contractor telling suppliers to expect more purchase orders.
3. **Why shares fall on beats.** The market priced a bigger beat; guidance was held; growth concentrated in one segment whose turn investors fear. The record has all three.
4. **For a seller.** Follow the guidance path and the N&C line, not the day's share move.

**Self-check:** which matters more for next year's equipment orders — a quarterly EPS beat or a raised revenue guide? *(The raised guide.)*

## Module 8 — The electrical half, briefly

**The single idea:** the same group also installs the power path; the Faith Technologies plan teaches it in depth.

1. **Scope.** Medium-voltage service, switchgear, transformers, generators and paralleling, UPS, busway and PDUs — plus low-voltage, cabling and EV charging.
2. **Where it sits.** The electrical segment's N&C share passed 50% first; the mechanical half is catching up as halls go liquid.
3. **Procurement.** Fixed-price and GMP contracts put much of the equipment under the contractor's purchase orders; the filings describe reliance on 'third-party vendors and manufacturers' and advance buying to beat lead times.

**Self-check:** under which contract forms does this contractor sign for your switchgear? *(Fixed-price and GMP — most of its construction work.)*

## Risks to keep in view (from the dossier)

- Concentration: network and communications is a third of backlog and growing; the company names a data-centre capex decline as its first demand risk.
- Margin mix: GMP and cost-plus in new geographies; a fixed-price challenged project in Q4 2025.
- Conversion: twelve-month share falling as projects lengthen.
- Power: management calls power infrastructure the biggest long-term constraint; local moratoria are a new one.
