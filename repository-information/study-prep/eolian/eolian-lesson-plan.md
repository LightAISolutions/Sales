# Eolian — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** Eolian (private; GIP/BlackRock portfolio; long-duration merchant storage in ERCOT and PJM) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no wholesale-market background.

**What this teaches.** Where a grid battery's money actually comes from. The revenue stack layer by layer; why one layer collapses in every maturing market while another does not; how the same hardware becomes five different businesses in five US markets; what duration is really a bet on; what a forward capacity auction sells; and why an existing grid connection has become more valuable than the plant attached to it.

**Why this gap.** Without the stack, every downstream fact is unreadable — why fleets drift longer, why ancillary-heavy business plans age badly, why a developer would expand into a market with worse prices, why a battery company appears in a data-centre siting conversation. This is the module the rest of the buyer set leans on.

**Its place in the buyer set.** Third of five. Apex Clean Energy covers the project lifecycle, Arevon the contract, this one the revenue, Jupiter Power the dispatch decision, Key Capture Energy the regulated purchase. Cells and containers stay with REPT and Gotion.

## Module 1 — The four layers
Energy arbitrage, ancillary services, capacity payments, contracted fees. What each is really buying, and — the part that matters — how each behaves as the storage fleet grows. Exercise: classify a described project by which layers it depends on, then name what would have to change to break it.

## Module 2 — Why reserves stop paying
Frequency regulation and contingency reserves, why batteries are unfairly good at both, and why the requirement is a fixed quantity set by the largest credible contingency rather than by fleet size. The arc every market repeats: reserves pay brilliantly, then they do not. The second-order effect: as reserve income disappears, surviving revenue rewards energy rather than power, so the fleet built for the first era is the wrong shape for the second.

## Module 3 — Five markets, one battery
ERCOT's energy-only design with a very high scarcity cap; CAISO's resource-adequacy obligation; PJM's forward capacity auction; MISO's seasonal construct and locational congestion; NYISO's installed-capacity market and zonal separation. What each does to the stack. Why geographic expansion is a revenue-diversification decision rather than a growth one.

## Module 4 — Duration as a bet
One to two hours against four and beyond, argued from where the money is rather than from a technical ideal. The asymmetry that decides it: a short battery in a market shifting toward capacity value holds the wrong product, while a long battery in a market that stays volatile is merely over-invested. Why fleets drift longer even where short units still earn.

## Module 5 — Forward capacity
What a capacity auction procures and how far ahead. Unforced capacity as the unit sold; the must-offer obligation as the thing bought. Why clearing an auction is a financing instrument — a dated, creditworthy revenue line before construction finishes — and why clearing prices are so volatile when a small increment is bought against an inelastic requirement.

## Module 6 — The connection as currency
Why interconnection queues were designed for slowly growing demand and are inverted by very large new consumers. Speed to power as the binding constraint. Energy-plus-load: placing a large consumer at a site that already holds a dated queue position and a substation, and the real deal risk — whether existing injection rights permit withdrawal without a fresh study.

## Module 7 — A day inside the asset
Overnight charging, the midday trough, reserve sales while standing still, the evening ramp, and the scarcity window. The single lesson: energy sold now cannot be sold later, so every dispatch exercises an option on the rest of the day. This module is deliberately a bridge into the Jupiter Power guide, which takes the same clock apart in detail.

## Pacing
Seven modules, roughly 35 minutes each. Modules 1–2 must be taken together; 3–4 together. Module 6 is the one to read before any conversation about data-centre power, and Module 7 is best read immediately before starting the Jupiter Power guide.
