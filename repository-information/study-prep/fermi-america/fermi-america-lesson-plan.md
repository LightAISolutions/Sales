# Fermi America — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the generation-first campus** — the data-center developer that builds its own power plant behind the fence before it has a tenant: what a gas turbine is and why a fleet is sequenced by time to power rather than size (mobile aeroderivatives in months, small industrial frames in a year, F-class heavy frames in two), why every unit starts in simple cycle and becomes combined cycle only when the load is there to run it, what a "private grid" campus replicates of a utility and what it still buys, what a tenant is actually buying when rent is quoted per kW-month of total facility power, how turbines are financed before a lease exists (equipment warehouse lines, asset-backed loans at 12–13%, convertible notes with a capped call, a build-own-operate-transfer partner) and what each lender wrote about the missing tenant, and how a large-reactor combined-license application proceeds and why "first unit 2033" is a schedule with no financing attached. Fermi America is the worked example because it is the extreme case in the corpus: pre-revenue, Nasdaq-listed since October 2025, about 1.5 GW of turbines landed and 6 GW of gas permitted, four AP1000s under NRC review, nothing energised, one binding-but-conditional tenant, a founder removed and litigating, and going-concern language in the latest 10-Q.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **PowerHouse** plan teaches powered land and the two utility contracts behind a gigawatt campus; the **Intersect** plan teaches the energy park where generation, storage and load share one grid connection; the **Vantage** plan names behind-the-meter microgrids as bridge power; the **Oklo** and **X-energy** plans teach what a reactor does and how it is licensed; the **Xcel** plan teaches the utility side of the SPS contract. This plan starts where none of them does — at a developer whose first product is a power station — and repeats none of them.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A turbine fleet is a schedule, not a plant: the machines are chosen for when they can run, and "landed" means steel at a port. A generation-first developer owns the delivery date and therefore the slip, and pays for that ownership with a balance sheet made of turbines financed at double-digit coupons whose covenants name the tenant that does not yet exist. And a binding lease can be conditional on the landlord's ability to finance the plant — the model's signature clause.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Fermi discloses more than any private developer in the corpus because it is listed, and the plan uses the filings (10-K, 10-Qs, 8-Ks, the prospectus, the Q2 2026 prepared remarks and deck) as the spine; the company newsroom and IR site are readable but the IR press-release and results pages are JavaScript shells, Business Wire and several mirrors are blocked, and the TensorWave guarantor, the Hillcore definitive agreement and the data-center general contractor are undisclosed. Turbine physics is taught as the industry's standard account, anchored to Fermi's own fleet table. Where the plan gives arithmetic (about USD 163 per kW-month on total facility power) it says so.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 in one sitting (~35 min). Module 2 with the Q2 2026 investor presentation's fleet table open (~30 min). Module 3 with the TensorWave release and 10-Q Note 9 side by side (~30 min). Module 4 with the February–July 2026 8-Ks (~35 min). Module 5 with the NRC COL page (~25 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — What a gas turbine is, and the three classes a campus buys

**The single idea:** a gas turbine is a jet engine driving a generator; the three classes a developer can buy trade cost per kW and efficiency against time to power, and a generation-first campus buys them in order of speed.

**The machine.** Compressor, combustor, turbine on one shaft; nothing boils, so it starts in minutes and derates in thin, hot air — nameplate is at ISO conditions (15 °C, sea level), and Fermi's TM2500s fall from 157.5 MW nameplate to 126–132 MW at a 3,500-foot Panhandle site.

**The three classes.** Aeroderivative (GE TM2500, an aircraft engine on a trailer, ~20–35 MW a unit, two months to power, dearest per kW, modest efficiency — bridge power). Small industrial frame (Siemens SGT-800, ~50–60 MW, ~15 months; six with HRSGs make a 400–480 MW block). Heavy-duty F-class (Siemens SGT6-5000F ~240 MW, GE 7F; cheapest per kW, most efficient in simple cycle, ~22 months from port to power and years on the OEM order book if a slot was not bought).

**Fermi's fleet as a schedule.** Six second-hand SGT-800s (from an LNG developer), three refurbished 1980s GE Frame 6Bs (from a New Jersey plant, now in litigation), seven leased TM2500s (pick-up deferred to July–September 2027), three new F-class units (fixed-price agreement, USD 324.4 million). The Q2 2026 deck's "time to power" column — 2, 8, 15, 22 months — is the fleet's organising principle.

**What "landed" means.** "Total landed: 1.5 GW" is turbines cleared at the Port of Houston; the plant is the turbine plus foundation, fuel, switchyard, cooling, controls, permit and an EPC contractor. The civil scope actually built by August 2026 (six "bathtub" excavations, five miles of gas pipe, seven miles of water) is the distance between the two.

**Exercise:** from the deck's fleet table, build Fermi's cumulative simple-cycle MW by quarter from the first-power date (210 MW on 1 July 2027) to 640 MW nameplate in Q4 2027, and mark which units are owned, leased and third-party.

## Module 2 — Simple cycle first, combined cycle later

**The single idea:** a simple-cycle turbine wastes roughly 60% of its fuel as hot exhaust; adding a heat-recovery steam generator and a steam turbine makes about half again as much power from the same gas, at the cost of a year of construction, cooling water and a plant that must run continuously.

**The arithmetic in Fermi's numbers.** Six SGT-800s: 296 MW simple, 396 MW combined (deck; 478 MW ISO on the site). Three F-class: 728 MW simple, 1,042 MW combined. The Siemens package arrived with six HRSGs and an SST-600 steam turbine — Phase 2 hardware delivered with Phase 1.

**Why phasing follows.** Phase 1 is everything in simple cycle — grid power, mobiles, 6Bs, SGT-800s — because a first tenant's 200 MW cares about the date, not the heat rate. Phase 2 converts once there is a load to run continuously; a campus that never reaches it has bought expensive electricity, one that reaches it early has bought a boiler for nothing.

**Water and air.** Combined cycle needs condenser cooling; on the Ogallala that is political — hence hybrid dry-wet cooling (an 80% water-reduction claim), a 2.5 MGD city contract at double the industrial rate, a 30-year groundwater lease, and a city council weighing a moratorium the campus is outside of. Six GW of combustion is a major source; the TCEQ permit of 25 February 2026 is what made it legal, the 5 GW application is the difference between 11 and 17 GW, and the company paused site work nineteen days rather than build ahead of it.

**Exercise:** compute the heat-rate improvement implied by 296 → 396 MW and 728 → 1,042 MW, and estimate the annual gas saving at Fermi's stated 175,000 MMBtu/day for the first gigawatt if the whole first gigawatt were combined cycle.

## Module 3 — Time to power as the product: what a tenant is buying

**The single idea:** on a grid-fed campus the delivery date belongs to the utility; on a generation-first campus the developer owns the date and therefore the risk, and the lease has to carry the power plant.

**The rent.** TensorWave: about USD 6.5 billion over fifteen years for 222 MW of total facility power — roughly USD 163 per kW-month (third-party arithmetic). Two corrections before comparing it with a landlord's rent: it is quoted on total facility power, not critical IT load (divide by PUE), and under a modified net lease the fixed and variable power charges pass through — part of the rent is a fuel bill whose hedge is the developer's firm gas contract.

**What the tenant gets.** Islanding: a date that does not depend on a transmission line the utility has not built, and generation-level redundancy it can inspect ("HyperRedundant"). What it does not get: independence from the utility — the campus still bought 200 MW from SPS because grid power in late 2026 beats owned nameplate in late 2027.

**The signature clause.** Binding, but effective only on closing by 30 September 2026 with financing, board approvals and "requisite project guaranties"; a third-party "backstop" guarantor named and then unnamed in the same week's releases. The tenant will not pay for a plant that may not be financed; the lender will not finance a plant without the tenant.

**Exercise:** convert USD 163 per kW-month on total facility power into a per-kW-of-critical-load rent at PUE 1.2 and 1.4, then split it into a shell rent and a power pass-through at Fermi's stated gas volume and a chosen gas price; state every assumption.

## Module 4 — Financing generation before a tenant: the lender's clauses

**The single idea:** a landlord with a lease borrows against the lease; a developer with turbines borrows against the turbines, and every lender writes the missing tenant into its terms.

**The 2026 stack.** MUFG USD 500 million equipment warehouse (SOFR + 4%, matures August 2027, amortisation steps up if no ≥400 MW lease by November 2026). Keystone/Cape USD 120 million + USD 100 million accordion for high-voltage equipment (12.90%; an approved customer agreement by 31 December 2026 or a 1.05× prepayment; USD 20 million minimum liquidity). Beal Bank/CSG USD 165 million on the SGT-800s (12.00%; USD 37 million exit fee). Yorkville USD 156.3 million 0% standby note, undrawn, expiring with the TensorWave window.

**Equity with two clocks.** The IPO (37.4 million shares at USD 21; USD 746 million net) and, when cash reached USD 62.5 million, USD 431.25 million of 5.00% converts due 2031 at about USD 9.52 with a capped call to USD 14.64 — cheap debt that becomes dear equity if the company succeeds, and an option bought up front to blunt that.

**Going concern.** The 10-Q's "substantial doubt" test (twelve months of obligations against cash and committed facilities) failed on the numbers and passed on management's plans — the converts, undrawn lines, capex "sequencing" and "possible equipment monetization", i.e. selling turbines, the one liquid asset a balance sheet of F-class units has.

**BOOT as the alternative.** Hillcore finances, builds, owns and operates 2.6 GW on subleased acres; Fermi buys under long-term take-or-pay PPAs on at least half of tenant load and holds a 10-year FMV purchase option — ownership of the megawatts traded for not paying for them, and a fuel-and-capacity obligation only rent can service. A framework is not a contract; definitive documents were due about ninety days from 11 August 2026.

**Exercise:** build Fermi's twelve-month liquidity bridge from 30 June 2026 (cash + converts + undrawn facilities − USD 2 billion of stated capex − debt service) and identify which covenant date bites first.

## Module 5 — The nuclear application: what a COL is and what "2033" means

**The single idea:** a Part 52 combined licence authorises construction and operation in one proceeding; docketing is acceptance for review, not approval, and a reactor schedule with no EPC, financing or tenant attached is a licensing asset rather than a plant.

**The process.** Parts 1 and 2 filed June and August 2025, docketed 5 September 2025 (four dockets, one per AP1000); Part 3 (site-specific safety analysis) due 31 December 2026; then safety review, environmental review, a mandatory Commission hearing and an undated decision. Under the applicant-prepared EIS pilot Fermi drafted the environmental statement itself (complete July 2026; final EIS targeted December 2026) — faster for the environmental record, irrelevant to the safety clock.

**The schedule.** One unit in each of 2033–2036, slipped a year inside twelve months; about five years of construction per unit after a licence with no date; USD 70–90 billion of all-phase capital for a company capitalised at about USD 3.3 billion; Hyundai's FEED complete, Doosan's forgings agreement, applications to DOE and a Texas fund, no award.

**Why file.** A docketed COL reserves the site's nuclear future, attracts the vendors whose names carry the story, and distinguishes a gas campus in a market where everyone has turbines — worth exactly what the next financing says it is.

**Exercise:** draw the NRC timeline from docketing to first power with the dated milestones on the COL page, and mark where a financing decision, an EPC contract and a tenant would have to sit for a 2033 date to hold.

**Where it fails (for the whole plan):** the date belongs to the developer, so the slip does too; steel is not a plant; a lease that is not yet a lease; debt written for a tenant that did not exist; a framework counted as 2.6 GW; governance as a cost of capital.
