# Galaxy Digital — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **how a digital-asset financial-services firm works, and what happens when one of them builds a data centre**. Galaxy sits in this library beside seven converted miners and owns a comparable asset — 526 MW of critical IT let to CoreWeave on a 1.63 GW ERCOT campus — and is not remotely the same kind of company. The SEC classifies it as a securities broker-dealer. It reports $60.4bn of revenue against $3.0bn of equity. Its risks are a financial institution's.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** Nothing in this library describes market making, a lending book, assets under management or lessor accounting, because no other covered company has them. The **Cipher Digital** plan teaches why miners pivoted; the **Hut 8** plan teaches the consolidation boundary and non-recourse project finance. This plan is the third and last of the C9 set and takes the layer neither reaches: the business of intermediating other people's money, and the accounting question that decides when a landlord's profit appears.

**Three ideas carry it.** A principal trading firm's revenue is turnover and is not comparable with an operating company's — a distinction worth $60bn here. A financial firm earns most of its money without predicting prices, which changes where its risk actually sits. And whether a fifteen-year lease is a rental or an effective sale is a judgment that moves earnings between year one and year fifteen without changing a dollar of cash.

**One boundary, held throughout.** This plan does not teach cryptocurrency as a technology. Proof of work, hashing and mining economics are the Cipher plan's subject and are not repeated; Galaxy stopped mining at the end of Q1 2025. What is taught here is the *financial* business — trading, lending, staking, asset management — and the accounting where it meets a building.

**A note on the fourth module.** Lessor classification is the most consequential technical fact in this dossier and the newest: the policy paragraph appears for the first time in the Q2 2026 10-Q and is absent from the FY2025 10-K. A student who understands it can read any landlord's earnings profile; one who does not will mistake an accounting choice for a business outcome.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 first and alone — nothing else makes sense until the revenue line is understood (~35 min). Module 2 with a counterparty-count series (~40 min). Module 3 with the AUM table (~25 min). Module 4 with the Q2 2026 10-Q's lease note open — the technical core (~50 min). Module 5 with the three bond coupons side by side (~40 min). Then the flashcards and self-test.

## Module 1 — Why the revenue line says sixty billion

**Concepts:** principal against agency recognition · gross revenue and transaction expense · adjusted gross profit · why a single trade can move a revenue line.

**The move to make:** have the student compute the netted figure themselves before being told it, so the scale of the distinction lands.

**Work through:** FY2025 revenue of $60,406.7m against transaction expenses of $60,175.8m — a difference of about $231m. Galaxy's own 10-K wording that gross recognition means the revenue line "overshadow[s] other parts of the business". The single Q3 2025 trade of **80,000 BTC** named as the principal driver of the year's increase. Then the measures to use instead: adjusted gross profit of $426m and adjusted EBITDA of $33.7m against $715.4m in FY2024.

**Exercise:** Galaxy's revenue is roughly ten times Digital Realty's and its equity is about a third of the size. Explain both facts in two sentences without using the word "bigger".

**Common error to pre-empt:** dismissing the revenue line as meaningless. It measures turnover, which is real and is the base from which adjusted gross profit is extracted. Use it for what it measures.

## Module 2 — Earning without predicting

**Concepts:** market making · bid-ask spread · inventory risk · collateralised lending and loan-to-value · staking and validator economics · where a trading firm's risk actually sits.

**The move to make:** separate fee and spread income from directional income, then show the student that Galaxy's losses come almost entirely from the directional part.

**Work through:** the spread as gross margin per trade and volume as the driver — 1,741 counterparties at Q2 2026 from 1,381 in Q1 2025; Global Markets adjusted gross profit of $294.5m in a record quarter and $30-49m in the three that followed. Then lending: an average book of about $1,438m, typically 110-140% collateralised. Then staking: 5-10% of rewards retained on third-party assets. Then the contrast — the Treasury and Corporate segment, which does take direction, and posted a $434.1m FY2025 net loss against Digital Assets' $193.9m profit.

**Exercise:** a market maker quotes 99/101 and turns its inventory fifty times a year. Sketch its revenue and its risk, and say which one a falling market damages.

**Common error to pre-empt:** assuming a crypto firm's earnings are a leveraged bet on crypto. Most of Galaxy's businesses are fee and spread businesses; the balance-sheet segment is where the beta lives, and it is separately reported.

## Module 3 — Other people's money

**Concepts:** assets under management · fee income against the balance sheet · net flows against market movement · sub-advisory economics.

**The move to make:** establish that AUM is not the firm's asset, then use the flows-against-markets distinction to read a 40% fall correctly.

**Work through:** about $12bn of platform assets at 31 December 2025 falling to $7.1bn at 30 June 2026 — ETFs $1,805m, Alternatives $2,553m, assets under stake $2,790m — against segment adjusted gross profit of $21m, $18m and $17m in three quarters. Then the sub-advisory model and its named partners, and the fact that the fee split is **explicitly not disclosed**. Then scale: the Invesco Galaxy Bitcoin ETF at about $516-526m against BlackRock's IBIT at about $51.9bn.

**Exercise:** a manager's AUM falls 40% while its fee revenue falls 15%. Give two explanations and say what data would distinguish them.

**Common error to pre-empt:** citing a published ranking without checking its date. The most-quoted table places Galaxy fifth of five at the $12bn figure, which is now stale by roughly 40%.

## Module 4 — The lease classification

**Concepts:** operating against sales-type lease · straight-line recognition · lease and non-lease components under ASC 842 and ASC 606 · deferred revenue · why the asset stays in property and equipment.

**The move to make:** build both treatments on the same cash flows and have the student draw the two earnings profiles before being told which Galaxy chose.

**Work through:** the operating classification confirmed in the Q2 2026 10-Q and absent from the FY2025 10-K. Its three visible consequences: the campus at $2,218.2m in property and equipment with no lease receivable; consideration split so that $5.816m of operator revenue is exactly offset by $5.816m of operator cost; and a **$150.0m prepayment still unrecognised at 30 June 2026** although rent had commenced.

**Then the five figures:** $4.5bn (Phase I total lease payments), $10.4bn (Phase II minimum contracted lease payments), **$4,747.8m (the audited ASC 842 total future minimum lease income)**, over $1.2bn (average annual revenue, all phases) and over $30bn (total potential value over 25 years with both options). Only one is audited; none is comparable with a peer's base-term aggregate.

**Exercise:** given identical cash flows, draw the fifteen-year earnings profile under each classification and mark the year in which the difference is largest.

**Common error to pre-empt:** ranking Galaxy's "$1.2bn" against Hut 8's "$26.6bn". One is an annual rate, the other a fifteen-year total. Galaxy publishes no all-phase base-term aggregate at all.

## Module 5 — Underwriting a building like a lender

**Concepts:** yield on cost · CPI-collared escalators · near triple net · construction loan against project notes · cap-rate refinancing and the equity takeout · credit enhancement priced in basis points.

**The move to make:** show that a finance house builds a data centre the way it would structure any other asset, and that the market prices the tenant, not the building.

**Work through:** the 13.7% starting gross yield on cost stated in Galaxy's own words; the 3.0-5.0% CPI collar; the near-triple-net split that explains a ~90% rather than ~100% margin. Then the two instruments — a $1.4bn Deutsche Bank construction loan at SOFR plus 4.75% with a 2.50% floor at 80% loan-to-cost against $350m of equity, and $3,507m of 9.875% notes due 2031.

**Then the comparison that carries the module:** 9.875% against Hut 8's 6.192% and Cipher's 6.125% in the same market at the same time. Unrated single tenant, no disclosed enhancement, roughly 370 basis points.

**Finally the exit:** Galaxy's own illustration — $1bn cost, 80% loan-to-cost, $150m NOI at a 7% cap rate giving $2.1bn, refinanced at 60% for a roughly $500m equity takeout.

**Exercise:** compute the equity takeout if the cap rate widens from 7% to 8.5% and say what that does to the plan.

**Common error to pre-empt:** treating the 9.875% as a judgment on Galaxy's creditworthiness. It is a judgment on CoreWeave's, filtered through a ring-fenced structure that deliberately isolates the parent.
