# Gotion High-Tech — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** Gotion High-Tech Co., Ltd. (SZSE: 002074, Hefei) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no chemistry or tax background.

**What this teaches.** Two things that are the same subject for a Chinese cell maker with an American factory: the chemistry ladder from iron-phosphate through manganese and sodium to solid electrolytes, and the US legal machinery that decides whether any of it may be sold into a subsidised project. Then a third skill the company's own filings demand — telling a reported profit from an earned one.

**Why this gap.** The corpus repeats foreign-entity framings that the 2025 statutory changes made obsolete, and no guide explains how the current rules actually operate. This subject forces the lesson, because it is simultaneously the most US-invested and most US-constrained Chinese cell maker in the coverage.

## Module 1 — The chemistry ladder
Iron-phosphate, manganese-doped iron-phosphate, nickel-manganese-cobalt, sodium-ion and solid-state, taught as a series of trades rather than a progression. What each rung buys in energy and what it costs in life, safety, price or manufacturability. Exercise: given a duty cycle and a cost target, pick a chemistry and name the trade you accepted.

## Module 2 — Roadmaps and manufacturing readiness
How to read a chemistry roadmap by its manufacturing readiness rather than its energy density. Pilot lines measured in fractions of a gigawatt-hour against gigafactories measured in tens. The distance between them is where most battery roadmaps stall, and it is a manufacturing problem, not a chemistry one.

## Module 3 — Nameplate, built and shipped
Three different numbers that get reported as one. Why announced international capacity is the least reliable figure in the industry, and why construction in progress — audited capital spent on assets not yet in service — is a better guide to what is really being built than any announcement. Exercise: given a base list, an aggregate capacity and a set of circulating per-site figures that sum to twice it, decide what to publish.

## Module 4 — How the restricted-entity rules work
The two-part status test — named-entity or control-based — and why incorporation alone can be enough. Why status follows ownership into subsidiaries, so an American plant owned by a restricted parent cannot claim the domestic manufacturing credit. Then the separate content test: a sliding cost ratio, not a blacklist, and why that distinction decides every edge case.

## Module 5 — Four regimes that are constantly confused
Tax-credit eligibility, defence procurement bans, military-company designation and forced-labour import lists — four instruments with four authors, four triggers and four consequences. Why being absent from three while named in one is an ordinary outcome. Includes the discipline of checking whether a named bill was actually enacted rather than assuming it from coverage volume.

## Module 6 — Reading a profit that is not from operations
The headline profit line and the excluding-non-recurring line, and what sits between them: fair-value changes, disposals, government grants and impairments. Three cross-checks — the quarterly pattern, the disclosed composition of total profit, and whether the board has authorised further disposals. The point is not that anything improper is happening; the disclosure is complete and audited. The error would be reading the headline as an operating result when the filing says it is not.

## Module 7 — Control without a majority
How a shareholder can hold the largest stake and not control a company, through a contractual voting-rights waiver — and why the expiry date is the event, because on expiry votes revert with no transaction taking place. Also why a falling percentage on an unchanged share count is dilution rather than a sale.

## Pacing
Seven modules, roughly 40 minutes each. Modules 1–3 are the technology sequence; 4–5 are the policy sequence and should be taken together; 6 and 7 are filing-literacy modules useful for any mainland-listed subject.

The company's FY2025 annual report and H1 2026 interim report, retrieved from the CSRC-designated disclosure host, for financials, capacity, construction in progress and shareholding; the United States Code and the Internal Revenue Service's interim guidance for the restricted-entity and cost-ratio mechanics; SNE Research and CABIA for installation and shipment ranking; law-firm analyses for the statutory chain.
