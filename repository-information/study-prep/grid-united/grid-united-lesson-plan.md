# Grid United — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** Grid United (private, wholly owned by the Arnold family office per its own Oklahoma filing; Houston developer of interregional HVDC transmission — five pre-construction lines led by the 420-mile, 3,000 MW North Plains Connector between the Western grid, MISO and SPP) · **Written:** 2026-09-12 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no power-systems background.

**What this teaches.** Why the United States is three grids and not one, and why only direct current can cross the seams between them; how an HVDC line and its converter stations work and why the converter slot is the scarce input; what a seam connector is worth and how that value is counted in three regions at once; the three ways such a line can be paid for and why Grid United chose utility-owned slices; what development capital is and who can afford to lose it; the permit sequence from notice of intent to record of decision; how to read the five kinds of paper in the project's record; and the industry map.

**Why this gap.** The corpus's transmission guides so far are pieces of other companies' stories — Invenergy's Grain Belt, Hitachi's converters. This is the first guide on a company whose whole product is the interregional line, and the first on the participant-funded, utility-owned model rather than the merchant one.

**Its place in the segment set.** Adjacent to grid-equipment (the developer as the converter buyer) and to utilities (the developer utilities own through). Read beside the Pattern Energy guide, which teaches the same technology from the side of a developer that also owns the generation and has finished a line.

## Module 1 — Three grids and the seams between them
Synchronism; why AC cannot join out-of-step machines; the Eastern, Western and Texas interconnections; DC ties and where they are; interregional transfer capability and why it is tiny; why every Grid United line is a seam crossing.

## Module 2 — How an HVDC line works
Losses and voltage; why AC reach shrinks with length; line-commutated versus voltage-source converters and what a rural endpoint needs; bipole redundancy; converter stations as the long-lead item and the Hitachi capacity reservation.

## Module 3 — What a connector is worth
Weather diversity; effective load carrying capability counted per region; congestion value; deferred grid investment; the free-rider problem — value that accrues to customers who do not pay.

## Module 4 — Who pays
Regional planning and cost allocation (Order 1920) and why seam lines fall outside it; merchant lines under negotiated rates; participant funding and utility-owned slices; DOE as anchor customer (Transmission Facilitation Program) and as grantor (GRIP); what each shape does to the developer's risk.

## Module 5 — Development capital
Land options and easements; interconnection studies; the land-first strategy and its cost; MOUs versus development agreements versus definitive agreements; why a family office and not a fund; the Clean Line lesson.

## Module 6 — The permit gauntlet
NEPA under DOE's coordinated process (notice of intent, draft and final EIS, record of decision); Montana's siting act and North Dakota's route permit; county permits; grid-operator studies at SPP and MISO; the conditional grant and the decision tied to the record of decision.

## Module 7 — Reading the agreements
Development agreement, non-binding MOU, capacity reservation, conditional grant, federal capacity contract: who signed, what it binds, what it signals.

## Module 8 — The industry map
Buyers (western utilities buying eastern access; reserve margin and load growth); competitors (Invenergy, Pattern, NextEra, LS Power, utility affiliates); the policy sorting of 2025–26 and why a utility-owned corridor has survived it so far.

## Pacing
Eight modules of roughly 30 minutes. Modules 1–2 are the technical core and pair with the Hitachi Energy guide's HVDC section. Modules 3–4 are the economics and should be read together. Modules 5–7 read in one sitting before any conversation about the project's status. Module 8 stands alone.

## Sources for the technology and industry content
Grounded in the company's dossier and the sources registered there — its own and its project sites, DOE's NEPA and grant notices, the Montana, North Dakota and Oklahoma regulatory records, partner utilities' filings, the company-commissioned Astrapé studies and DOE's 2026 draft National Transmission Needs Study — plus the corpus's existing transmission and grid-equipment guides. No company trivia is drilled.
