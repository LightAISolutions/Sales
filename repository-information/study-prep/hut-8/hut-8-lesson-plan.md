# Hut 8 — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **where a company ends** — how the boundary of a reporting entity is drawn, and how four ordinary accounting treatments scatter one company's economics across four places a casual reader will not find them. Hut 8 is the corpus's clearest case: a mining fleet inside a separately listed subsidiary controlled on votes but not economics, its second-largest operating site in a joint venture whose revenue never reaches the revenue line, two campuses inside ring-fenced subsidiaries carrying $7.5bn of debt with no recourse to the parent, and a bitcoin holding carried at market that dominates the reported result.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Cipher Digital** plan is the direct prerequisite — it teaches why a miner must become a landlord. **TeraWulf** teaches credit enhancement for a thin-credit tenant and **Core Scientific** teaches reading GAAP through warrants and a pivot. This plan goes past both to the layer none of them touches: consolidation, the equity method, non-recourse ring-fencing and fair-value measurement, treated as four answers to one question.

**Three ideas carry it.** Revenue is not a measure of activity but of activity inside a boundary drawn by control tests, so two companies with identical assets can report revenue differing by a factor of two. Credit support has a price, and this sector supplies an unusually clean four-point comparison of what that price is. And a non-GAAP measure is defined by the company, which means the same quarter can be published as two numbers $105m apart.

**One boundary, held throughout.** This plan stops at the accounting and the capital structure. Cooling, density, siting and construction sequencing belong to the guides above.

**A note on evidence.** More of this plan than usual asks the student to find where something *is not*. King Mountain's $57.3m of revenue is not in the revenue line; the Beacon Point tenant is not named anywhere; Google's warrants at Hut 8 do not exist. Reading for absence is the skill, and it is harder to teach than reading for presence.

**Suggested pacing:** Modules 1 and 2 together, with the segment note and the equity-earnings line open (~50 min). Module 3 with FY2024 and FY2025 side by side (~35 min). Module 4 with the two bond press releases (~45 min). Module 5 with the three landlords' backstop terms in a table the student builds (~35 min). Then the flashcards and self-test.

## Module 1 — Control, not ownership

**Concepts:** consolidation · control as a votes test · dual-class and super-voting shares · noncontrolling interest · the difference between voting power and economic interest.

**The move to make:** insist that the student can state the two percentages separately and say which one drives consolidation. Almost every published description of this company conflates them.

**Work through:** the March 2025 contribution of the mining fleet to American Bitcoin for Class B stock carrying 10,000 votes a share; 80.0% of voting power against 55.30% of economics at 31 March 2026 and about 54% by August; the $110.5m subsidiary ATM that widened the gap; and the Q2 2026 mechanics — a consolidated loss of $177.1m becoming $150.2m attributable after $27.0m went to the noncontrolling interest.

**Exercise:** a parent holds 70% of votes and 40% of economics of a subsidiary earning $100m. What appears in consolidated revenue, and what appears on the noncontrolling-interest line?

**Common error to pre-empt:** "Hut 8 owns 80% of American Bitcoin." True of votes, wrong about money, and wrong in a direction that widens every time the subsidiary issues equity.

## Module 2 — The other side of the line

**Concepts:** the equity method · joint control · one-line consolidation · why revenue is a function of the boundary.

**The move to make:** show that a company's second-largest operating asset can be invisible above the line, and have the student find it.

**Work through:** King Mountain — 280 MW in McCamey, Texas, 50% through TZRC LLC, described in the filings as jointly controlled. $57.3m of colocation revenue in FY2025 and about $27.0m in Q2 2026, of which **none** reaches revenue; $8.7m arrives as "Equity in earnings of unconsolidated joint venture" instead. Note that King Mountain is larger than every consolidated site except Vega.

**Exercise:** rebuild Hut 8's FY2025 "revenue" on a proportionate basis — 54% of American Bitcoin plus 50% of King Mountain plus 100% of the rest — and say which version you would use to compare it with a wholly-owned peer.

**Common error to pre-empt:** assuming that a bigger revenue line means a bigger business. Move one ownership percentage across the 50% line and revenue changes by tens of millions with no change in economics whatsoever.

## Module 3 — When the bottom line is a price chart

**Concepts:** fair-value measurement of digital assets · unrealised gains and losses in the income statement · non-GAAP measures as company-defined · definitional changes and recast prior periods.

**The move to make:** get the student to separate "what happened to the business" from "what happened to the price", using a year in which the two point in opposite directions.

**Work through:** FY2024's $509.3m gain against FY2025's $220.0m loss — a $729m swing with no operating cause. Then Q1 2026: revenue more than tripled to $71.0m while the reported loss doubled to $253.1m on $295.7m of primarily unrealised losses. Then the definitional change: adjusted EBITDA of $(135.4)m for FY2025 and $(250.5)m for Q1 2026 on a definition containing no add-back for the mark, against $10.4m for Q2 2026 on a definition that excludes it — and $(94.6)m for the same quarter on the old basis.

**Exercise:** given two adjusted EBITDA figures from the same company eight months apart, state what you must check before comparing them, and where you would look.

**Common error to pre-empt:** treating "adjusted EBITDA" as a standard measure. It has no definition in any accounting standard. The company writes it, and can rewrite it.

## Module 4 — Ring-fencing, and how a pre-revenue building borrows at investment grade

**Concepts:** non-recourse project finance · the special-purpose issuer · loan-to-cost · debt service coverage ratio · completion guarantees · why consolidated leverage overstates parent risk.

**The move to make:** build the structure field by field, then have the student say who bears what if construction fails.

**Work through:** River Bend's $3.25bn at 6.192% maturing 2042, rated BBB- by S&P and Fitch with a published minimum DSCR of 1.31x, at up to 85% loan-to-cost; Beacon Point's $4.25bn closed six weeks later; about $7.5bn in total against $159.3m of parent recourse debt and roughly $1.3bn of cash and bitcoin.

**Then the seam:** the completion guarantee is where recourse deliberately survives, because no lender takes construction risk on a shell company. Contrast Galaxy, whose parent gave an explicitly *uncapped* completion guarantee at Helios.

**Exercise:** a project company holds $2bn of non-recourse notes and the parent has given an uncapped completion guarantee. Construction stalls at 60%. Who is exposed, and to what?

**Common error to pre-empt:** reading consolidated debt as parent obligation. It is on the balance sheet and it is not the parent's — except to the extent of the guarantee, which is the first thing to find.

## Module 5 — What credit support costs

**Concepts:** credit enhancement · backstops against guarantees · warrants as consideration · pricing credit in basis points · the limits written into guarantees.

**The move to make:** have the student build the four-point comparison themselves and then price it.

**Work through:** the same guarantor (Google), the same tenant (Fluidstack), three landlords. Hut 8 paid **nothing** and issued at 6.192% with BBB- ratings. Cipher paid warrants over about **5.4%** of itself and issued at 7.125%. TeraWulf paid about **14%**. Galaxy, with an unrated tenant and no disclosed enhancement, issued at **9.875%**. Then the limits: Cipher's 10-K states guarantees are "only effective following the rent commencement date", are capped, and that a delayed completion may let a tenant terminate without the guarantee triggering.

**Then the naming discipline:** at River Bend the landlord is Hut 8, the tenant is a Fluidstack subsidiary, the backstop is Google's, and Anthropic is the end user under a separate agreement and not a party to the lease. Two press releases the same day, deliberately kept apart.

**Exercise:** given a landlord, a tenant, a guarantor and an end user, write the one sentence that correctly describes who owes the rent and who stands behind it.

**Common error to pre-empt:** "Hut 8 leased River Bend to Anthropic." It lost the credit structure, which is the only part that matters if compute demand disappoints.
