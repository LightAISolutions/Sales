# HyperStrong — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** HyperStrong (listed; capital-light Chinese storage integrator that buys all of its cells and sells integration, construction and operations) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no battery-procurement or component-qualification background.

**What this teaches.** The cell as a purchased component rather than a chemistry: the four numbers that specify it, how series and parallel counts fall out of a bus voltage, and why capacity is quoted in amp-hours. The format ladder the industry has climbed and what each step transfers — cost out of the battery, risk into the thermal and safety layers. What a qualification programme actually contains and why it cannot be compressed. Why a second cell source is an engineering project rather than a purchase order, and why the industry standardises formats rather than suppliers. The instruments through which cells are actually bought, and the symmetry that no structure secures supply and preserves flexibility at once.

**Why this gap.** The corpus taught what is inside a cell, and it taught what a finished storage plant earns. It had never taught the transaction between them — the point at which a company that makes nothing electrochemical takes on twenty years of liability for someone else's product. That transaction is the defining constraint of the entire integrator tier, and it was undescribed.

**Its place in the integrator set.** First of seven, and the course's entry point. It defines what an integrator is, names the two neighbouring courses it will not repeat (cell chemistry; revenue, dispatch and project finance), and sets the seven-stage structure the other six guides follow. It hands the format-churn problem forward to the enclosure guide, the qualification cost forward to the certification guide, and the supplier-is-also-a-competitor problem forward to the closing guide on industry structure.

## Module 1 — What an integrator actually adds
The one-sentence definition the whole course elaborates: converting cells it did not make into a warranted system somebody will finance. What belongs to the neighbouring courses and must not be re-derived here. The seven stages, in the order a product comes into existence.

## Module 2 — A cell as a purchased component
Amp-hours as charge rather than energy, and why cell generations are named by that number. The bus-voltage window and how it fixes the series count; the energy target and how it fixes the parallel count. C-rate read as duration, and how a low-rate energy duty cascades into electrode design and cooling. Cycle life and calendar life as two clocks, either of which can end the asset first.

## Module 3 — The format ladder
The rough sequence from roughly 200 Ah through 280, 314 and the 550–700 Ah class to the 700–800 Ah class, and the parallel climb in energy per enclosure. Two consequences: the enclosure is largely a passenger in that climb, and an integrator that qualified a format two years ago is now beaten on density by someone no better at integration. Format churn as the largest source of obsolescence risk in the business.

## Module 4 — What a bigger cell buys and costs
Fewer parts, better material utilisation and fewer monitoring channels against a worse surface-to-volume ratio for heat, more energy behind one failure, coarser configuration granularity, immature supply and a construction change from winding to stacking. The pattern to carry: format progress moves cost out of the battery and risk into the thermal and safety layers.

## Module 5 — What qualification means
Seven layers — electrical characterisation, life testing, abuse testing, propagation behaviour in the intended geometry, mechanical and environmental, manufacturing audit, pilot build. Why the life-test extrapolation *is* the capacity guarantee, so an error there is an unfunded twenty-year obligation. Why qualification is of a line rather than a company, and why the duration cannot be compressed without changing the mechanism being measured.

## Module 6 — Why a second source is an engineering project
The cell as a structural, thermal and electrical datum. What a dimensional or terminal difference actually changes: module frame, busbar and welding, cooling contact, compression hardware, monitoring harness, the software's capacity model, the degradation model behind the warranty, and usually the full-scale fire test. The honest accounting of both sides, the structural awkwardness that the cell maker is also a systems competitor, and format standardisation as the industry's affordable middle ground.

## Module 7 — The shape of a cell contract
Six instruments — framework agreement, take-or-pay, capacity reservation and prepayment, indexed pricing, allocation priority, epidemic-failure terms — and what each costs the buyer. The symmetry: every instrument that secures supply increases commitment, and every one that preserves flexibility weakens the claim on output. Cell strategy read as a company's statement about its own demand.

## Pacing
Seven modules, roughly 25 minutes each. Modules 2 and 3 belong together and are the technical foundation the other six guides assume. Module 5 is the one to keep as a reference when reading any supplier's quality claims. Module 6 is the intellectual centre of this guide — if only one module is read, read that one, because the same argument reappears in the certification and structure guides in different clothes.
