# Intersect — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the co-located energy park** — generation, storage and a data centre behind one grid connection — what surplus interconnection service, behind-the-meter load and Texas co-location net metering each buy and forbid, how a park is contracted and financed, why a hyperscaler bought the developer instead of signing power purchase agreements, and where the model fails. Intersect is the worked example: bought by Google for USD 4.75 billion plus debt (USD 5.9 billion after adjustments), closed 10 March 2026, with its grid-tied fleet carved out to IPX Power.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Crusoe plan** teaches energy-first siting and the islanded gigawatt campus; the **xAI plan** teaches owning a data centre and building one in months; the **Google plan** teaches hourly matching and TPUs. This plan is about the fence line — the interconnection and the rules about who may use it. The **Aypa and Spearmint plans** are the contracted and merchant developer models this one completes.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** The grid connection is the scarce asset, and a park makes one connection serve three things. Every structure that permits sharing buys speed in one direction and gives something up in another. And a hyperscaler that buys the developer is buying time, control and the pipeline — and removing the developer's margin from its own power bill.

**One boundary, held throughout.** No data-centre megawatts, gas capacity or contract value is published for any park; the 840 MW Meitner load figure is New Project Media's reading of county filings, and no FERC, ERCOT or PUCT filing names Intersect. The financial facts are Alphabet's 10-Qs and the sellers' releases; the campus facts are county minutes, the June 2026 joint release and trade press. The brief's Wyoming campus does not exist in any source.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 (~25 min). Module 2 with the Baker Botts SB 6 note open (~40 min). Module 3 (~30 min). Module 4 with Alphabet's Q2 2026 10-Q acquisition note open (~30 min). Module 5 (~20 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — Three assets, one interconnection

**The single idea:** a park files for one connection and puts generation, storage and load behind it.

**The pieces.** Quantum: 640 MW of solar and 1.3 GWh of storage, generation operating June 2026, data centre under construction. Meitner: 'more than a gigawatt of wind, solar, and battery storage', on-site gas firming for 'a minority share', 840 MW of behind-the-meter load in three phases per filings. The founder's rule: cheap solar and wind cover roughly 60–70 per cent of hours at a good site; the battery shifts the site's own midday power; firming closes the rest; the grid takes exports 'when the system needs power'.

**Powered land.** Greenbelt described Quantum's deal as 'a 20-year off-take and powered-land lease' — a site priced on secured megawatts, not acres.

**Self-check:** the battery in a park chases the hourly ERCOT price. True or false? *(False — it serves the site's load first; its value is avoided grid purchases.)*

## Module 2 — What the structures buy and forbid

**The single idea:** three legal structures share a connection, and each has a price.

**Surplus interconnection service.** A federal rule letting a battery use a generator's unused connection without re-queuing; only the surplus, and not inside ERCOT. No Intersect filing found.

**Behind-the-meter load / private use network.** The load consumes the site's power before the meter; one large-load request; the arrangement must be registered and the grid operator's rules accepted for what crosses. Meitner's filings show this, with amended agreements with Cross Texas Transmission.

**Co-location net metering under SB 6.** Regulator-approved netting for loads of 75 MW or more: 60-day PUCT review, curtailment during firm load shed, shutoff equipment, a USD 100,000 minimum fee. The ownership-date exemption fell on 1 January 2025, before Google owned Intersect. First test case: a Crusoe–Google campus.

**Self-check:** ERCOT declares firm load shed. Can a park with 1 GW of its own generation keep its data centre running from it? *(Not as of right — SB 6 requires the load to accept curtailment and install shutoff equipment; the park's generation does not exempt it.)*

## Module 3 — How the park is contracted and financed

**The single idea:** before the sale a park needed contracts between a developer, a tenant and investors; after the sale most of them collapse into one company.

**Before.** Intersect owned by TPG Rise Climate, CAI, Greenbelt and a Google minority stake; Google as anchor tenant; project debt and tax equity in the classic shape (MUFG and Santander construction loans, HPS term debt, Morgan Stanley and U.S. Bancorp tax equity) with unusual revenue — a Radian hedge, CCA and corporate offtake at Oberon, RECs at Lumina, merchant batteries on Autobidder.

**After.** A consolidated variable interest entity funded by the parent; no Google-era project financing; the county's abatement conditions (air cooling, water) bind the same group; the old fleet and its contracts carved out to IPX Power, which closed USD 4.95 billion for Darden two months later.

**Self-check:** who is the tenant at Meitner after March 2026? *(The parent — no lease exists between affiliates.)*

## Module 4 — Why buy the developer

**The single idea:** four reasons are in the record — time, control, the pipeline, and the margin.

'Operate more nimbly in building new power generation in lockstep with new data center load' and 'come to market even faster' (Alphabet). County conditions negotiated for a tenant it 'could not share … just yet'. 'Multiple gigawatts of energy and data center projects' rather than one PPA. And the purchase-price allocation: USD 5,868 million total, USD 5,129 million of property and equipment, USD 1,214 million of debt assumed, USD 2,174 million of goodwill — the accountants' name for the team and the future business.

**Self-check:** what does goodwill of USD 2.2 billion say about what Google thought it was buying? *(A machine that makes parks, not a set of assets.)*

## Module 5 — Where it fails

**The single idea:** six failure points, four of them visible in the record.

Curtailment orders under SB 6. County conditions with claw-backs. The hours renewables cannot cover and the unpublished gas that covers them. Load that arrives after the generation — Quantum's solar ran while the data centre was being built. A parent that changes its plans and leaves a park sized for 840 MW of load. And opacity — no megawatts, no filings, nothing for an outside supplier or utility to analyse but county minutes.

**Self-check:** a supplier wants to size a bid for Meitner's gas firming. Where would it look? *(Nowhere public yet — the record has 'a minority share of the site's demand' and no capacity or vendor.)*
