# Invenergy — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the diversified private developer** — how one development machine (land, interconnection, permits, construction) is pointed at wind, solar, storage, gas and transmission in turn; why owning a merchant HVDC line changes what a developer can site; how a 38-gigawatt private company is owned by a pension fund, an infrastructure fund and its own managers; how it borrows at portfolio and project level; and where the model fails. Storage is one line in the capital budget here, not the subject.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Aypa plan** teaches the contracted storage platform as an asset a buyer prices; the **Spearmint plan** the merchant developer and the pre-COD market; the **Intersect plan** the co-located energy park and the fence line. The **GridStor plan** is the sponsor-created specialist and the **Available Power plan** the seller of small projects at notice-to-proceed. The **Classroom** owns tolling, ancillary stacking and duration-and-degradation. None of that is repeated.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A diversified developer converts permits and grid positions into whichever asset the buyers and the policy of the year will pay for. Economic ownership and control are separable — a pension fund can hold 52.4 per cent of the profits while the founders remain managing member. And a merchant transmission line is a way to create connection points instead of queueing for them, which is why a developer would carry fifteen years of certificates and a state attorney general's investigation to build one.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Invenergy publishes no accounts, no per-project storage list and no battery supplier. Every figure in this plan is a transaction, facility or contract stated by a party to it — Invenergy, Blackstone, CDPQ, a lender, a utility filing, a court, the Department of the Interior. The 'largest privately held' claim is corrected to second-largest by pipeline (Cleanview) and largest among non-utility developers on that measure.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 in one sitting (~30 min). Module 2 with the 2022 Blackstone release and the 2018 CDPQ release open (~35 min). Module 3 with I&M's April 2026 filing open (~25 min). Module 4 with the Grain Belt resources page open (~35 min). Module 5 (~25 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — One capital budget, five products

**The single idea:** the machine is the asset; the technology is the output. Wind and solar are sold as PPAs, virtual PPAs and environmental-attribute agreements, then held or farmed down (WEC Energy Group at Delilah I and Hardin III). Storage is APS- and SRP-contracted in Arizona with 2.4 GW of unoffered microsites. Gas is built and transferred to utilities. Transmission is merchant. Modules and operations are captured in-house.

**The tell.** The June 2026 offshore settlement (USD 765 million, the four lease prices refunded) was committed to gas plants in five states and geothermal. Capital moved from what Washington stopped permitting to what it will.

**Self-check:** a developer's boilerplate moves from '36 gigawatts developed' to '38' in a month. What is the number measuring? *(Cumulative projects developed, including ones sold or transferred; it is a résumé, not a balance sheet.)*

## Module 2 — Who owns a 38-gigawatt private company

**The single idea:** economic ownership and control are different things. CDPQ holds 52.4 per cent of Invenergy Renewables' economics since 2018; Blackstone put about USD 4 billion into the holding company in 2022–2023; 'CDPQ and Invenergy management remain majority owners'; management is managing member and runs the business. InfraBridge owns half the gas subsidiary separately.

**Why these owners.** A pension fund's liabilities are decades long, so it wants decades of cash flow and no exit. An infrastructure fund invests limited partners' money through a general partner that needs to show growth — hence the follow-on and the pipeline ranking's importance. Neither wants to run a developer, so the founders keep control.

**What it buys.** A USD 1.5 billion revolver from sixteen banks priced on the owners' names; project debt in the USD 1.1–1.3 billion range per portfolio; the standing to buy AEP's 1,365 MW for USD 1.5 billion with a USD 580 million credit transfer inside.

**Self-check:** Blackstone's USD 4 billion is 'the largest recorded equity investment in North American renewables'. Does that make Blackstone the controlling owner? *(No — it is a minority economic stake with management as managing member; the release says so.)*

## Module 3 — Build-and-transfer: selling a gas plant to a utility

**The single idea:** the developer builds; the utility buys the plant at commissioning under a CPCN and puts it in rate base; the developer is paid once and is out. Sycamore Riverside (918 MW) goes to Indiana Michigan Power in March 2029; two Wisconsin plants are filed for We Energies' data-centre load, with two more proposed in August 2026.

**Why now.** Utilities facing hyperscaler load cannot self-build fast enough; a permitted developer plant on the developer's own site (beside its solar, beside the transmission work) is the fastest capacity a commission can approve.

**Self-check:** when does the developer get paid, and who carries forty years of operation? *(At transfer on completion; the utility and its ratepayers.)*

## Module 4 — What owning transmission changes

**The single idea:** a merchant HVDC line creates grid connection points that the queue would never grant, and the developer decides what plugs into them. Grain Belt Express: 800 miles, 5,000 MW, USD 11 billion, Siemens Energy converters, USD 1.7 billion of Quanta and Kiewit awards, 39 Missouri municipal utilities under a FERC open-access tariff, four state certificates, an Illinois Supreme Court win, a terminated USD 4.9 billion DOE conditional commitment, a Missouri Attorney General's investigation, thousands of easements and eminent-domain petitions.

**The comparison.** Intersect's answer to the connection problem was one fence line; Invenergy's is a wire across four states. Both stop waiting in the queue.

**Self-check:** the DOE 'terminated' a USD 4.9 billion loan. Had any money been lent? *(No — a conditional commitment is withdrawn before closing; the line is now privately financed, and no financing has been announced.)*

## Module 5 — Where it fails

**The single idea:** each product carries its own political risk, and opacity hides which one is failing. The line needs USD 11 billion of private capital against a contested certificate; the gas plants earn nothing until a commission approves the price; the offshore refund came with a condition on where it is spent; storage cannot be sold because it has never been described; three owners with different clocks sit behind USD 1.5 billion of change-of-control clauses.

**Self-check:** a supplier wants to sell batteries to Invenergy. Who is the counterpart, and what does the record say about what they buy? *(The EVP of Renewable Engineering and Procurement, new on the leadership page in 2026; the record names no battery supplier for any project — the opening is real and the history is blank.)*
