# NiSource (NIPSCO) — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach the ring-fenced affiliate as a way of serving a data center — what a commission's declination of jurisdiction does and does not do, how a fixed 15-year capacity charge from a guaranteed counterparty finances a plant nobody reviewed, why the same package reads as "3 GW", "two 1,300 MW combined cycles" and "2.6 GW plus a 400 MW battery" in three documents, what a 2 x 2 x 1 combined cycle is and why its turbine order is the constraint, how an affiliate serves a second hyperscaler from plant it already owns, and how a 19.9 percent minority partner lets a utility carry a program a quarter the size of its plan — starting from high-school STEM. **How this plan relates to what you already have.** WEC keeps the data-center plant in rate base under a tariff; DTE keeps it in the utility under a special contract; PPL's Invitium builds it outside the utility group entirely. ## Module 1 — The ring-fence

**The single idea:** a commission can decline jurisdiction over an entity that serves one large customer under contract; a utility can then build for a data center in that entity with no certificate, no rate-base review and no cost recovery from retail customers.

1. **Public utility or not.** A state commission regulates companies that serve the public at large. An affiliate that sells to one customer under a negotiated contract can ask the commission to declare it is not a public utility for that purpose — a declination. Indiana's commission approved NIPSCO Generation LLC's petition in September 2025.
2. **The order.** In Cause 46362 (June 17, 2026) the commission went further: it declined certificate jurisdiction over GenCo's two 1,300 MW combined-cycle units and its 400 MW battery, found the construction "reasonable and necessary" solely for compliance with the Amazon settlement, and barred cost recovery from non-megaload ratepayers without independent review. The cost is confidential.
3. **What the fence is made of.** A separate entity (Generation Holdings II owns GenCo); separate equity (Blackstone Infrastructure's 19.9 percent for $1.5 billion); a rule that retail customers pay nothing for it; and the company's own statement that the data center funds the plant "even if the customer leaves before contract completion".
4. **What it means for a seller.** The affiliate buys the plant, not the utility — and an affiliate the commission does not regulate does not file its equipment choices. The turbine OEM and the battery supplier are undisclosed in every document read.

**Self-check:** name the four vehicles in this corpus for data-center generation and who reviews the cost in each. (Bespoke-resource tariff — the commission; special contract — the commission; ring-fenced affiliate — nobody for the build; unregulated joint venture — nobody.)

## Module 2 — The contract and the credit

**The single idea:** a fixed capacity charge for 15 years from a guaranteed counterparty finances the plant; a bill credit to everyone else is the political licence.

1. **The contract.** Amazon Data Services, September 2025: up to 3 GW of new generation; deliveries from January 1, 2027 rising to 2.4 GW by the end of 2032; a term ending 15 years after the first data center is energized; from January 2027 a fixed capacity charge plus pass-throughs "structured to provide us with a return of our invested capital over the fifteen-year term"; Amazon.com's guarantee. Take-or-pay against an investment-grade parent — the most financeable shape in the corpus.
2. **The credit.** About $1 billion over 15 years when Amazon signed (about $7 a month on a residential bill); about $1.25 billion with Alphabet added; "approximately $1.4 billion of bill reductions" by August 2026; a $17 million community fund. The arithmetic is that a plant paid for by one customer produces excess capacity and cost sharing that flow back to the others.
3. **The framing.** Kentucky's and Arizona's tariffs exist so that a data center costs the ratepayer nothing; NiSource markets its contracts as making the ratepayer money. Same take-or-pay mechanics, opposite politics.

**Self-check:** the initial term is 15 years from which date? (The initial energization of Amazon's first data center — not signing, not first delivery.)

## Module 3 — The package: 2 x 2 x 1, the battery and the EPC

**The single idea:** three documents describe one build in three vocabularies, and reconciling them tells you what is actually being bought.

1. **The reconciliation.** Quanta: "approximately 3 gigawatts" of generation and infrastructure. Zachry: "two nominal 1,300-megawatt gas-fired combined cycle units in a 2 x 2 x 1 configuration" near Wheatfield. The commission: two 1,300 MW combined-cycle units at Schahfer and one 400 MW battery at Mitchell. So: 2,600 MW of gas plus 400 MW of storage — and a dossier elsewhere in the corpus that read the 3 GW as all combined cycle was corrected on that basis.
2. **The configuration.** A 2 x 2 x 1 combined cycle is two gas turbines, two heat-recovery steam generators and one steam turbine: the exhaust of both gas turbines boils water for a single steam turbine, lifting efficiency from the mid-30s percent of a simple-cycle unit to the high-50s. Two blocks of 1,300 MW nominal is four gas turbines and two steam turbines — a large order in a decade when turbine slots are the binding constraint, with no OEM named.
3. **The EPC.** Quanta and Zachry were selected on October 30, 2025, before the commission's order; a 50/50 joint venture delivers the combined cycles, Zachry leading engineering, procurement, project management and commissioning, Quanta the craft workforce. Execution from 2026; full power by 2032. The seller's door for the turbines is the joint venture.
4. **The battery.** 400 MW for four hours — 1,600 MWh — on the retired Mitchell coal site in Gary, due by January 1, 2027: the date Amazon's deliveries begin and four years before the gas reaches full power. A brownfield coal site brings an interconnection, a switchyard and permits; the battery serves the first megawatts while the gas is built.

**Self-check:** what serves Amazon on January 1, 2027? (The 400 MW battery, market purchases and the existing system — the combined cycles are not at full power until 2032.)

## Module 4 — The second customer and the fleet

**The single idea:** an affiliate can also contract plant it already owns — and its first program is three-quarters the size of the utility's whole fleet.

1. **Alphabet.** April 16, 2026: an Alphabet subsidiary served from about 340 MW of GenCo-owned generation plus up to 175 MW of seasonal purchases, service from summer 2026, full capacity by 2030, commission approval in July. No new plant. The pipeline is not only new build.
2. **The ladder.** About 4 GW signed (Amazon and Alphabet); about 3 GW in active negotiation; about 2 GW "developing"; up to 9 GW that would take generation in the NIPSCO area from 5.2 GW in 2026 to as much as 9 GW. The July 2026 amendment adding 400 MW to Amazon's contracted load is served within the 3 GW build.
3. **The fleet behind it.** NIPSCO owns about 4 GW: Schahfer coal (722 MW), held open by a federal 202(c) emergency order through March 23, 2026 after MISO warned of an energy emergency; Michigan City coal (455 MW), to retire by the end of 2028; the Sugar Creek combined cycle (665 MW); gas peakers; and a solar-and-storage fleet including Fairbanks Solar, bought from Invenergy under a build-transfer agreement. The coal was held open on the site where the affiliate's combined cycles go.

**Self-check:** a 202(c) order arrives for Michigan City in 2028. What does it do to the plan? (Keeps the coal running past its retirement date with cost recovery — and moves the replacement schedule the new gas was meant to serve.)

## Module 5 — The partner, the map and where it fails

**The single idea:** a 19.9 percent partner at the affiliate level is how a 14–16 percent FFO-to-debt utility carries a $7.6 billion program and guides 9–10 percent growth.

1. **Why 19.9, twice.** Below 20 percent a buyer holds a minority interest that does not change control or trigger change-of-control approvals. NiSource sold Blackstone Infrastructure 19.9 percent of NIPSCO in January 2024 ($2.16 billion plus $250 million) and 19.9 percent of GenCo's parent in October 2025 ($1.5 billion). The same sponsor holds 49 percent of PPL's Invitium — the corpus's pair.
2. **The plan.** $28.6 billion for 2026–2030: $21.0 billion base, $7.6 billion data center; gas hardening 38 percent, GenCo 25 percent; funded 40–45 percent debt, 45–50 percent cash from operations, 5–10 percent parent equity ($400–600 million a year), 3–5 percent minority interest. GenCo's earnings contribution rises from $0.01–0.02 a share in 2026 to $0.40–0.60 in 2032–2033 — the reason the 2026–2033 growth rate is 9–10 percent against 6–8 percent for the base plan.
3. **Who buys what.** The combined cycles: the Quanta–Zachry joint venture. The battery: GenCo. The Alphabet ramp to 2030: GenCo's existing portfolio. The next build: sized by the next signed customer. The retail fleet's coal replacement: NIPSCO under its IRP, including build-transfer from developers. The wires to the campuses: NIPSCO.
4. **Where it fails.** The fence is only as strong as the contract — no certificate, no prudence review, one home for the cost. Four turbines with no named OEM against a 2032 date. A credit story whose numerator ($1.4 billion over 15 years) is small against the program. Coal held open by federal order on the affiliate's site. And a second-quarter 2026 miss on storms and union-negotiation costs whose agreements expire between March 2026 and June 2027.

**Self-check:** a turbine seller wants the Schahfer order. Who is the account, and what is not yet decided? (The Quanta–Zachry EPC joint venture, with Zachry leading procurement; the OEM.)
