# Nscale — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the anchored neocloud as a financing structure** — how a company that reported about USD 33 million of revenue for 2025 comes to carry a reported USD 51 billion contracted backlog, about USD 6.1 billion of debt facilities and a USD 14.6 billion valuation, and what each of those numbers does and does not mean. The plan works through the vocabulary of not-yet (ambition, memorandum, letter of intent, capacity agreement, signed contract, drawn loan, energised hall), the anatomy of what an anchor such as Microsoft actually signs, the sequence by which a signature becomes collateral (site special-purpose vehicle, delayed-draw term loan secured on GPUs, project financing, an investment-grade rating on the loan while the parent has none), why the Nordics offer cheap hydro and scarce transmission, what a government is buying when it buys sovereign compute, and what happens when the anchor changes hands — which at Monarch, Narvik and Cobalt Park it did three times in a year. Nscale is the worked example because Aker ASA, a listed shareholder, reports the contracts in its quarterly filings and because Statnett, the UK planning system and the ratings on the Texas and North Carolina loans put regulators and lenders on the record.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **CoreWeave, Nebius, Lambda, Crusoe and IREN guides** own the neocloud as an operator — GPU-backed debt, take-or-pay, goodput, revenue against ARR against backlog, contracted against connected power, the service ladder, energy-first siting, the miner-to-AI pivot and investment-grade GPU financing. The **Microsoft guide** teaches "buying power that doesn't exist yet" from the anchor's side. The **Fluidstack plan** teaches the tenant-of-record chain — the intermediary that signs a landlord's lease on a guarantor's credit. This plan repeats none of it: it teaches the neocloud that owns or develops its own sites, whose contracts are stated in GPU counts and monthly capacity fees rather than in rent, and whose lenders lend to a site company against a single customer's signature. Where a definition already exists in those guides it is referenced, not re-taught.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** An announcement is a claim on a customer's intent; only a signed contract at a site company is collateral. A loan can be investment grade when the company is not, because the loan is secured on one anchor's promise to pay a monthly fee for a hall that exists. And a single-anchor site is a single point of failure until that contract is signed and that loan is rated — the anchor can leave before the building exists.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Nscale is private. Its revenue figures (about USD 33 million for 2025, about USD 100 million in the second quarter of 2026) and its backlog (USD 51 billion) are press-reported; the Anthropic Monarch contract — reported at about USD 45 billion over six years for 460 MW — has not been confirmed by either party, and Aker's disclosure did not name the customer. Microsoft's contracts are stated in GPUs, not dollars. The plan uses the company's own releases, Aker's quarterly reports, the ratings announcements, Statnett's and the UK planning record, and says so where the record stops: the dollar value of any Microsoft contract, the terms of the Figure commitment beyond its USD 3.5 billion headline, the residual value assumed for the Vera Rubin fleet.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 in one sitting (~30 min). Module 2 with Aker's latest quarterly report open (~40 min). Module 3 (~40 min) — it is the one that pays. Module 4 (~30 min). Module 5 (~25 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — The vocabulary of not-yet: from ambition to signed capacity

**The single idea:** every headline sits on one rung of a ladder, and only the top rung is money.

**The rungs.** An ambition ("a gigawatt by 2030") binds nobody. A memorandum of understanding records that two parties intend to talk. A letter of intent names a site, a size and a customer and is still not a contract — Monarch's 1.35 GW with Microsoft was an LOI, and it was withdrawn. A capacity agreement fixes GPUs, site, term and price and is what a lender reads. A signed contract at the site company is collateral. A drawn loan is cash. An energised hall is revenue — and only that last rung appears on the income statement.

**Nscale's ladder, read once.** Running: Glomfjord (30 MW hydro, the Arkon Energy site the company launched from) and the first European halls. Contracted: about 200,000 GB300 GPUs across Ward County, Sines, Loughton and Narvik, plus 96,000 Vera Rubin for Norway and Portugal, all Microsoft; the Figure commitment of USD 3.5 billion. Reported: Anthropic at Monarch. Imagined: the 2 GW Monarch build-out and the rest of the "AI-native hyperscaler" map.

**Contracts stated in chips.** When the customer's contract names 52,000 GB300 and the roadmap moves, the contract becomes 30,000 Rubin at Narvik. The dollar value may or may not have moved; the GPU count is the wrong thing to hold constant.

**Self-check:** a release says a site is "contracted" to a hyperscaler. Which rung is that? *(Unknown from the word alone — ask whether there is a signed capacity agreement at the site company, and whether a lender has lent against it.)*

## Module 2 — Anatomy of an anchor contract: what Microsoft actually signs

**The single idea:** the anchor buys delivered GPU-hours at a monthly fee, against milestones, from a company it does not have to keep.

**The terms that matter.** A GPU count and generation; a site; a term (five to six years, the useful life the lender will assume); a monthly capacity fee that starts when the racks are accepted, not when the contract is signed; delivery milestones with the anchor's right to walk if they are missed; and, usually, no exclusivity — the anchor signs the same paper with three other neoclouds. What is absent is as important: no equity, no guarantee of the neocloud's debt, no obligation to renew.

**Why the anchor does it.** The anchor's own queue for power and halls is years long; a neocloud that already holds an energised miner's site or a Nordic hydro allocation is the fastest megawatt available, and a monthly fee is an operating expense the anchor can stop. The Microsoft guide teaches the anchor's book of such commitments; here the point is the asymmetry — the anchor's signature is the neocloud's entire collateral and one line in the anchor's capex plan.

**Reading the Figure commitment.** USD 3.5 billion from a customer that is neither a hyperscaler nor a frontier lab is the first sign of a second kind of anchor — and a first test of whether a lender will rate a loan against a robotics company's promise the way it rated one against Microsoft's.

**Self-check:** the anchor's contract is for 200,000 GPUs and the neocloud has raised USD 3.7 billion of equity. Who is financing the other 90 percent of the build? *(Lenders, at the site companies — Module 3.)*

## Module 3 — From signature to collateral: the financing sequence of an anchored build

**The single idea:** the signature moves down to a site company, the loan is made there, and the rating attaches to the loan — not to Nscale.

**The sequence.** Equity at the parent (Series A USD 155 million, Series B USD 1.1 billion led by Aker, a USD 433 million SAFE, Series C USD 2 billion at USD 14.6 billion led by Aker and 8090 Industries). A site special-purpose vehicle per campus holds the land, the power reservation and the customer contract. A delayed-draw term loan — the February 2026 facility "secured by GPU infrastructure" against "multiple executed customer contracts" — funds the GPUs as they are delivered, drawn against milestones. Project financing at the Texas and North Carolina sites, where the August 2026 loans "received investment-grade ratings with stable outlooks". A USD 900 million revolving credit facility with twelve banks for working capital. And, reported, an IPO of up to USD 3 billion.

**Why the loan is investment grade and the company is not.** The rating agency looks at one hall, one anchor, one fee stream and the GPUs as collateral; the anchor is investment grade and the cash flow is contracted for the loan's life. The parent is a two-year-old company with about USD 100 million of quarterly revenue and USD 6.1 billion of facilities — the Leverage Shares note's phrase is that contracted demand is "not necessarily guaranteed demand". Both are true at once; they are ratings of different things.

**What can break it.** A milestone missed before the loan is fully drawn; an anchor that exercises its walk right; a GPU generation that is worth less as collateral than the model assumed; a hall that is delivered but not accepted. Every loan in the stack is drawn against milestones the company has not yet hit.

**Self-check:** a lender is offered a term loan at the parent and a project loan at a site company with the same anchor. Which is rated higher, and why? *(The site loan — its only exposures are one hall and one anchor; the parent loan is exposed to every site, every anchor change and the equity story.)*

## Module 4 — Power-first siting: hydro is cheap, transmission is scarce, gas is certain

**The single idea:** the binding constraint at every European site is the wire, and the capital went where the wire is not needed.

**The Nordics.** Glomfjord and Narvik sit on hydro that is cheap to run and, until recently, easy to reserve; but Statnett halted new large-load connections in May 2026 and said "phase 2 is more uncertain" without touching the existing 230 MW reservation. A reservation is a promise by a regulated body with its own duties; it is not a substation. Loughton, in an AI growth zone, slipped from "operational Q4 2026" to 2027 through a planning fight settled by ministerial letter; OpenAI paused Stargate UK citing "the cost of energy".

**What sovereign compute buys.** A government buying "sovereign" capacity is buying jurisdiction over the data and the hardware, a domestic operator, a domestic grid and, usually, a domestic power price; it is not buying cheaper compute. The growth-zone designation buys planning priority and a seat at the transmission queue, which is worth exactly as much as the queue moves.

**Monarch and Ward County.** The 2,250-acre West Virginia campus bought with its 2 GW Caterpillar gas order runs "independently from the local grid" — an islanded microgrid that trades the interconnection queue for fuel and air permits. Ward County sits on an ERCOT campus a miner had already energised. Both answer the European problem by not having it; both carry costs (fuel, permits, heat) the Crusoe and xAI guides teach and this plan only names.

**Self-check:** a Nordic site has a 230 MW reservation and a customer for 500 MW. What does the second phase depend on? *(The transmission operator's next connection round — not the customer, the capital or the chips.)*

## Module 5 — When the anchor changes hands: the Monarch case, and how the chain is bought

**The single idea:** a single-anchor site is a single point of failure until the contract is signed and the loan is rated.

**Three changes in a year.** OpenAI was the announced offtaker at Narvik (July 2025) and Cobalt Park (September 2025); by April 2026 Aker records the Norway LOI "replaced" by Microsoft, and OpenAI paused the UK. Microsoft signed the 1.35 GW Monarch LOI in March 2026 and, per Semafor, "walked away" over the summer; Anthropic took 460 MW on reported terms of about USD 45 billion over six years. Each time the site survived, because the site is the asset and the anchor is a customer of it — but each time the loan that would have been rated against the first anchor had to wait for the second.

**Reading the reported Anthropic terms.** USD 45 billion over six years for 460 MW is about USD 16.3 million per MW-year, roughly 1.9 times an estimated USD 24 billion build cost — a price for delivery certainty rather than a landlord's margin, if the figures are right. Neither party has confirmed them; the residual value of the Vera Rubin fleet in 2033 is the largest open variable in the economics.

**How the chain is bought.** The anchor chooses the neocloud for the earliest energised megawatt and the right to walk; the lender chooses the site for the anchor's name; the equity investor (Aker, 8090, the sovereign funds) chooses the parent for the map. The neocloud's product to each is the same signature, sold three times: as capacity to the anchor, as collateral to the lender, as backlog to the investor.

**Self-check:** the announced anchor at a site is replaced by a larger one before the loan is drawn. Is the site better or worse off? *(Better, if the new contract is signed at the site company and the loan is re-rated against it; worse for every month the old LOI was counted as backlog and was not.)*
