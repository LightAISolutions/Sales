# Pattern Energy — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** Pattern Energy (private; CPP Investments' majority-owned renewables and transmission platform with APG/ABP, Australian Retirement Trust and management — about 12 GW of wind, solar and storage across the US, Canada and Mexico, and owner-operator of SunZia: 3,650 MW of New Mexico wind on a 550-mile, 3,000 MW VSC HVDC line to Arizona) · **Written:** 2026-09-12 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no power-systems or finance background.

**What this teaches.** How a wind farm produces and what its three capacity figures mean; why a remote plant needs its own wire and how a voltage-source HVDC line enters a grid operator's market as a subscriber line; the capital stack that let USD 11 billion be borrowed against wind; the offtake menu and who buys from a plant this size; what an independent power producer is and what pension ownership, a take-private, a secondary sale and share-based consolidation do to it; SunZia's eighteen-year permit and court history; why the same developer's Texas-to-Southeast line has not moved; the 2026 policy clock; and the industry map.

**Why this gap.** The corpus's IPP guides teach storage owners and merchant generators; the transmission material is scattered. This is the first guide on a developer that owns its own long-haul HVDC line and the generation behind it, and the first on a pension-owned platform whose ownership moved twice in twelve months.

**Its place in the segment set.** Adjacent on grid-equipment (the converter and turbine buyer), utilities (the merchant transmission owner utilities schedule and buy from) and storage-developers-and-IPPs (an IPP at scale with a small storage book). Read beside the Grid United guide, which teaches the seam-crossing developer without generation.

## Module 1 — What a wind farm produces
The cube law; rated speed and cut-in; capacity factor as the site's number; nameplate at order versus net summer capacity versus owned capacity; gen-ties and collector systems; curtailment when the path is full.

## Module 2 — Moving it
Why 550 miles is DC; bipole and converter stations; voltage-source converters at a weak landing point; the WECC path rating; California's Subscriber PTO model and what it solved.

## Module 3 — The capital stack
Non-recourse construction and term debt; the tax-equity term loan; structured equity (HASI); preferred equity (Nuveen); sponsor equity; who takes which risk and in what order they are paid.

## Module 4 — Selling the power
Utility, aggregator, corporate and merchant offtake; term, credit and volume; what a lender will size debt against; SunZia's disclosed buyers.

## Module 5 — What an IPP owned by a pension fund is
Allowed return versus contract spread; the yieldco era and the take-private; the secondary sale of a founder's stake; share consideration and consolidation inside one owner; what a private company does and does not disclose.

## Module 6 — Permits and courts
BLM decisions and rights-of-way; state certificates; the NHPA case and what 'final agency action' means; the WECC rating and CAISO application as grid approvals; energisation, testing and commercial operation as three different dates.

## Module 7 — SunZia against Southern Spirit
Seam crossing versus in-grid line; incumbent at the terminus; captive generation; state expropriation law; federal jurisdiction over a DC tie to ERCOT; anchor money; why one is operating and one has slipped.

## Module 8 — The policy clock and the industry map
Tax-credit deadlines, safe harbor, foreign-entity rules, Section 232 and the federal permitting posture; buyers, competitors (Invenergy, NextEra, the European arms, Grid United) and why owning the wire is the differentiator.

## Pacing
Eight modules of roughly 30 minutes. Modules 1–2 are the technical core. Modules 3–4 are the finance core and should be read together. Module 5 is self-contained and useful for any conversation about private-company ownership. Modules 6–8 read in one sitting.

## Sources for the technology and industry content
Grounded in the company's dossier and the sources registered there — its own sites and reports, CPP Investments' releases, HASI's and Quanta's filings, WECC and CAISO records, the Louisiana and Mississippi commission dockets, the Ninth Circuit opinion, EIA and the trade press — plus the corpus's existing storage-developer and grid-equipment guides. No company trivia is drilled.
