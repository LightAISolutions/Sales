# PPL Corporation — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach why one holding company answers the data-center question three different ways — a wires-only utility in a retail-choice state that prices the load through a large-load tariff and a transmission rider; a vertically integrated pair that builds regulated combined cycles against signed reimbursement agreements; and an unregulated joint venture that reserves turbines before it has customers — and, along the way, how to read a pipeline ladder rung by rung, why transmission cost allocation lives at a different regulator from distribution, what a state commission is saying when it approves the plants and denies the recovery mechanisms, and where a battery could enter in each of the three places — starting from high-school STEM. PPL is the first plan where the same company uses several of these at once because its utilities sit in different regulatory regimes — so the lesson is less "what is the mechanism" than "which mechanism applies where, and why the law decides it".

## Module 1 — Three answers in one company

**The single idea:** a wires company in a restructured state cannot build a plant, an integrated utility can, and a joint venture outside both regimes can build for a hyperscaler — and PPL owns one of each.

1. **Restructuring.** In a retail-choice state the distribution utility owns the wires and provides default service — supply bought through commission-approved auctions and passed through — but owns no generation. A plant in its rate base would be paid for by every customer, including those buying power from a competitor: the cross-subsidy the market was designed to prevent. PPL Electric (about 1.5 million customers) is such a company. Its answer to a data center is a price.
2. **Integration.** LG&E and KU own about 7,264 MW of generation under the Kentucky commission and can add plant with a certificate of public convenience and necessity. Their answer is to build — two 645 MW combined cycles approved in October 2025.
3. **The venture.** Invitium Energy, 51 percent PPL and 49 percent Blackstone Infrastructure, builds combined-cycle plants for hyperscalers under long-term energy services agreements — outside PPL Electric, outside any rate base, outside any commission's review. Its answer is a contract with the customer directly. The chief executive's line: "bilateral contracting will likely be the predominant path for getting new generation development in PJM."
4. **Three buyers.** PPL Electric buys transformers, switchgear, lines and substations. LG&E and KU buy turbines, a battery, an SCR and, if a hyperscaler pays, a pumped-storage plant. Invitium buys combined-cycle turbines — and perhaps batteries the hyperscaler prefers to own itself.

**Self-check:** a data center asks PPL Electric to build it a gas plant. What is the answer, and where does the plant get built instead? (It cannot — restructuring removed generation from its rate base; the customer contracts with a generator, Invitium or a merchant, and PPL Electric delivers.)

## Module 2 — The Pennsylvania tariff and the transmission rider

**The single idea:** the June 2026 tariff fixes what a data center pays for the wires; the transmission rider proposed in September 2026 would do the same at the federal level, where the network rate otherwise spreads the cost to everyone.

1. **The terms.** For loads of 50 MW or more (75 MW aggregate within ten miles at 69 kV or above): an initial term of at least ten years, a ramp of up to five years, guaranteed payments of at least 80 percent of the reserved capacity, exit fees, security equal to the cost of the upgrades, and an interruptible option — inside the PUC's May 2026 model framework (thresholds, ramping, minimum terms, exit provisions, deposits and collateral, a six-month study, a public queue, self-construction).
2. **Two kinds of wires.** Distribution is priced by the state commission, and the tariff assigns its upgrades to the customer. Transmission is priced under a FERC formula rate that spreads the network's cost across every load in the zone by its share of the peak — so a substation built for one campus is, by default, paid by everyone. The Customer Protection Transmission Rider (filed September 14, 2026, for Q1 2028) would replace the Transmission Service Charge and assign certain network-upgrade costs to the LP-6 large-load class.
3. **The ladder.** Advanced stages — 31.8 GW at mid-2026: projects with signed developer agreements whose costs are reimbursable if they do not proceed. Signed electric service agreements — more than 11 GW: contracts on the tariff terms. Under construction — more than 6.5 GW. In service — two data centers ramping to about 2 GW by 2031. The May 2026 ramp: 0.6 GW of new load in 2026 to 20.7 GW in 2030. Hold the company to the lower rungs.
4. **The arithmetic.** If the 31.8 GW is realized, the transmission component of a residential bill falls by about $25 a month over time — a larger load sharing a network whose growth the large-load class carries. That is the wires company's version of the customer credit.

**Self-check:** a 60 MW customer reserves 60 MW and draws 40 MW in year three of a five-year ramp. What does it pay for, and for how long? (At least 48 MW — 80 percent of reserved capacity — for at least ten years.)

## Module 3 — The Kentucky build and the regulator's warning

**The single idea:** Kentucky's commission approved the plants and denied the pre-collection — build for the forecast, but do not charge for it early, and do not build storage for unsigned load.

1. **The application.** February 2025, $3.7 billion: Brown 12 (645 MW, 2030) and Mill Creek 6 (645 MW, 2031) combined cycles, a 400 MW / 1,600 MWh battery at Cane Run (2028), a selective catalytic reduction retrofit on Ghent 2 — against a forecast of 32,808 GWh growing to 48,129 GWh by 2032 and a peak of 6,230 MW growing to 8,034 MW, more than 6,000 MW of it data-center related.
2. **The order.** October 2025: the two gas units and the SCR approved at $2.798 billion including transmission; the battery withdrawn as moot with the right to refile; a Mill Creek 6 recovery mechanism, a retirement adjustment clause and advance recovery of notice expenses all denied; a monitoring case opened; the load probabilities called "troubling" and unsupported — yet a "reasonable basis" that economic-development load is coming.
3. **What was real enough.** Developer commitments: a 400 MW PowerHouse–Poe campus in Louisville (130 MW by October 2026) and, by mid-2026, 1.3 GW of signed reimbursement agreements against a 13.7 GW pipeline. The trigger for the next certificate — a third combined cycle, the deferred 400 MW of batteries, a 266 MW eight-hour pumped-storage plant — is "conversion from developer to hyperscaler contract".
4. **The tariff.** The Extremely High Load Factor rate: a 15-year minimum term, a minimum bill of 80 percent of forecast consumption, collateral up to $100 million. A 525 MW data center would owe about $1.4 billion of demand charges over 15 years — the take-or-pay arithmetic that finances the plant. The legislature's no-cross-subsidy bill died without a Senate vote; the tariff stands. The recovery question moved to the rate case: the commission rejected the sharing mechanism the August 2028 stay-out depended on, and a rehearing was sought in March 2026.

**Self-check:** a storage seller wants a Kentucky order this year. Which door? (The July 2026 RFP — 50–400 MW, 200–1,600 MWh, PPA, asset purchase or build-transfer — not a CPCN, which waits on a hyperscaler contract.)

## Module 4 — The joint venture: turbines before customers

**The single idea:** in a market where turbine delivery slots are the binding constraint, the reservation is the scarce asset, and Invitium bought it before it had a customer.

1. **The structure.** Formed July 2025; 51/49 with Blackstone Infrastructure; combined-cycle gas under long-term energy services agreements with "regulated-like risk profiles"; excludes PPL Electric; construction financing expected off PPL's balance sheet.
2. **The position.** By August 2026: more than 5 GW of turbine reservations (supplier unnamed); more than 5 GW accepted into PJM's interconnection queue; sites for 8–14 GW; $12.5–15 billion of potential investment at the venture level through 2032, none of it in PPL's $23 billion plan; first commercial agreements promised by year-end 2026; first turbines running as early as 2031–2032.
3. **Where the batteries go.** The chief executive expects batteries and other short-lead-time resources to earn first, in 2029 or 2030, calls them "the easiest for the hyperscalers to embed in their designs", and says "some of that could be owned directly by the hyperscalers". If so, the storage purchase order sits with the customer.
4. **The pair.** Blackstone Infrastructure is also NiSource's 19.9 percent partner in NIPSCO and in its data-center generation affiliate — the corpus's other utility-affiliated vehicle, ring-fenced inside the utility rather than placed outside it.

**Self-check:** what turns Invitium's $12.5–15 billion from an option into a plan? (The first commercial agreement — the customer contract the turbines were reserved for.)

## Module 5 — Storage in three places, and where it fails

**The single idea:** a battery can reach PPL through Kentucky rate base, a Kentucky RFP or a hyperscaler's own design, and each route puts the purchase order with a different party.

1. **The three routes.** Rate base: E.W. Brown (125 MW / 500 MWh, in service early 2027) and, if refiled, Cane Run — the utility signs the supply agreement, a certificate fixes size and date. The RFP: 50–400 MW, bids due September 3, 2026, any commercial date, the developer picks the OEM. The venture: batteries embedded in the hyperscaler's design, possibly hyperscaler-owned, nothing disclosed.
2. **Where it fails.** The top of the Pennsylvania ladder is a developer's signature, not a hyperscaler's. Kentucky built for a forecast its regulator called "troubling"; if the campuses do not convert, two plants serve 1.3 GW of reimbursement agreements. Invitium has turbines and no customer. And the risk is recovery timing rather than plant approval: a failed Kentucky rehearing means another rate case within the year, Rhode Island's 9 percent return stands, and the Pennsylvania transmission rider is a 2028 proposal.

**Self-check:** name the party that buys the battery in each of the three routes. (The utility; the RFP bidder — a developer; the hyperscaler or the venture.)
