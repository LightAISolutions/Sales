# Prime Data Centers — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **recapitalising a build-to-suit pipeline** — how a founder-owned developer is funded five times without giving up control (a strategic partner, two institutional investors, a joint-control clearance, a joint-venture cheque, preferred equity at the parent), why "majority founder-owned" and "jointly controlled" are both true, how project debt is priced on a neocloud lease (USD 900 million of 7.5% notes on a 15-year CoreWeave lease against Fleet's 5.9% on a AA- tenant) and why a Nordic bond for the unleased pipeline failed the same summer, what phantom equity is and how to read a valuation stated only by plaintiffs, and how utility pacing — Oncor's push of 2 GW to 2027, SRP's deposit regime, ComEd's queue — sets delivery dates a developer's capital cannot buy. Prime Data Centers is the worked example because its record is unusually legible for a private company: the European Commission's notice of who controls it, Bloomberg's account of the bond it priced and the bond it shelved, a plaintiffs' complaint that states the valuation, and location pages that list every building's status.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Compass** plan teaches how a pension fund and an infrastructure fund own a landlord and how a building is paid for three times; the **EdgeCore** plan teaches the lease and the limited-recourse loan; the **PowerHouse** plan teaches the GP fund with 10% of the equity; the **Tract** plan (same session) teaches project bonds on a AA- tenant and the intra-group sale; the **Fluidstack** plan teaches the neocloud's side of the credit chain. This plan is about the developer with no single sponsor and repeats none of them.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A founder who keeps control must raise equity in forms that do not vote him out, and each form has a price — a strategic partner's consent rights, joint control, preferred stock at the parent. Project debt is available building by building once a lease is signed and priced on the tenant's credit; corporate debt for a pipeline of unnamed tenants is not. And a founder-controlled cap table is paid for in phantom equity, whose value is whatever the company's valuation says — which is the dispute in the 2026 suits.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Prime publishes no accounts, no revenue, no round sizes; the live site returns 403 to automated fetches, so every company page was read from Wayback captures dated March–August 2026; the new Solutions pages, the Avondale groundbreaking post and the 2026 blog posts are not archived; DCD, Bloomberg originals and court dockets were unreadable. The bond terms come from Bloomberg via mirrors; the control structure from EUR-Lex; the lawsuit allegations from KERA and plaintiffs' counsel and are allegations only. Where the plan gives arithmetic (a 160-basis-point spread between two project bonds) it says so.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 with the EUR-Lex notice and the Grain release open (~30 min). Module 2 with the Investing.com and swissinfo Bloomberg mirrors (~30 min). Module 3 with the KERA article (~25 min). Module 4 with the Bisnow 2023 Oncor article and the Avondale release (~30 min). Module 5 with the location pages' status fields (~20 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — The recapitalisation ladder

**The single idea:** a developer that owns its buildings needs equity in proportion to its pipeline; a founder who wants control raises it in forms that do not vote him out, and each form has a price.

**The five rounds.** 2021 strategic partner (Macquarie Capital; "USD 5 billion over the next 10 years"). 2023–2025 institutional investors (Ares, Siemens Financial Services). 2025 joint control (EC Case M.11843: Ares "through contractual means" with Macquarie via WDCAH and Data Realty Group). July 2025 joint-venture equity (Snowhawk + Nuveen) during a reported USD 2 billion raise. April 2026 preferred equity at the parent (Grain into Data Realty Holding Corp.; "ability to provide additional capital over time").

**Ownership versus control.** "Majority founder-owned" is a share count; "joint control" is veto rights over strategic decisions — a budget, a sale, a new campus. Both are true. Compare the corpus: sponsor-owned (EdgeCore, Compass), one-fund GP (PowerHouse), negotiated middle (Prime).

**Preferred at the parent.** A fixed return ahead of common, convertible or redeemable on terms; placed in the holding company so the founder's stake funds the plan without diluting the operating company's other investors. The August 2026 suits name that parent.

**The missing rung.** Infralogic, May 2025: the USD 2 billion raise slipped because of "prolonged ongoing lease negotiations" — the capital would not come until the leases did.

**Exercise:** for each round, write what the investor received (economics, consent rights, security), what the founder gave up, and whether the operating company or the parent was the counterparty.

## Module 2 — Pricing a bond on a neocloud lease

**The single idea:** project bonds price on the lease counterparty's credit; a fully leased campus can still be expensively financed, and a pipeline with no lease cannot be bond-financed at all.

**Elk Grove.** Elk Grove Village Property LLC — "wholly-owned indirect subsidiary of Prime Data Centers LLC" — USD 900 million five-year senior notes at par, 7.5%, 2 June 2026, upsized from USD 850 million, Santander sole bookrunner; refinances USD 500 million and funds remaining construction; security: the three Chicago buildings and CoreWeave's 15-year lease (~USD 2.2 billion, two 7-year renewals).

**The spread.** Fleet's Nevada notes: 5.875% on a AA- tenant; Prime's: 7.5% on CoreWeave, a leveraged sub-investment-grade neocloud — about 160 basis points for the tenant's credit, in the same market and season. A credit tenant lease lets a landlord borrow near the tenant's cost of funds; a neocloud lease carries the neocloud's risk into the landlord's bond.

**The failed bond.** A Norwegian-law high-yield bond (fast, covenant-light) for the pipeline: USD 600 million cut to USD 500 million, shelved July 2026 "after investors balked at the proposed terms". Elk Grove had a building, a lease and a tenant; the Nordic bond had a roadmap. The market's rule: project debt building by building, corporate debt for the pipeline not at any price the company would pay.

**Exercise:** estimate the annual coupon cost of the Elk Grove notes against the lease's average annual rent, and state what refinancing at maturity (2031) depends on.

## Module 3 — Phantom equity and the price of control

**The single idea:** phantom equity is a cash promise equal to a share's value without the share; its value is whatever the company's valuation says, which is exactly what the 2026 suits contest.

**The instrument.** Units that pay on a sale or valuation event; common in founder-controlled developers because real equity dilutes control and complicates every investor round; its weakness is that the valuation is set by the party that pays.

**The suits.** Seven former employees (including the former CCO, VP HR and Head of Utilities and Incentives) in Dallas County, S.D.N.Y. and California against Prime, Data Realty Holdings, Laag and Pelz: fraud, misrepresentation, civil RICO; USD 185 million in Texas, "more than USD 400 million" total; alleged "two-tiered valuation system", coerced re-signing, "undisclosed property transactions absorbing co-owner interests", dilution via "unfunded paper commitments"; valuation "from about USD 140 million to more than USD 6 billion". No defence reported by September 2026; allegations, not findings.

**Two readings.** The USD 6 billion is the plaintiffs' number and the only valuation on the record; and the commercial team that signed Lambda and negotiated with Oncor is now adverse to the company.

**Exercise:** write the three questions a supplier should ask before contracting — which entity signs, what stands behind it, whether the building has a status field or a pipeline claim — and answer them for Elk Grove, Avondale and the parent.

## Module 4 — Utility pacing

**The single idea:** delivery dates belong to the utility; capital cannot shorten them, and the record of Prime's Texas and Arizona campuses is the record of the utility's clock.

**Texas.** November 2023: Oncor pushes "2 GW of capacity across five separate data center projects" from 2025 "into 2027" after more than a year of negotiation. DFW04's filed completion (January 2026) passes with no reported opening; Austin phase 1 shrinks from three buildings and USD 1.3 billion (2024) to two shells and USD 400 million (June 2026–September 2027); Caldwell County suspends abatements in August 2026.

**Arizona.** Avondale: 210 MW with a first building "Q3 2025" (2023) → 240 MW with a groundbreaking on buildings 1–3 on 21 May 2026, phase 1 of a ~250 MW SRP substation commissioned for 144 MW, no delivery date; SRP's programme (15-year contracts, 80% minimum takes, collateral deposits) cut 24 applicants to three after February 2026.

**Where it went faster.** Vernon: a municipal utility and a self-built 49.5 MVA substation — 33 MW opened November 2024, 100% leased. Chicago: a 275 MVA on-site substation and the first Illinois data-center tax exemption — operational and fully leased by 2025 despite ComEd's market-wide queue.

**Behind the meter.** UGI (May 2026): land sale for an on-site gas plant, >100,000 Dth/day within three to five years, UGI capex >USD 100 million; no county, MW or permit; opposition within a fortnight. John Bates hired in March as EVP for "grid-connected and behind-the-meter assets".

**Exercise:** tabulate each campus's first announced date, its utility instrument and its current status from the dossier, and compute the slip in months where both dates exist.

## Module 5 — Reading a founder-owned developer

**The single idea:** the company publishes no accounts, but five outside parties have published pieces of its balance sheet; the discipline is knowing which document answers which question.

**The map.** Control: EUR-Lex M.11843. A building's value to a lender: Bloomberg's bond reporting. The company's worth: the plaintiffs (and only them). What operates: the location pages' status fields (~151 MW operational, 141 MW leased; 222 MW in construction). Pipeline: boilerplate by date — "3+ GW / 22 locations" (2024 report), "4+ GW / 13 campuses" (site), "7 GW / 38 sites" (Grain release) — three figures in eighteen months. Why a financing failed: Bloomberg via swissinfo.

**Exercise:** for each of the six questions, name the best source, the answer it gives and the thing it cannot say; then list what a Q4 2026 refresh should look for first.

**Where it fails (for the whole plan):** the pipeline number is marketing; control and ownership are different sentences; project debt is not corporate capacity; the tenant sets the coupon; the commercial team is in court; dates belong to the utility.
