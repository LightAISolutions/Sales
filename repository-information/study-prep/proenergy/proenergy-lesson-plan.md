# ProEnergy — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** ProEnergy (private; aeroderivative gas-turbine packager, EPC, service provider and developer of fast-build power plants) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no thermodynamics or power-plant background.

**What this teaches.** The gas turbine as the other main answer to bridge power. Why an aircraft engine core becomes a fifty-megawatt generator; the Brayton cycle and why simple-cycle machines waste so much heat on purpose; how to read a heat rate without being misled by four separate quoting conventions; why turbine output collapses on the afternoon it is most needed and what the two standard recoveries cost; why starting fast is a design decision with a maintenance price; and why bridge plants are built by one kind of owner and end up owned by another.

**Why this gap.** The corpus had no thermodynamics at all. Heat rate is the single number by which every thermal machine in the ecosystem is judged and compared, it is quoted four incompatible ways, and nothing in the repo explained how to convert between them or what each convention conceals. The commercial half of the gap is equally real: the develop-build-sell-retain-service pattern is how most fast-build capacity actually changes hands, and it was undocumented.

**Its place in the bridge-power set.** Second of six. VoltaGrid establishes why bridge power exists and covers reciprocating machines; this guide covers turbines and owns thermal efficiency for the whole course. Emissions chemistry belongs to Mainspring, permitting and run hours to Enchanted Rock, the electrical interface to ON.energy, storage and hybrid plants to Prevalon. The regulated buyer at the end of this guide's last module is developed properly in the Key Capture Energy guide.

## Module 1 — An aircraft engine bolted to the ground
What an aeroderivative machine actually is: a turbofan core with the fan removed and a power turbine added behind it. Why everything that made the engine light, compact and quick to start in the air carries over to the ground. The trade stated in one number, and why availability of the machines themselves is decisive for bridge duty.

## Module 2 — Compress, burn, expand
The Brayton cycle in three steps, and the fact that a large share of the shaft work drives the compressor itself. Why efficiency rises with pressure ratio and firing temperature, and why turbine development is therefore a metallurgy and blade-cooling story. Simple cycle against combined cycle against combined heat and power — and why a plant whose job may last five years should not spend three of them building a steam cycle.

## Module 3 — Two kinds of gas turbine
Aeroderivative against heavy-duty frame: cycling tolerance, power density, simple-cycle efficiency and module-exchange maintenance against the lowest fuel cost per megawatt-hour at scale. Why the delivery-slot bullet is the one that actually decides bridge projects, and why refurbishing retired aviation cores is a real business rather than a curiosity.

## Module 4 — How to read a heat rate
Btu per kilowatt-hour and the 3,412 conversion. The four conventions that make quoted numbers incomparable: LHV against HHV, gross against net, and the load and ambient at which the figure was taken. A worked example carried through all three transformations, and the single question that cuts through them — *how much gas per unit of electricity the site actually uses*.

## Module 5 — Why output falls when it is needed
ISO rating conditions, and the turbine as fundamentally an air pump whose output depends on inhaled air mass. Temperature and altitude compounding. Inlet air cooling — evaporative against chilled — set against water injection, which adds mass directly and suppresses NOx at the same time. Why both trade water for megawatts in exactly the arid places where the derate is worst.

## Module 6 — Starts, cycles and availability
Thermal stress as the reason maintenance intervals are counted in equivalent operating hours. Why aeroderivatives tolerate cycling by inheritance rather than by addition. Start reliability as a property distinct from start speed, and more important for bridge duty. Module exchange against in-place rebuild, and why spare-core inventory is a strategic rather than logistical question.

## Module 7 — Who owns the plant when the bridge is over
Two phases with opposite risk appetites: development and construction, which earn a margin because they can fail; and long-term ownership of dispatchable capacity, which wants certainty and cheap capital. Why the natural transaction is build, prove, sell and retain the O&M. The service annuity, the crews kept employed between builds, and the operating record no brochure can substitute for. Why one company can be manufacturer, developer, IPP and service business without being unfocused — those are four stages of one asset's life.

## Pacing
Seven modules, roughly 30 minutes each. Modules 1–3 are one argument about the machine. Module 4 is the one to work through with a calculator rather than read — the conversions do not stick otherwise, and it is the module the rest of the corpus leans on hardest. Modules 5–6 are independent. Module 7 is commercial rather than technical and pairs naturally with the Key Capture Energy guide.
