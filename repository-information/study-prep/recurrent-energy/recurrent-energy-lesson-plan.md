# Recurrent Energy — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **how to read a developer's pipeline, and what a convertible preferred stake actually buys**. Specifically: what the four development stages mean and why 85% of a headline number can be a queue position; why the interconnection is the asset being traded; the economics of developing-and-selling against developing-and-holding; what a captive supply channel is worth and what it costs; and why "BlackRock bought 20%" is the least informative true sentence available about this company's ownership.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Clearway** plan (same session) teaches the buyer's side — what happens to a project after it is finished. This is the **seller's side**: how projects are originated, why most never exist, and what a developer's balance sheet looks like while it waits. The **Canadian Solar** dossier covers the manufacturing parent; the **RWE** plan covers what an interconnection is worth from an owner's perspective. Nothing in the library currently teaches preferred-equity structure, and Module 5 is the first pass at it.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A pipeline is an option portfolio, not an asset base, and the honest question is never "how big" but "how much is connected". Whether a developer holds or sells is a balance-sheet decision dressed as a strategic one. And preferred equity is a contract with five moving terms, of which the headline percentage is the least important.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Recurrent Energy is **not wholly owned**, and a great deal of published material still says it is — including Canadian Solar's own e-STORAGE project pages. BlackRock's climate infrastructure business holds Series A convertible preferred representing 20% of Recurrent Energy B.V. on an as-converted basis, closed in two tranches in 2024. Also stale in most sources: **Ismael Guerrero has not been chief executive since 6 July 2026** (Dylan Marx succeeded him), and **Shawn Qu has not run the parent since 14 May 2026** (Colin Parkin, formerly president of e-STORAGE, did).

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 with the Q2 2026 6-K pipeline tables open (~30 min). Module 2 with an ISO interconnection-queue report (~25 min). Module 3 with the segment P&L (~30 min). Module 4 with the intersegment elimination note (~25 min). Module 5 with the FY2025 20-F redeemable-NCI note (~35 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — What a pipeline counts

**The single idea:** the headline is dominated by its weakest category.

**The numbers, 30 June 2026.** 84.1 GWh of storage development pipeline: 600 MWh under construction (0.7%), 4,378 MWh backlog (5.2%), 7,841 MWh advanced development (9.3%), **71,238 MWh early-stage (84.7%)**.

**The company's own definitions.** Early-stage is projects "in the process of securing interconnection rights". Advanced has "secured or have more than 90% certainty of securing an interconnection agreement". Backlog has "passed their risk cliff date" and is expected to start construction within one to four years.

**Strip the weakest category and 84.1 GWh becomes 12.8 GWh.** Both are true statements about the same company.

**It churns rather than compounds.** 83.5 GWh at 31 December 2025, 80.6 GWh at 31 March 2026, 84.1 GWh at 30 June 2026 — the June rise driven by Latin America nearly doubling while EMEA was pruned by 3.3 GWh. Management's phrase is "pruning less attractive projects". A pipeline that only rises is one nobody is cleaning.

**And the company contradicts itself across channels.** Recurrent's own website advertises 74 GWh with no as-of date — 10 GWh below what the parent filed with the SEC ten weeks earlier.

**Exercise:** compute what fraction of the 84.1 GWh is North American, then compute what fraction of *that* is early-stage (21,840 of 23,040 MWh). State in one sentence what the North American figure alone would tell an investor who did not read the stage split.

## Module 2 — The interconnection is the asset

**The single idea:** in a queue-constrained market, the scarce good is permission to connect, and it is what actually changes hands.

**Why queues bind.** A generator joins an interconnection queue, waits for studies determining what network upgrades its connection requires, and signs an agreement committing it to pay for them. This takes years, and most projects that enter never leave as operating plants.

**The one number that measures pipeline quality.** On its Q2 2026 call management said about **6 GW of solar and 13 GWh of storage** had secured interconnection globally, excluding operating projects. Against 84.1 GWh that is roughly **15%**.

**The trend is the finding.** At the January 2024 BlackRock announcement: 12 GWh interconnected out of 55 GWh — about 22%. The pipeline has grown faster than the connected subset inside it. Note where this fact lives: an earnings call, relayed by a third-party transcript, not a filing.

**Why it matters commercially.** An interconnection agreement transfers with the project. When Recurrent sells a development-stage asset, much of what the buyer pays for is the queue position and the studies behind it.

**Exercise:** two developers each claim 80 GWh. One discloses 15% interconnected, the other discloses nothing. Write the three questions you would put to the second, and say which single disclosure would let you rank them.

## Module 3 — Hold or sell

**The single idea:** the choice looks philosophical and is a balance-sheet decision.

**The case for holding.** Papago Storage — 300 MW / 1,200 MWh on a 20-year Arizona Public Service tolling agreement. One construction effort, twenty years of contracted cash flow, market risk on the utility, and an operating track record that lowers the next financing cost.

**The case for selling.** Fort Duncan — built, run merchant in ERCOT South for eight months, sold to Hunt Energy Network in February 2026. Years of development converted to cash in a quarter, capital recycled without new equity.

**Which one the numbers force.** Asset sales were $61.1m of $117.3m of Q2 2026 revenue. Segment debt is $4.1bn against roughly $210m of annualised recurring revenue and $74.9m of segment cash. Q2 interest expense alone was $41.9m — 36% of segment revenue. Four consecutive quarterly operating losses.

**The company said so plainly.** Its then-chief executive, May 2026: monetising assets means "the impact on our results of operations may be less favorable in the near term. However, this strategy remains necessary to deleverage our balance sheet and recycle capital."

**The tell.** The $500m BlackRock round in 2024 was raised to support becoming "both a developer and a long-term owner/operator". Two years later, sales are half of revenue. The transition was announced; it has not completed.

**Exercise:** a developer sells an operating 100 MW / 200 MWh battery eight months after commissioning. List four things it gives up and three it gains. Then say which single balance-sheet figure would make the decision unavoidable.

## Module 4 — The captive channel

**The single idea:** a manufacturer that owns a developer manufactures its own demand — and imports its own risk into the developer's financing.

**The structure.** Recurrent is a segment of Canadian Solar, whose e-STORAGE arm makes SolBank. e-STORAGE sits in the *Manufacturing* segment, so supply to Recurrent is intercompany.

**The accounting shows the scale.** Eliminated intersegment revenue: $790.1m (FY2024), $420.6m (FY2025), $18.1m (H1 2026). The FY2024 elimination also removed $253.5m of gross profit. You cannot book profit selling to yourself, so what gets eliminated is the clearest published measure of the channel. Its collapse in 2026 is unexplained in the filings — do not assert a cause.

**Do not invert the one disclosed ratio.** In Q2 2026, 471 MWh of 3.7 GWh of e-STORAGE shipments went to internal projects. That is e-STORAGE's internal exposure — about 13% — not Recurrent's supplier share, which is not disclosed anywhere.

**It is not exclusive.** The Tranquility battery retrofit, a Recurrent development scope, used **Powin** batteries. And on development-services jobs the asset owner — Southern Power with AIP at Tranquility and Garland, MN8 at Mustang — often chooses the equipment.

**The cost of the structure.** Recurrent's US projects run on tax equity while its usual supplier is the storage arm of a Chinese-founded group, precisely when foreign-entity material-assistance thresholds are tightest for storage. When Citi cut the parent to Sell it listed foreign-entity restrictions second and project-development leverage fourth.

**Exercise:** explain why an intersegment elimination of $790m is more informative about the captive channel than any statement the company could make about it in prose.

## Module 5 — What preferred equity actually is

**The single idea:** "20%" is a headline; the instrument is five terms.

**The transaction.** $500m from a BlackRock climate infrastructure fund, announced January 2024, closed in two tranches in 2024, for Series A convertible preferred in Recurrent Energy B.V.

**Term one — a 9% dividend, compounding quarterly, payable in kind.** It is being paid in kind: the parent discloses that diluted EPS includes "paid-in-kind dividends on the Recurrent Energy redeemable preferred shares". Two years of that on $500m compounds to roughly $598m of stated value. Because conversion is off stated value, the as-converted share **ratchets upward** while the dividend goes unpaid. The 20% is stated "as determined immediately upon closing" and has never been restated publicly.

**Term two — a 1.3x liquidation preference.** On a sale the holder takes the greater of 1.3 times stated value or its converted share: about $650m off the top.

**Term three — an exit clock.** From the fifth anniversary of the first closing, 31 May 2029, the holder can demand a company sale or a qualified listing.

**Term four — governance.** One board seat and a consent block over certain actions.

**Term five — it is not permanent equity.** Redemption can be triggered by events outside the parent's control, so it sits in **mezzanine**, between liabilities and equity. The carrying line was $317.8m at 30 June 2026 — but it commingles the preferred with US tax-equity interests and the filings do not disaggregate, so the preferred's own carrying value is genuinely not public.

**Put it together.** A claim compounding at 9%, senior at 1.3x, with a forced-exit right in 2029, sitting on a subsidiary with four consecutive quarterly operating losses. That behaves more like expensive structured debt than like a minority shareholding — and 2029 is a date to carry forward.

**Exercise:** compute the stated value of $500m accreting at 9% compounded quarterly over five years to May 2029. Then state what the holder receives at a 1.3x preference on that figure, and what would have to be true of the business for conversion to be the better choice.
