# SOLV Energy — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** SOLV Energy (listed; a pure-play solar and storage constructor with a large independent operations-and-maintenance fleet) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no geotechnical, electrical-installation or commissioning background.

**What this teaches.** The work itself, organised around one observation: almost every defect found during commissioning was created during installation, weeks earlier, by work that looked finished — so the useful checks happen at the moment the work becomes invisible. What a geotechnical investigation establishes and the sampling limitation nobody internalises, with the differing-site-conditions clause as the mechanism that exists to make bids cheaper. The civil scope activity by activity and the specific way each one overruns. Three foundation methods chosen by the ground rather than by preference. Then the electrical installation as hundreds of thousands of hand operations that look identical whether done right or wrong: torque and the two opposite failure modes, compression terminations, cable pulling with its invisible damage, and grounding as the purest case of a check with a moment. What first energisation actually finds, and why almost none of it is an equipment fault. The commissioning sequence from mechanical completion to handover. The two completions and what each one moves. And the handover document set, whose absence destroys the operator's ability to detect change.

**Why this gap.** The corpus had a works test and a site acceptance test from the manufacturing guide, and a commissioning concept, and nothing between them. Nobody had written down what a construction site does to convert tested equipment into an accepted plant, why the defect population at energisation is dominated by installation rather than hardware, or why the interval between substantial and final completion behaves so differently from the phase before it. The handover module closes a gap with a longer reach still: every guarantee in the integrator course is measured against a starting condition, and the starting condition is whatever the handover recorded.

**Its place in the construction set.** Third of four. It receives the seasonal and site-conditions risk the Blattner guide set up, and the crew-density and fatigue consequences the MasTec guide explained, and shows where both land — in the quality of terminations and in a compressed commissioning window. It begins exactly where the integrator course's manufacturing guide ends, at the site acceptance test, and it ends by handing the plant into the regime the integrator course's warranty guide describes.

## Module 1 — What is under the site
Borings, samples and laboratory tests, and what they establish: bearing capacity, groundwater, rock, soil chemistry and resistivity, frost depth, shrink-swell. Which downstream decisions each one sets, including two twenty-year ones — corrosion allowance and the grounding design — taken in the first weeks. The sampling limitation: point samples across thousands of acres, with everything between them interpolated. The differing-site-conditions clause and its two types. The counter-intuitive commercial logic that the clause exists to make bids cheaper, and the corollary that thinning the investigation buys a claim.

## Module 2 — Moving the ground and standing on it
Clearing and stripping, grading and the cut-and-fill balance, compaction and its testing, stormwater control as a regulated obligation inspected during rather than after the work, access roads and laydown sized for the largest delivery, pile installation, and foundation load testing. Haul as the expensive part of earthwork and the balance as designed rather than discovered. Why a failed load test questions a design assumption across a whole site rather than at one location.

## Module 3 — Three ways to hold a structure down
Driven piles, drilled piers and helical piles, and ballasted foundations. Speed against ground tolerance; concrete cure as a fixed duration on the critical path; corrosion and resistivity; noise and vibration limits; spoil handling; haul mass and wind uplift. The lesson that this is not an engineering preference — the ground, the neighbours and the lease decide it, which makes it the clearest case in the course of a construction cost set by a development decision.

## Module 4 — The terminations
Why clamping force cannot be judged by feel, and the opposite failure modes of an under-torqued and an over-torqued connection. Calibrated tools, documented values, torque striping as a permanent audit trail, and re-torque after thermal cycling. Compression lugs and the die-size defect that passes every casual check. Cable pulling: tension, sidewall pressure at bends, minimum bend radius, and damage that is invisible until it is buried. Grounding as installed-first and buried-immediately, and the witnessed open-trench inspection that is the only practical opportunity. The closing idea that workmanship is a process rather than a talent, which is the same argument the integrator course makes about a cell plant.

## Module 5 — What energisation finds
The actual defect population: swapped phases, reversed instrument-transformer polarity, mis-landed control wiring, wrong ratios, default protection settings, wrong parameter revisions, loose terminations, blocked cooling, unbonded grounds. Why almost none of it is an equipment fault. The order-of-magnitude cost escalation at each stage a defect survives. Why it is discovered at the point of maximum accumulated delay and maximum pressure to accept a marginal result. The response: inspection and test plans, hold points, and evidence captured before it disappears — the same substitution of process proof for output inspection the integrator course describes.

## Module 6 — The commissioning sequence
Mechanical completion, de-energised pre-commissioning checks, independent electrical acceptance testing, inspections and permission to energise, back-feed, the supplier's site acceptance test, integrated functional testing against the real connection, the performance demonstration the guarantees are measured against, and the reliability run whose clock a single trip can restart. Reading the sequence by lane rather than by date: from energisation onward most of it sits with parties the contractor does not employ.

## Module 7 — Two completions and the handover
Mechanical completion, energisation, site acceptance, substantial completion, commercial operation and final completion, and what each one moves — testing, safety regime, warranty clocks, damages, custody and insurance, revenue, and the last of the retention. Why punch-list closure routinely outlasts commissioning. The handover package item by item, why it is the deliverable whose absence stops nothing and therefore the one that slips, and the consequence years later: a missing record does not inconvenience the operator, it destroys the ability to detect change.

## Pacing
Seven modules, roughly 25 minutes each. Modules 1 and 4 are the two that most reward slow reading — the first because everything civil follows from it, the fourth because it is the physical origin of most of what Module 5 describes. Modules 5 to 7 form a continuous argument and are best read in one sitting. Module 7's final section is the seam into the integrator course's warranty guide and should be read immediately before it.
