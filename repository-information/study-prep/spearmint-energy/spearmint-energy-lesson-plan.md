# Spearmint Energy — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the merchant developer** — the choice between selling projects and keeping them, the development-stage ladder as a price list, how a battery with no contract is financed to construction and beyond, the market in which unbuilt projects change hands, why the trading desk is the collateral, and where the model breaks. Spearmint is the worked example: 350 MW / 700 MWh operating in ERCOT, 300 MW / 600 MWh under construction, Minnesota's first two standalone-storage permits, and not one named customer.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Classroom** owns the hour-by-hour revenue of an ERCOT battery — ancillary-service stacking, energy arbitrage, duration and degradation. The **Aypa plan** teaches the contracted platform from the buyer's chair; this plan is its mirror from the developer's chair. The **Intersect plan** teaches the third model, the captive developer. None of that is repeated.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** With no offtaker, the lender lends against three things — the tax credit, the desk and the option to sell the project — and the stack is built to make each bankable. The ladder is a price list: each rung retires a risk and raises the price a buyer will pay. And a merchant fleet that reaches scale in a saturated market meets the market on day one, however good the desk.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Spearmint publishes no accounts, names no owner and names no counterparty. Every figure here is a facility, a tax-equity commitment, a permit condition or a market statistic stated by the company, a lender, counsel, a regulator or Modo Energy. The brief that commissioned this plan supposed a Blackstone sponsor and a develop-to-sell model; neither is in the record, and the plan says so where it matters.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 (~25 min). Module 2 with the Red Egret financing release open (~40 min) — it is the whole no-PPA stack. Module 3 (~30 min). Module 4 with Modo's 2025 revenue note open (~30 min). Module 5 (~20 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — Sell the project or keep it

**The single idea:** at the top of the ladder a developer either sells to an owner or becomes one.

**Develop-to-sell.** Carry a project to ready-to-build and sell it to an IPP or fund; capital returns in two or three years; no construction loan, no operations team, no twenty-year price view. The price depends on the rung reached.

**Develop-to-own.** Finance the build, keep the cash flow for decades; needs long-term capital, operations and — for a merchant — a trading desk.

**Spearmint's choice.** 'Developing, owning, operating, and optimizing' in every boilerplate; buys from develop-to-sell developers (Con Edison Development, an unnamed 'major developer', BW Solar) and holds what it builds. The only sale is a post-COD stake to Kyuden International. The founder's reason: the volatility is the product, and 'utilities … are not as focused' on the places that have it.

**Self-check:** a company buys three early-stage MISO queue positions from a solar developer. Which side of the trade is develop-to-sell? *(The seller.)*

## Module 2 — Financing a battery with no PPA

**The single idea:** the stack is built around the three bankable things.

**The credit first.** USD 92 million of Greenprint tax equity at Revolution — one of the first standalone-storage ITC deals; USD 98 million of Sugar Creek tax-equity commitments at Tierra Seca and Seven Flags; about USD 126 million from an ITC transfer at Red Egret.

**The platform loan.** USD 200 million from Aiga (2023), joined by Nuveen and Elda River, expanded to USD 325 million with Harrison Street (June 2026): private credit lending to the company against the pipeline and the desk — the substitute for a financial sponsor.

**Construction debt sized to the exit.** Manulife's USD 59 million construction-to-term loans and USD 95 million of tax-equity bridge loans at the two 2025 projects; Red Egret's USD 225 million construction facility repaid at COD by USD 96 million of Nuveen preferred equity and the credit sale — no term loan named.

**What is absent.** No revenue put, no hedge, no toll; the Minnesota applications toll to 'its affiliated merchant energy business'. Waranch: merchant-only projects suit 'approximately 20%' of investors.

**Self-check:** Red Egret's COD slips a year. What happens to the construction lenders? *(They wait — the take-out is preferred equity plus credit proceeds that arrive at COD; the bridge has nowhere to land until then.)*

## Module 3 — The ladder as a price list, and the pre-COD market

**The single idea:** projects are priced by the risks retired, and the direction of trade reveals each company's model.

**The rungs.** Site control → queue position → studies and interconnection agreement → permit → offtake or hedge → ready-to-build and notice to proceed → construction → COD. Spearmint skips the offtake rung.

**The trades.** Con Edison Development → Spearmint (development stage); unnamed developer → Spearmint (900 MW portfolio); BW Solar → Spearmint (early-stage); Eolian → Aypa (permitted; then contracted); Spearmint → Kyuden (operating); Intersect → IPX (a carve-out); Blackstone → Brookfield (a platform).

**Self-check:** which rung is 'the classic develop-to-sell exit'? *(Ready-to-build — the buyer takes construction risk and little else.)*

## Module 4 — The desk is the collateral, and the market that turned

**The single idea:** the trading desk is what the lenders are lending against, and no desk out-trades a flat curve.

**The desk.** A founder from power portfolio management; a chairman who founded an FTR-and-congestion trading firm (which a lender says controls the company); a director who ran EDF Trading for 21 years; a trader posting that describes 'around-the-clock physical dispatch' with the company's own software. In-house optimisation keeps the margin and carries the risk.

**The market.** Modo: ERCOT battery revenue about USD 193 per kW (2023), USD 56 (2024), about USD 29 (2025 forecast) as roughly 9 GW arrived; Enverus: ancillary revenue down about 90 per cent. Tierra Seca and Seven Flags came online in December 2025.

**Self-check:** the desk is excellent and the 2025 price curve is flat. What does the fleet earn? *(Roughly the market average — the desk's edge is a few per cent on a base the market sets.)*

## Module 5 — Where it fails

**The single idea:** the model has six failure points, and the record shows four of them already.

Saturation (2025). FEOC and a single supplier — four of four ERCOT projects on Sungrow, Minnesota designs on Tesla, a supplier search since May 2024 with no order announced. Permits — Pumpkinvine denied unanimously in Kokomo; Red Egret reworked; Minnesota conditions. Pipeline as inventory — 'over 15 GWh' dropped from the boilerplate in May 2026. No sponsor — a lender's patience is shorter than an owner's; headcount 'over 70' to 'over 50'; no CFO listed. The take-out — a construction loan that must be repaid at COD.

**Self-check:** which of those six could a signed toll have prevented? *(Saturation exposure — a toll fixes the fee; it does nothing for permits, FEOC or the take-out.)*
