# Stonepeak — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** Stonepeak Partners LP, a private SEC-registered infrastructure manager; its closed-end, core and evergreen funds; the data-centre and battery platforms it controls or created (Cologix, Digital Edge, Montera, Kingdom); and its non-controlling power positions · **Written:** 2026-10-05 from the Profiler dossier (profileVersion 1) · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no finance background.

**Purpose:** teach how a private manager that builds companies reaches data-centre and storage buyers without buying anything itself:
- how to read a private manager's public record when there is no annual report;
- closed-end, core and evergreen funds, and why a manager moves an asset into a continuation vehicle;
- building a company around a management team, and who signs at such a company;
- the two kinds of data centre in its portfolio, and the case where the utility owns the power plant on the customer's site;
- why a long auction contract makes a battery bankable;
- a large cheque beside an operator, the gap between signing and closing, and the buying-authority test.

## Module 1 — Reading a private manager's public record

**The single idea:** with no annual report, the record is the adviser registration, the funds' offering notices and the counterparties' filings.

1. **Form ADV.** Every SEC-registered adviser files one each year. Stonepeak's (April 2026) states USD 81.9bn of regulatory assets under management across 125 accounts, 359 employees, and that the co-founder and chief executive controls the adviser through its general partner.
2. **Two AUM figures.** Stonepeak's website quotes about USD 93bn as of March 2026, 'inclusive of subsequent committed capital'. Regulatory AUM follows the SEC's formula; the published figure counts money promised but not yet called. Record each as itself.
3. **Form D.** Each fund files one when it sells interests, showing the amount sold — which is how a fund with no announced close can still be sized.
4. **A GP stake.** Blue Owl's funds hold a passive minority in the manager; with other outside investors they receive under 17% of its fees and carried interest and have no control over investment decisions.

**Self-check:** Why might a private manager's website AUM be larger than the ADV figure? *(It may include capital committed but not yet called, which the regulatory formula treats differently.)*

## Module 2 — Closed-end, core and evergreen funds

**The single idea:** how long a fund may hold an asset shapes what it buys and when it sells.

1. **Closed-end flagships.** Investors make fund commitments; the manager calls the money over a few years to buy businesses and sells them to return it within roughly a decade. Fund IV sold USD 14.0bn; Fund V had sold USD 7.29bn by January 2025, with no final close on the record.
2. **Core.** The Core Fund holds steadier, contracted assets for longer at lower but more predictable returns.
3. **Evergreen.** Stonepeak-Plus Infrastructure Fund, for individual investors, keeps raising money with no end date; vehicles of this kind allow withdrawals only on set terms.
4. **Continuation vehicles.** When a manager wants to keep an asset past a closed-end fund's life, it moves it into a new single-asset fund whose investors buy out the old one. Stonepeak holds Cologix this way, in a vehicle with about USD 5.0bn of gross assets per the ADV.

**Self-check:** Why does a continuation vehicle matter to a seller? *(The same owner stays in place for years longer, so the platform's strategy and procurement team are likely to stay too.)*

## Module 3 — Building a company around a team

**The single idea:** Stonepeak often creates the buyer, so the first orders arrive with the first financed project.

1. **Platform creation.** A sponsor-created platform starts with a management team and a capital commitment. Digital Edge was formed in 2020 on a USD 1bn commitment and reports 31 data centres and 1.8 GW of secured IT power across Asia. Montera launched in April 2025 on a USD 1.5bn commitment; its first published campus is 500 MW in Glendale, Arizona, due in 2028. Kingdom, the Japanese battery platform, was established with a partner in 2024.
2. **Who signs.** Each platform's own management. At Cologix the president's remit covers data-centre design, engineering, construction and supply chain; Montera's delivery team comes from a hyperscale cloud and a colocation operator.
3. **When.** A young platform places its large orders as its first projects are financed. Kingdom's first project loan, in April 2026, is also the release that names its battery supplier.

**Self-check:** A new Stonepeak-backed platform announces its first campus. Whom do you approach, and when? *(Its delivery team, before the project's financing closes — that is when the equipment list is fixed.)*

## Module 4 — Two kinds of data centre, and the utility's power plant on site

**The single idea:** an interconnection building and a hyperscale campus buy different equipment, and sometimes the utility buys it instead of the operator.

1. **Interconnection.** Cologix runs 45-plus carrier-neutral data centres in 13 North American markets. Their value is the number of networks meeting inside; they sit where networks meet and are smaller per site than campuses.
2. **Hyperscale.** Montera builds whole campuses for large tenants — Glendale is 500 MW — which buy substations, medium-voltage switchgear and large UPS and generator fleets.
3. **The utility's plant.** In 2025 Ohio regulators approved AEP Ohio building onsite fuel cells for Amazon Web Services and for Cologix, about 98 MW across the two contracts, with Cologix's running 15 years. AEP Ohio buys, owns and operates the fuel cells; Cologix buys the electricity and covers the project cost through its contract.
4. **The lesson.** Equipment on a customer's site can still be bought by someone else. Always ask who owns it.

**Self-check:** A fuel-cell plant sits inside a data centre's fence. Who is the buyer of its switchgear? *(Whoever owns the plant — at Cologix's Ohio site, the utility.)*

## Module 5 — Why a long auction contract makes a battery bankable

**The single idea:** a fixed payment for about 20 years turns a battery into something a bank will lend against.

1. **The problem.** A battery that earns only from trading power prices has uncertain revenue, so banks lend little against it.
2. **Japan's answer.** The long-term decarbonization auction gives winners a fixed capacity payment for about 20 years to cover most of their fixed costs.
3. **Kingdom.** Its nine Japanese projects, 479 MW in total, each hold such a contract — four from the first auction and five from the second. That income let Kingdom sign its first long-term project-finance loan, with MUFG Bank, for the 29 MW Mimasaka project, which 'incorporates batteries from CATL'.
4. **Who signs.** Kingdom's management; Stonepeak supplied the equity.

**Self-check:** Why does each new project financing matter to a battery seller? *(The supplier is chosen by then, and the financing release often names it.)*

## Module 6 — A cheque beside an operator, signing versus closing, and the test

**The single idea:** the size of the cheque does not decide who buys; the operator and the closing do.

1. **Beside an operator.** Stonepeak owns 50% of the Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind project, but Virginia Power 'will retain full operational control of the construction and operations'. Stonepeak funds half the costs up to a total of USD 11.3bn — 48% of the remaining capital per Dominion's mid-2026 deck. It holds 40% of Louisiana LNG, which Woodside operates, and minority stakes in Repsol and Ørsted portfolios.
2. **Data-centre minorities.** About 29% of the common equity of American Tower's CoreSite business plus all of its convertible preferred, about 36% fully converted; American Tower keeps control.
3. **Signed, not closed.** A majority of Cleco, a Louisiana utility with 2,676 MW of generation, on closing and subject to approvals; a majority of Castrol at about USD 10.1bn enterprise value, with CPP Investments committing up to USD 1.05bn of equity alongside; an equity investment in AMPYR Distributed Energy with the stake undisclosed.
4. **The test.** Cologix, Digital Edge, Montera and Kingdom pass through their own management; Cleco and Castrol are pending; CVOW, Louisiana LNG, the co-owned renewables, CoreSite and Princeton Digital Group fail.

**Self-check:** A headline says 'Stonepeak buys a USD 10bn business'. What two things do you check? *(Whether USD 10bn is an enterprise value or an equity cheque, and whether the deal has closed or only been signed.)*
