# Strata Clean Energy — Technology Lesson Plan

*Full-depth curriculum behind the in-app study guide (`live-site-pages/profiler-data/strata-clean-energy.study.json`). Not deployed. Baseline: high-school STEM. Dossier: `strata-clean-energy.profile.json` (schema v7, v1, 2026-09-05).*

## What this plan teaches, and what it leaves to other guides

Strata is the **seller's side** of the late-stage storage market. The GridStor guide reads the White Tank transaction from the buyer's chair; this plan reads it from the originator's. It teaches (1) what a de-risked project is as a product, (2) why a founder-owned developer sells a 20-year utility contract it could have kept, (3) the development margin and the retained service contracts that replace an owner's cash flow, (4) how project debt and tax equity travel with a sold project, and (5) where the originate-and-sell model breaks.

It does **not** re-teach: the tolling revenue model, ancillary stacking or duration arithmetic (Classroom); the contracted platform as a financed asset base and how a sponsor exits (Aypa guide); the merchant developer and the pre-COD price list (Spearmint guide); the energy park (Intersect guide); economic ownership and build-and-transfer (Invenergy guide); buying late-stage projects and RA vs toll vs swap (GridStor guide); the sub-10 MW distributed play and selling at NTP (Available Power guide).

## Module 1 — Fundamentals: a project as a product (45 min)

- **The development stage ladder.** Site control → interconnection queue position → interconnection agreement → permits → offtake contract → supply agreement → financing → notice to proceed → construction → commercial operation. Each rung retires a way the project can fail. A buyer pays for retired risks, not megawatts.
- **The contract rung.** A 20-year utility tolling agreement (APS) or resource-adequacy contract (SCE, PG&E) converts uncertain hourly earnings into a fixed payment from a regulated counterparty. Regulated utilities procure through all-source RFPs; the RFP is where the product's core is made.
- **The supply rung.** A battery supply agreement plus a long-term service agreement (LTSA) fixes price, delivery and maintenance before sale. White Tank: Canadian Solar e-STORAGE SolBank 3.0, 576 MWh DC, 20-year LTSA, signed March 2025 — six months before the sale.
- **The financing rung (optional).** Scatter Wash's USD 559 million construction and tax-equity financing closed February 2024; sale to CIP September 2024. A financed, contracted project under construction is the top of the ladder short of operation.
- **Check:** list the rungs White Tank had when sold versus Scatter Wash. (White Tank: contract, supplier, permits — no financing. Scatter Wash: all of those plus financing and construction start.)

## Module 2 — Why sell a 20-year contract (50 min)

- **Cost of capital arithmetic.** A fixed 20-year cash flow discounted at a fund's rate is worth more than the same cash flow discounted at a developer's. Work an example: USD 10 million a year for 20 years at 7 percent ≈ USD 106 million; at 12 percent ≈ USD 75 million. The USD 31 million gap is the room for a development margin.
- **Who Strata is.** '100% privately held company owned by our founders' (financing page). No infrastructure fund. Growth capital = revolvers: USD 170 million (Zions, 2022) → USD 300 million (Nomura, First Citizens, ING; Green Loan Principles; 2023) → USD 450 million (July 2026). Blackstone's 2021 USD 150 million was credit per the CFO.
- **Capital recycling.** Sale proceeds return the equity and fund the next RFP bid. The revolver is working capital for a manufacturer of projects; it is not built to own 1 GWh for 20 years.
- **The buyers' identities.** Capital Dynamics (fund manager) → Arevon; CIP CI V (EUR 12 billion target); GridStor (a GSAM-created owner). Every buyer has cheaper, longer money.
- **The 'over 50% retained' claim.** True of the ~1 GW owned solar base (NC, VA PPAs); not of utility-scale storage (three sold, Justice kept, Inland Empire kept at 70 MW / 280 MWh, 20 MWh in Vermont).

## Module 3 — What the seller keeps (35 min)

- Construction-management agreement (fee; EPC keeps fixed-price risk; owner keeps overrun risk above it) — Scatter Wash for CIP with Mortenson as EPC.
- Asset-management agreement (contract administration for the toll, LTSA, warranties, ISO compliance) — Scatter Wash; '260+ assets under O&M management' company-wide.
- O&M contract (availability, remote operations) — Strata Solar Services, 180+ staff; at Scatter Wash the equipment O&M sits with Tesla and Novasource.
- Consultative role to COD — White Tank for GridStor.
- EPC awarded with the sale — Cerulean Solar to Dominion (November 2022).
- **Lesson:** 'under management' counts contracts, not assets. Strata's figure moved from gigawatts (4.2 GW in 2024) to asset counts ('260+' in 2026).

## Module 4 — How the lender reads a sold project (40 min)

- Project debt sits with the project company; a sale transfers it with lender consent. The DSCR is set by the contract's revenue, so the debt sizing is the same whoever owns the equity — a founder-owned seller can raise the financing and sell the financed project.
- Tax equity travels: U.S. Bancorp Impact Finance was Scatter Wash's primary tax-equity investor; the ITC belongs to the owner at placement in service. On the two Vermont batteries Strata kept, it raised USD 8.7 million of tax equity itself (among the first standalone-storage ITC financings under the IRA).
- The corporate lender underwrites the recurring business (EPC, O&M, owned solar) and the sale proceeds — the CFO's stated use of the 2026 upsizing.

## Module 5 — Where it fails (30 min)

- The buyer disappears (fund appetite, rates, FEOC uncertainty on the supplier) → the developer holds what its balance sheet was not built to hold.
- The utility writes the product: fewer or shorter RFPs cut supply; peers in the same APS RFPs (Recurrent Energy, EDF Renewables).
- Fees are not ownership; they end when the buyer chooses.
- Siting risk lands on the originator alone — Hanover (2024) and Culpeper (2026) denials; Halifax approved 6-2 (2023).
- Record holes a buyer would want filled: no sale prices; no Justice completion release five months after the stated April 2026 date; owned fleet never broken out by ISO; inconsistent site statistics; unverified snippets (attrition, DEQ citations).

## Module 6 — The industry map (25 min)

- Buyer/seller pairs in the corpus: Strata → GridStor (White Tank); Strata → CIP (Scatter Wash); Strata → Capital Dynamics/Arevon (Ventura).
- Peer models: Aypa (holds contracts, sells the company); Spearmint (merchant, keeps what it builds); Available Power (sells small batteries at NTP); esVolta (keeps and operates a mixed book; same lead bank, Nomura); Invenergy (build-and-transfer to utilities — Strata's Dominion solar work is the regional version).
- The 2026 reorganisation: hyperscaler 'Entitled Power Land', natural gas, Power-to-X — statements of intent, no named projects.

## Corrections to the brief (record them, do not smooth them)

- Not a 'contracted and merchant mid-size BESS owner across CAISO, PJM and ERCOT': no owned battery in ERCOT or PJM in any source; one owned CAISO battery (Inland Empire, PG&E RA); Arizona assets in APS territory outside any ISO; every disclosed storage contract is a toll or RA agreement.
- No sponsor: founder-owned; Blackstone was credit.
- White Tank sold 18 September 2025 with Strata 'in a consultative role' — confirmed by both parties' releases.

## Suggested pacing

Two sittings: Modules 1–3 (about 2 h 10 min) before reading the GridStor guide's 'buying late' table; Modules 4–6 (about 1 h 35 min) after. Drill with the study guide's flashcards; the quiz's 'sell or hold' question is the one to get right.
