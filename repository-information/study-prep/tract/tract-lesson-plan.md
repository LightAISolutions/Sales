# Tract — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **the developer's developer** — the horizontal developer whose product is a finished parcel (zoned, permitted, watered, fibred, with a substation and a utility date) sold or leased to the developer who will build on it: the land value ladder from farmland to entitled to powered to leased and who captures each rung, what "shovel-ready" precisely and fuzzily means, why a land developer grows a vertical arm and how an intra-group sale between sister funds works, how a 197-month lease to a AA- tenant becomes USD 8.4 billion of Rule 144A senior secured notes and what loan-to-cost says about the equity beneath them, and who decides the price of power when a developer reserves gigawatts ahead of tenants — the commission through a large-load electric service agreement, the private contract through its arbitration clause, or the developer through bridging gas plants while the utility and the courts decide. Tract is the worked example because it is the purest one in the corpus: fourteen master-planned parks and about 21,000 entitled acres in seven states, a sister platform (Fleet Data Centers) that builds the campuses, USD 8.4 billion of notes raised by that affiliate against two leases to a tenant Bloomberg names as Nvidia, a utility lawsuit and a regulator's decision pending on its temporary generation — and not one parcel sold to a third party on the public record.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **PowerHouse** plan teaches what powered land is, the entitlement toolkit and the two utility contracts; the **STACK** plan teaches land-and-power banking as one rung of a developer's ladder; the **Compass** plan teaches the sponsor's capital stack and ABS; the **EdgeCore** plan teaches the build-to-suit lease from the landlord's chair; the **Fluidstack** plan teaches the neocloud lease from the tenant's. This plan sits under the landlords and beside the utility and repeats none of them.

**Three ideas carry the plan.** A parcel is priced by what has been done to it, and the powered rung — a dated utility instrument — is the widest, slowest and least controllable step. A horizontal developer's only observed customer being its own vertical affiliate means the middle rung has no market price yet. And "powered" is the utility's word: the developer's energisation dates are promises the utility makes, which is why the 2026 dispute is about which instrument governs and why the developer applied for its own gas plants.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Tract, Tract Capital and Fleet publish no accounts; the live sites block automated fetches, so every company page was read from Wayback captures dated October 2025 to August 2026; the sellers and prices of the Nevada parcels, the outcome of the March 2026 easement trial, headcount and any third-party parcel sale are unknown. Fleet's bond terms come from its own releases and Bloomberg's reporting; the NV Energy dispute from the utility's suit, Tract's statements and the PUCN docket index. Where the plan gives arithmetic (per-acre prices; notes per utility MW) it says so.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 in one sitting (~30 min). Module 2 with the Tract Capital Fleet launch release and Kirkland's March 2026 release open (~30 min). Module 3 with Fleet's two notes releases and the Bloomberg syndications (~35 min). Module 4 with NewsData's account of the NV Energy suit and the PUCN docket list (~35 min). Module 5 with the Virginia Business and WTVR Hanover coverage (~20 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — The land value ladder

**The single idea:** a data-center parcel is priced by what has been done to it, not by its acreage; the rungs are raw → entitled and serviced → powered → leased, and only the top rung supports large debt.

**The rungs in Tract's numbers.** Raw: Buckeye USD 136 million for 2,069 acres (~USD 66,000/acre, 2024); Hanover ~USD 32.7 million for 1,200 acres; Morris ~USD 51.5 million for 343 already-zoned acres (~USD 150,000/acre). Entitled and serviced: by-right zoning, development agreements, annexations, water reservations, roads, wet utilities, substations — "USD 50–100 million" or "USD 100–150 million" of horizontal spend per site (press). Powered: a signed utility instrument and a date (Peru Shelf February 2027; South Valley September 2027; Caldwell 250 MW Q1 2028; Morris June 2028; Hanover 2028). Leased: Fleet's two campuses.

**Where the margin must sit.** The horizontal developer roughly doubles its basis to reach shovel-ready, so the powered rung must be worth several times raw land — and the corpus holds no third-party price at that rung.

**Two meanings of shovel-ready.** Precise: zoning, development agreement, graded pads, utilities at the boundary ("7-Day Grading and 30-Day Building Permits"). Fuzzy: "Site energization February 2027" — the utility's date.

**Exercise:** place every Tract park from the dossier's projects table on the ladder using the dossier's evidence (utility instrument yes/no; date yes/no; buyer yes/no), and total the acres on each rung.

## Module 2 — Horizontal and vertical: why a land developer grows Fleet

**The single idea:** a parcel sold earns a margin once; a campus leased for 197 months earns rent, is bond-financeable and keeps the land in the group — so the widest rung (powered → leased) pulled the horizontal developer into building.

**The structure.** Tract Capital Management (Denver, ~USD 6 billion AUM) sponsors the land fund ("Tract I", ~25,000 acres) and Fleet Data Centers I, LP (~USD 620 million of equity). Fleet launched January 2025 as "the mega-campus vertical development strategy" — "leasing model preferred over sales" — under a Google Cloud/AWS data-center executive; van Rooyen is CEO of the manager and executive chairman of both platforms.

**The intra-group sale.** Kirkland & Ellis, March 2026: "the lease and purchase and sale of real property" — Fleet's project company bought a 252-acre parcel from "private equity funds sponsored by Tract Capital" and leased the campus; the USD 4.6 billion notes' proceeds "reimburse the Issuer's sponsor for prior equity contributions used to fund acquisition of property". Nothing improper; no market test — the only observed price for a Tract parcel was set by Tract.

**Who signs.** A parcel buyer contracts with a Tract fund entity (Grundy County Power NR I; Reno Power); a Fleet tenant with a bankruptcy-remote project company (SV RNO / PR RNO Property Owner I) holding one campus and one lease.

**Exercise:** draw the ownership chain from Tract Capital Management down to the two SPVs and the tenant, and mark which entity holds the land, the lease, the notes, the utility contracts and the litigation.

## Module 3 — Project bonds: how a lease becomes USD 4.6 billion of notes

**The single idea:** senior secured notes issued by a single-purpose company whose only assets are one campus, one substation and one lease price on the tenant's credit, not the sponsor's.

**The two deals.** USD 3.8 billion 5.875% due 2031 (SV RNO; priced 13 February 2026, closed 24 February; 2.5% annual amortisation; first-priority liens on the issuer's assets and its holding company's equity; JPMorgan lead, Morgan Stanley co-manager per Bloomberg). USD 4.6 billion 6.500% due 2031 (PR RNO; priced 28–29 April at 99 to yield 6.74%, closed 1 May). Fleet's claim: "the highest-rated, largest issuance, highest loan-to-cost, and tightest pricing for any single data center high-yield bond project financing to date".

**The credit.** "AA- investment grade rated tenant with a market cap in excess of USD 3 trillion", 197-month triple-net — Nvidia per Bloomberg (initial ~16 years plus two 10-year options), unconfirmed by Fleet. That counterparty is why USD 8.4 billion sold at under 7% for campuses that had not broken ground.

**Loan-to-cost.** Notes of USD 3.8–4.6 billion against a build reported at USD 4.6 billion, with land equity repaid from proceeds: efficient for the sponsor, thin for the bondholder; the cushions are 2.5% amortisation and funded debt reserves, and the risks are overrun, energisation delay and the single tenant.

**Compare.** Compass's ABS (operating, tenanted buildings), EdgeCore's limited-recourse bank loans (sized to a lease), PowerHouse's syndicated construction loan (one building): Fleet took the same idea to the bond market before construction, on the strongest tenant credit in the industry and the horizontal arm's finished parcel.

**Exercise:** compute notes per utility MW and per IT MW for both campuses; then estimate the equity cushion under three build-cost assumptions (USD 4.0, 4.6 and 5.2 billion) and say at which one the bondholders own the campus.

## Module 4 — Who decides the price of power

**The single idea:** a developer that reserves gigawatts years ahead of tenants is asking the utility to build for it; whether the regulator, the contract or the developer's own generators answer is the 2026 dispute.

**The instruments.** The commission's: a large-load electric service agreement (NV Energy filed one with the two Fleet SPVs on 29 May 2026, docket 26-05027) and the resource plan. The contract's: Tract's June 2024 agreements with NV Energy via Reno Power, with an arbitration clause. The developer's: temporary self-generation — 144 MW at South Valley (26-04026) and 218 MW at Peru Ridge (26-04027), applied for 20 April 2026, decision scheduled 8 September 2026 — plus a new 345 kV substation (26-04007) and a noticed 88.6-mile 525 kV loop.

**The fight.** 16 June: Tract demands arbitration. 24 July: NV Energy sues in Washoe County (CV26-02142) to keep supply adequacy, cost allocation and resource-plan treatment before the PUCN — "shifting the infrastructure and energy costs to Nevada families". Tract: the complaint "seriously misstates … the contracts NV Energy signed"; ">USD 127 million" spent; NV Energy demanding "USD 1 billion in network infrastructure upgrades for the benefit of all NV Energy customers".

**The scale.** NV Energy: "at least five data centers with a combined load of nearly 5.5 GW" — about a third of its generating capacity against a Northern Nevada peak of ~2.1 GW; neither a 100 MW large-load tariff nor a 2024 private contract was written for a customer larger than the system.

**Bridging generation.** Lets the campus energise on the tenant's clock; per the Nevada Independent it "would skirt" the renewable standard, and the Sierra Club opposed it — the powered-land developer admitting "powered" was never entirely in its gift.

**Exercise:** write the four questions the PUCN must answer (adequacy, allocation, IRP treatment, the temporary plants) and, for each, which party's instrument would decide it and what Tract's stated position is.

## Module 5 — The consent record

**The single idea:** the entitlement rung is a vote; the product succeeds where a county wants growth and fails where residents organise, and the ratio is roughly even.

**Approvals.** Hanover (May 2024, unanimous; USD 3.5 million road cash; USD 1.78 billion tax claim); Buckeye (August 2024, unanimous; USD 50 million reimbursement after USD 60 million of city revenue); Farmington (November 2024); Lyon County (2025); Altoona (2025; 80 acres for the city); Morris (zoned at purchase; FERC-approved ComEd TSA).

**Defeats.** Chesterfield (denial recommended June 2025; withdrawn 2 July; county then required CUPs and "signaled an end"); Mooresville (withdrawn 13 August 2025); Hanover's Mountain Road (6–1 commission for, 4–3 board against, 27 May 2026, despite a USD 15 million water pledge and a USD 901 million tax claim). Pending: Goochland (870 acres, 900 MW; opposed at pre-application, July 2026).

**Consent as a cost line.** Road cash, water pledges, park contributions, "no county tax incentives requested", a March 2026 white paper on data centers and residential property values — the developer pricing the vote it cannot buy.

**Exercise:** for each Virginia proposal, list the decider, the offer and the outcome, and write one sentence on what a fourth Virginia application would have to offer differently.

**Where it fails (for the whole plan):** no buyer but yourself; the affiliate's price is not evidence; "powered" is the utility's word; acres are not megawatts; consent is a coin toss in the wrong county; the credit is the tenant's, not the developer's.
