# Whiting-Turner — Technology Lesson Plan

**Subject:** The Whiting-Turner Contracting Company (private, Towson, Maryland) · **Written:** 2026-09-06 · **Baseline assumed:** high-school STEM, no construction background.

**What this teaches.** How a hyperscale data centre is actually assembled by a company that physically builds none of it — the contracting machinery, the risk allocation, why mechanical and electrical work runs the schedule, and why commissioning is the part that cannot be compressed. Then the reading skills the subject demands: how to interpret a self-reported industry league table, and how to research a company that publishes nothing.

**Why this gap.** The corpus has three general contractors and no guide explaining what a general contractor does. It is also the first private subject in the programme where the research method is the lesson — almost every hard fact reached the record because a counterparty was compelled to speak.

## Module 1 — Three ways to buy a building
Hard bid, construction management at risk, design-build, and integrated project delivery, taught as four different answers to one question: who pays when the estimate is wrong. Then design-assist, which is not a delivery model but changes the outcome as much as one. Exercise: given a project with an unfinished design and a fixed opening date, argue for a model and name the risk you are accepting.

## Module 2 — The contracting tiers and flow-down
Owner, prime, first-tier, second-tier, supplier. How obligations travel downward and what happens when one fails to travel — the mechanism by which a prime contractor becomes liable for the wage violations of a firm two tiers below that it never selected. This module is short and it is the one most likely to be useful outside construction.

## Module 3 — Why the electricians set the schedule
Why a data hall inverts the normal relationship between structure and services. Long-lead equipment, clash detection, and the two shapes of mechanical and electrical capability: self-perform against coordination. Exercise: given a contractor's own description of its in-house group, decide which shape it is and say what evidence settled it.

## Module 4 — Commissioning
Levels one through five, and why integrated systems testing is where redundancy stops being a diagram. The link to the storage side of this corpus: an uninterruptible power supply is proven by the transfer test, not by its datasheet. Also substantial completion and why the date is litigated.

## Module 5 — Reading the industry's own numbers
The main contractor ranking is a confidential self-reported survey, unaudited, and for a private firm usually the only revenue figure in existence. How to separate total revenue from sector share; why a firm can add a billion dollars and still fall a place; and the publication-year labelling trap that makes one figure look like two independent confirmations.

## Module 6 — Researching a company that publishes nothing
The transferable method. Where the record actually lives for a private subject: litigation dockets, labour enforcement, federal award databases, and surety panels reconstructed from bond-claim party lists. Two disciplines to carry away — an unreachable database is a collection gap and not an empty record, and an ownership claim is best tested against a list that would have to include it rather than by searching for a positive statement.

## Pacing
Six modules, roughly 40 minutes each. Modules 1–4 are a sequence and should be taken in order. Modules 5 and 6 are research-method modules and are useful well beyond this company or this industry.

## Sources for the technology and industry content
The company's own market, innovation and in-house MEP pages for the delivery and coordination model; ENR Top 400 editions 2023 through 2026 for the revenue and sector-mix series; Building Design+Construction's 2025 data-centre ranking; Vantage Data Centers' Lighthouse campus materials and local trade reporting for the campus sequence and contractor allocation; EEOC, Department of Labor, OSHRC and USASpending for the independent record.
