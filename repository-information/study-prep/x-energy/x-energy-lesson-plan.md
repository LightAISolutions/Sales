# X-energy — Technology Lesson Plan

**Purpose:** teach **what is specific to a pebble-bed high-temperature gas-cooled reactor and to the company that sells one without owning it** — why the safety case lives inside a sub-millimetre TRISO particle and what that bought in licensing; why a 750°C helium outlet and 565°C steam make industrial heat the product and electricity the by-product; how continuous online refuelling changes what a reactor is for; why a fuel factory (TX-1) was licensed before any reactor and where HALEU sits in the chain from mine to core; what an NRC Part 50 construction permit on an 18-month clock is and is not (a final investment decision); the ring of owner, design-builder, engineer and buyer around a vendor that owns no plant; how to read an order book of 144 reactors of which four are binding; and what the income statement of a pre-revenue reactor vendor shows after an IPO (grant income, 50/50 cost share, Up-C and dual-class control). X-energy is the worked example because it is the only US HTGR front-runner, Amazon's SMR partner, and — since April 2026 — a public company whose prospectus and 10-Qs make all of this legible.

**How this plan relates to what you already have.** The **Oklo** plan teaches the reactor as a machine — moderators, coolants, passive safety, fuel as the constraint, what "modular" buys, two regulators, load-following, heat as product, years to first power — and this plan repeats none of it. **Dominion** teaches the regulated buyer's clock; **Talen** and **Constellation** teach the merchant plant and behind-the-meter loads; **Kiewit** gives the constructor's view of first-of-a-kind; **DNV** and **Sargent & Lundy** teach bankability and the engineer's seats. Read Oklo first if you have not.

**Five ideas carry the plan.** The TRISO particle is the containment. Heat at 750°C is what an industrial customer buys. Continuous refuelling makes a reactor behave like an always-on boiler. The vendor does not own the plant, and its revenue is grants and services until the first unit runs. HALEU is the constraint that survives every licence.

**One boundary, held throughout.** Steam pressure, helium pressure and inlet temperature are not on X-energy's product pages and are not stated here; the NRC-hosted topical reports that carry them were not read. Centrica's own pages were unreadable (HTTP 403); the "up to USD 1 billion" of additional DOE cost share rests on the Q2 2026 earnings call and trade press, not on a filing or a DOE release. Where the plan gives arithmetic (the implied USD 4.2 billion eligible cost) it says so.

**Suggested pacing:** Module 1 (~30 min). Module 2 (~25 min). Module 3 with the NRC Long Mott dashboard open (~35 min). Module 4 with the Energy Northwest release open (~30 min). Module 5 with the Q2 2026 release open (~35 min). Then flashcards and self-test in the app.

## Module 1 — The particle is the containment; the temperature is the product

**The single idea:** a pebble-bed HTGR puts the barrier that holds radioactivity inside the fuel, and runs hot enough to sell steam to a chemical plant.

**TRISO.** A uranium kernel about half a millimetre across wrapped in four layers: a porous carbon buffer (absorbs fission gases), inner pyrolytic carbon, a silicon carbide shell (the pressure vessel of the particle) and outer pyrolytic carbon. X-energy: "18,000 uranium kernels in each pebble", fuel that "Remains stable above 1450C", "600°C above light water reactor temperatures". About 200,000 graphite pebbles fill the core. The design claims a negative temperature coefficient strong enough that loss of cooling leaves the reactor hot, slow and stable without pumps or operators — the *functional containment* approach the NRC accepted.

**What it bought in licensing.** Long Mott's application was the first commercial advanced-reactor Part 50 application to receive an environmental assessment with a finding of no significant impact rather than a full environmental impact statement (issued 18 May 2026, "ahead of schedule"); one dismissed intervenor contention challenged functional containment directly.

**What it does not do.** It does not remove the need for enrichment — the kernels are HALEU at 15.5% U-235 — and it does not make itself (Module 2).

**Temperature.** Helium (does not boil, does not react, does not activate) leaves the core at "750°C Outlet Temperature" and makes "565°C Steam Temperature". A light-water reactor's coolant leaves at about 300°C. The Xe-100's unit of sale to Dow is megawatts thermal — 200 MWt per unit, 800 MWth for the four-pack against 320 MWe — supplying "electricity and high-temperature industrial steam" to a site making "more than 4 billion pounds of materials per year". The Chief Scientist's roadmap: 600°C steam for refining, 900°C for steam reforming and hydrogen.

**Continuous refuelling.** Pebbles enter at the top, pass through several times, are measured for burn-up and recirculated or discharged while the reactor runs ("Online Refueling — Continuous operation to maximize uptime"; "95% Target Reliability"; ramp "between 40% and full power in 12 minutes"). Commercial meaning: no refuelling outage for an industrial or data-centre load; fuel as a flow measured in pebbles per year; "recurring fuel sales" as a business.

**Self-check:** why does an HTGR's steam temperature matter to a data-centre buyer who wants only electricity? *(Higher Rankine efficiency and less cooling water per megawatt; and the same reactor can serve an industrial anchor, widening the customer base a campus can share.)*

## Module 2 — The fuel factory got its licence first: TX-1 and the HALEU chain

**The single idea:** the chain from mine to core has one pre-commercial link, and it is not the reactor.

**The chain.** Enrichment (0.7% natural → under 5% LEU → 5–20% HALEU; the Xe-100 needs 15.5%): Centrus at the American Centrifuge Plant (definitive agreement August 2026, phased, with prepayments), General Matter (long-term agreement), DOE's HALEU Availability Program allocation for the Dow first cores. Kernel and TRISO coating, pebble pressing: TRISO-X at TX-1, Oak Ridge (pilot line at ORNL under an extended CRADA). Licence: NRC Materials License SNM-7007, 13 February 2026, 40 years, the first Category II (10–20% enrichment) fuel-fabrication licence and "the first new fuel fabrication license in approximately 50 years" (DOE). Capacity: 5 MTU a year, about 700,000 pebbles, "enough fuel for up to 11 Xe-100 reactors". Qualification: 13-month irradiation in INL's Advanced Test Reactor from November 2025.

**Building it.** Geiger Brothers USD 40.8 million site work; Clark Construction USD 48.2 million core-and-shell; about USD 30 million of DOE-authorised early procurement; a USD 148 million IRA investment tax credit; USD 11 million Tennessee grant; POWER cites a USD 768 million budget. Vertical construction from November 2025; interior build-out Q3 2026; completion H1 2028. Campus expanded to about 180 acres for TX-2 and the TX-L research centre.

**The constraint.** Every core beyond the first needs HALEU from plants that are themselves not at commercial scale; the prospectus's risk factor says failure "could delay fuel fabrication (TX-1 and beyond) and reactor deployments"; management's answer — LEU first cores, HALEU "for the second core and beyond" — defers the dependency.

**Exercise:** sketch the six steps of the chain and mark, for each, who does it and whether it is licensed, built, contracted or pre-commercial as of September 2026.

## Module 3 — What a construction permit is, and what an 18-month clock means

**The single idea:** Part 50 is two steps — a construction permit to build, an operating licence to run — and the permit is a right, not a decision.

**The route.** Part 50 (two-step) suits a first-of-a-kind design whose design basis is not final; Part 52 folds design certification, site and construction into one combined licence. Dow's Long Mott Energy, LLC filed under Part 50 on 31 March 2025; docketed 12 May 2025.

**The clock.** Generic review: 36 months or more. NRC schedule set 10 June 2025: 18 months, "consistent with the 18 month timeline required by Executive Order 14300" — faster than X-energy's own 30-month estimate. Milestones: draft safety evaluation February 2026; EA/FONSI 18 May 2026 (early); advanced safety evaluation August 2026; final safety evaluation targeted November 2026; construction permit guided to Q1 2027. TerraPower, the other ARDP awardee, received its permit in March 2026 and broke ground in April.

**The decision.** Dow: no final investment decision before 2028 (June 2025); operations "likely 2031 to 2032" (February 2025); a USD 2 billion cost programme and 4,500 job cuts (January 2026). The one admitted intervenor contention — that Long Mott Energy "has failed to demonstrate its financial qualifications" — went to exactly this point and was dismissed in June 2026. The permit will exist before the money is committed.

**The UK equivalent.** The Generic Design Assessment — a site-independent design review by the ONR and environment agencies — entered 2 June 2026, "expected to conclude by end of 2029"; Centrica's Hartlepool plan depends on it.

**Self-check:** list three things that sit between a construction permit and first concrete at Long Mott. *(Dow's FID; the cost estimate the prospectus says "has increased"; the ARDP funding window, outside date "in or around 2030".)*

## Module 4 — The ring around the reactor, and an order book of options

**The single idea (ring):** X-energy sells reactors and fuel and owns nothing; every megawatt on its book has an owner, a builder, an engineer and a buyer around it.

**Cascade (Richland, WA).** Owner and licensee: Energy Northwest, a public-power joint operating agency — "will build, own and operate". Design-builder: Cascade Nuclear Partners — Kiewit Nuclear Solutions, Black & Veatch, Aecon, an equal JV under progressive design-build (selected October 2025), a model in which scope, schedule and price are developed in stages before a fixed contract. Owner's engineer: AtkinsRéalis. Funder and buyer: Amazon — funds the feasibility phase, holds rights to the first 320 MW project's power, options on "more than 5 gigawatts" by 2039 with "priority queue slots, rights of first refusal, and most favored pricing"; public power may buy up to 50% of the full 960 MW. Construction permit application by end-2026 / H1 2027; permit anticipated 2028; operations early 2030s.

**Long Mott (Seadrift, TX).** Owner and applicant: Long Mott Energy, LLC (Dow). Engineering: Fluor (FEL-2, April 2026); licensing engineer: Sargent & Lundy (site selection, licensing, environmental report with WSP). Buyer: Dow (steam and power); DOE cost share via ARDP.

**Supply chain.** Doosan (vessels; 16-unit reservation; a Changwon line for about 20 reactors a year); SGL Carbon (10-year graphite agreement; capacity doubling by 2030 "for up to 8 new Xe-100 reactors annually"); Toyo Tanso (graphite for Long Mott); Centrus and General Matter (HALEU); IHI (components MOU).

**The single idea (order book):** "144 reactors … approximately 11.5 gigawatts electric capacity (assuming full customer exercise of contingent rights)" is a ladder of commitments of very different strength.

**The rungs.** Binding and in licensing: Dow's four units. Rights and options: Amazon's more than 5 GWe by 2039, beginning with Energy Northwest's 320 MW. Non-binding development agreement: Centrica's "approximately six GWe (representative of 76 reactors …)", called "non-binding" by the prospectus. Letters of intent and studies: Talen (three or more four-unit plants, PJM), LG&E and KU, a "next 1-gigawatt project" with an investor-owned utility in "final throes". Half the gigawatts are Centrica's; about a third Amazon's; four reactors Dow's.

**Exercise:** for each rung, name the document that would move it up one rung (a definitive Cascade agreement; a Centrica FID; a Talen definitive agreement) and the party whose decision it is.

## Module 5 — Going public without a plant

**The single idea:** a pre-revenue reactor vendor is, in accounting terms, a cost-share programme with a listed equity attached.

**The IPO.** 44,254,659 shares at USD 23 (range USD 16–19), 23 April 2026; USD 1.02 billion gross; about USD 9.1 billion market value at pricing; trading as XE from 24 April; USD 17.71 on 4 September 2026. Three years after a USD 2.0 billion SPAC with Ares Acquisition Corp was abandoned for "challenging market conditions" and "peer-company trading performance".

**Up-C and dual class.** X-Energy, Inc. owns units of X-Energy Reactor Company, LLC; pre-IPO holders kept LLC units and matching Class B shares. Class A carries the economics; Class B the votes. Founder vehicles: 70.9% of Class B. Amazon: 23.4% of Class A, no Class B — a large economic owner without a voting block. Ares holds both.

**The income statement.** Q2 2026 "revenues and grant income" USD 54.6 million — USD 50.1 million services (engineering and licensing billed to sponsors and DOE), USD 4.5 million grants — against USD 164.6 million of operating expenses; net loss USD 105.3 million; H1 operating cash burn USD 164.6 million; liquidity USD 1,899.8 million. Sell: ARDP is "our largest source of revenue at our current stage of development".

**Cost share.** ARDP: USD 80 million initial (October 2020); cooperative agreement February 2021; "50/50 cost share of $2.4 billion of eligible costs ($1.2 billion reimbursement) through 2027"; USD 438 million reimbursed to 31 December 2025, USD 547 million through Q2 2026; budget period extended through March 2027; "estimated project costs have increased" (prospectus); per the Q2 call and ANS, up to USD 1 billion more, taking DOE's share to up to USD 2.115 billion — implying roughly USD 4.2 billion of eligible cost on a 50/50 basis (our arithmetic; no party has published a revised total; not yet in a filing or DOE release). Under cost share the government reimburses half of eligible spending after it is incurred, so income tracks spending and the company's cash need is the other half plus everything ineligible.

**Amazon's terms in the 10-Q.** From the fifth anniversary of the first Amazon-sponsored project's operation, X-energy pays Amazon "the cost efficiencies we have achieved relative to the power purchase agreement prices applicable to the initial projects" — most-favoured pricing with a rebate.

**Where it fails** (recap for the app's callout): the permit without the decision; fuel before reactors, enrichment after both; an order book that is mostly options; cost share as revenue; the public market's clock; concentration of counterparties, one of which is also the largest outside shareholder.

**Self-check:** DOE's share of Long Mott rises from USD 1.2 billion to up to USD 2.115 billion. Whose costs rose, and by roughly how much? *(Eligible project costs, from about USD 2.4 billion to roughly USD 4.2 billion — so the company's half rose as well; the exact revised total is unpublished.)*
